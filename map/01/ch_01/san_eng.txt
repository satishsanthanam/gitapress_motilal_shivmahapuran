### **CHAPTER ONE - 

(The Doubt of the Sages) 

---

Sanskrit
व्यास उवाच ।
धर्मक्षेत्रे महाक्षेत्रे गङ्गाकालिन्दिसङ्गमे ।
प्रयागे परमे पुण्ये ब्रह्मलोकस्य वर्त्मनि ॥ १॥

मुनयः शंसितात्मानः सत्यव्रतपरायणाः ।
महौजसो महाभागा महासत्रं वितेनिरे ॥ २॥

English

(Benedictory Prayer) - 

I meditate on Śiva, the lord of Ambikā (Pārvatī), auspicious from the beginning to the end, having no parallel, the noble lord, the unaging and the undying, the lord of Ātmans, the five-faced²⁵ and the dispeller of the five powerful sins.**

Vyāsa²⁶ said :— Sages of edified souls, engaged in truthful rites, powerful and blessed, performed a great sacrifice at the confluence of Gaṅgā and Kālindī (Yamunā) in the most sacred city of Prayāga,²⁷ a great holy centre, the path that leads to Brahmaloka²⁸. (1-2.)

[fn 25. Pañcānanam : In Hindu Mythology God Śiva has five faces. Pāśupata teachers had developed a special doctrine of Pañca-Brahma in which they ascribed five faces to Śiva symbolising the five elements (Liṅpa. 2. 14. 1. 33., SP I. 10. 1-9). Thus the whole scheme of creation is explained by the doctrine of Pañca-Brahma. The great statue of Śiva in the Elephanta caves represents the Pañca-Brahma form which is also known as Maheśamūrti in which the frontal view depicts three heads only, the fourth one on the back is concealed from view and the fifth one on the top dropped out as the symbol of invisible Ākāśa or Avyakta Prakṛti : See V. S. Agrawal : Matsya Purāṇa : A Study PP. 51-52.]

[fn 26. Vyāsa : The title is applied to Vedavyāsa, the arranger of the Vedas, the compiler of the Mahābhārata, the founder of the Vedānta philosophy and the arranger of the Purāṇas. Dowson doubts the identity of these different arrangers. Vyāsa is also called Kṛṣṇa-Dvaipāyana. From his complexion he received the name Kṛṣṇa and from his birth place he was called Dvaipāyana.]

[fn 27. Prayāga is a celebrated place of pilgrimage at the confluence of the Ganges and Jumna in the Naimiṣa forest (Sp. VS. I. 4). It is situated on the northern bank of the Ganges (Sk. II. ii. 12. 36). The name 'Prayāga' is recorded by Hwen Thsang in the seventh century and is as old as the reign of Aśoka who set up the stone pillar about 235 B. C. The Gupta emperors regarded the confluence at Prayāga as the visible symbol of Madhyadeśa.]

[fn 28. Brahmaloka, also called Satyaloka, is the abode of Brahmā.]

English
There is nothing in the three worlds that is not known to thee, of the past, present and the future. (8.)

Sanskrit

Sanskrit

English
Now, O Sūta of good mentality, we have only one point to be clarified. If thou dost desire to bless us, please explain the same, though it be the secret of secrets. (11.)

Sanskrit

English
At the advent of the terrible age of Kali men have become devoid of merits. They are engaged in evil ways of life. They have turned their faces from truthful avocations. (12.)

English
They are engaged in caluminating others. They covet other men's wealth. Their attention is diverted to other men's wives. Injuring others has become their chief aim. (13.)

Sanskrit

English

English
