### **CHAPTER THIRTEEN - *(Description of Good Conduct)***

---

Sanskrit
ऋषय ऊचुः ।
सदाचारं श्रावयाशु येन लोकाञ्जयेद् बुधः ।
धर्माधर्ममयान्ब्रूहि स्वर्गनारकदांस्तथा ॥ १॥

English
*The sages said :—*  Kindly tell us the mode of good conduct whereby the sensible man quickly attains higher worlds. Please tell us about virtue and evil that cause attainment of heaven or hell. (1.)

---

Sanskrit
सूत उवाच ।
सदाचारयुतो विद्वान् ब्राह्मणो नाम नामतः ।
वेदाचारयुतो विप्रो ह्येतैरेकैकवान्द्विजः ॥ २॥

English
*Sūta said :—*  A brahmin endowed with strict adherence to good conduct is perfectly wise. A brahmin learned in Vedas and of good conduct is called a Vipra. A brahmin endowed with only one of these two is a mere Dvija. (2.)

---

Sanskrit
अल्पाचारोऽल्पवेदश्च क्षत्रियो राजसेवकः ।
किञ्चिदाचारवान्वैश्यः कृषिवाणिज्यकृत्तथा ॥ ३॥

English
A brahmin following some of the prescribed rules of conduct and with a smattering of the Vedas is a Kṣatriya brahmin, at best a royal servant. Very careless in following the rules of conduct the brahmin is really a Vaiśya brahmin. One engaged in agriculture and trading activities is also likewise. (3.)

---

Sanskrit
शूद्रब्राह्मण इत्युक्तः स्वयमेव हि कर्षकः ।
असूयालुः परद्रोही चण्डालद्विज उच्यते ॥ ४॥

English
A brahmin ploughing the field himself is a Śūdra brahmin. One of envious and spiteful temperament is a degraded Dvija. (4.)

---

Sanskrit
पृथिवीपालको राजा इतरे क्षत्रिया मताः ।
धान्यादिक्रयवान्वैश्य इतरो वणिगुच्यते ॥ ५॥

English
A Kṣatriya who rules over a kingdom is a “King”; others are mere Kṣatriyas. A merchant dealing in grains etc. is a Vaiśya and others of his caste are mere “Vaṇiks” (5.)

---

Sanskrit
ब्रह्मक्षत्रियवैश्यानां शुश्रूषुः शूद्र उच्यते ।
कर्षको वृषलो ज्ञेय इतरे चैव दस्यवः ॥ ६॥

English
A person rendering service to Brahmins, Kṣatriyas and Vaiśyas is called a Śūdra. A working agriculturist is a Vṛṣala and the others are Dasyus. (6.)

---

Sanskrit
सर्वो ह्युषः प्राङ्मुखश्च चिन्तयेद् देवपूर्वकान् ।
धर्मानर्थांश्च तत्क्लेशानायं च व्ययमेव च ॥ ७॥

English
It is the duty of everyone of the four castes to get up early in the morning and sit facing the east and meditate on gods. He shall then think about the various acts of virtue, of matters regarding monetary dealings, the problems connected with them, the sources of income and the items of expenditure. (7.)

---

Sanskrit
आयुर्द्वेषश्च मरणं पापं भाग्यं तथैव च ।
व्याधिः पुष्टिस्तथा शक्तिः प्रातरुत्थानदिक्फलम् ॥ ८॥

English
The direction in which one casts one’s first glance on waking up indicates the good or bad that is likely to attend one on that day—the eight effects in order are—longevity, hatred, death, sin, fortune, sickness, nourishment and strength. (8.)

---

Sanskrit
निशान्त्ययामोषा ज्ञेया यामार्धं सन्धिरुच्यते ।
तत्काले तु समुत्थाय विण्मूत्रे विसृजेद् द्विजः ॥ ९॥

English
The last yāma (3 hours) of the night is called Uṣā and the latter half of it is sandhi (period of conjunction). A brahmin shall get up at that hour and answer the calls of nature. (9.)

---

Sanskrit
गृहाद् दूरं ततो गत्वा बाह्यतः प्रावृतस्तथा ।
उदङ्मुखः समाविश्य प्रतिबन्धेऽन्यदिङ्मुखः ॥ १०॥

English
It must be in a place far off from the house. It must be a covered place. He shall sit facing the north. If it is not possible due to any obstacle he can sit facing other directions. (10.)

---

Sanskrit
जलाग्निब्राह्मणादीनां देवानां नाभिमुख्यतः ।
लिङ्गं पिधाय वामेन मुखमन्येन पाणिना ॥ ११॥

English
He must never sit in front of water, fire, a brahmin or the idol of any god. He must screen the penis with the left hand and the mouth with the right. (11.)

---

Sanskrit
मलमुत्सृज्य चोत्थाय न पश्येच्चैव तन्मलम् ।
उद्धृतेन जलेनैव शौचं कुर्याज्जलाद् बहिः ॥ १२॥

English
After evacuating the bowels, the faeces should not be looked at. Water drawn out in a vessel should be used for cleaning (i.e. no one should sit inside the tank or river-water for cleaning purpose). (12.)

---

Sanskrit
अथवा देवपित्रर्षितीर्थावतरणं विना ।
सप्त वा पञ्च वा त्रीन्वा गुदं संशोधयेन्मृदा ॥ १३॥

English
Any way no one shall enter the holy tanks and rivers dedicated to deities, manes etc. and frequented by the sages. The rectum must be cleaned with mud seven, five or three times. (13.)

---

Sanskrit
लिङ्गे कर्कोटमात्रं तु गुदे प्रसृतिरिष्यते ।
तत उत्थाय पद्धस्तशौचं गण्डूषमष्टकम् ॥ १४॥

English
The penis must be cleaned with mud as large as a cucumber fruit and the quantity of mud for the purification of the rectum shall be Prasṛti (half a handful). After the purification of the excretory organs, hands and feet must be washed and gargling shall be done for eight times. (14.)

---

Sanskrit
येन केन च पत्रेण काष्ठेन च जलाद् बहिः ।
कार्यं सन्तर्जनीं त्यज्य दन्तधावनमीरितम् ॥ १५॥

English
For gargling, the water can be taken in any vessel or a wooden cup; but water shall be spit outside (not in the river or tank). Washing of the teeth with any leaf or twig must be without using the index finger and outside the water. (15.)

---

Sanskrit
जलदेवान्नमस्कृत्य मन्त्रेण स्नानमाचरेत् ।
अशक्तः कण्ठदघ्नं वा कटिदघ्नमथापि वा ॥ १६॥

English
After making obeisance to the gods of water, the twice-born shall perform the ablution with mantras. Sick or weak persons shall take bath upto the neck or hips. (16.)

---

Sanskrit
आजानुजलमाविश्य मन्त्रस्थानं समाचरेत् ।
देवादींस्तर्पयेद्विद्वांस्तत्र तीर्थजलेन च ॥ १७॥

English
Sprinkling water upto the knees he shall perform the Mantrasnāna. He shall propitiate deities etc. sensibly with the water from the holy tank or river. (17.)

---

Sanskrit
धौतवस्त्रं समादाय पञ्चकच्छेन धारयेत् ।
उत्तरीयं च किञ्चैव धार्यं सर्वेषु कर्मसु ॥ १८॥

English
A washed dry cloth should be taken and worn in the form of pañcakaccha (wearing of the lower garment in a special way). In all sacred rites the upper cloth should also be used. (18.)

---

Sanskrit
नद्यादितीर्थस्नने तु स्नानवस्त्रं न शोधयेत् ।
वापीकूपगृहादौ तु स्नानादूर्ध्वं नयेद्बुधः ॥ १९॥

शिलादार्वादिके वापि जले वापि स्थलेऽपि वा ।
संशोध्य पीडयेद्वस्त्रं पितॄणां तृप्तये द्विजाः ॥ २०॥

English
While taking bath in the holy river or tank, the cloth worn shall not be rinsed or beaten. The sensible man shall take it to a separate tank or well or to the house itself and beat it on a rock or on a plank to the gratification of the manes, O brahmins. (19-20.)

---

Sanskrit
जाबालकोक्तमन्त्रेण भस्मना च त्रिपुण्ड्रकम् ।
अन्यथा चेज्जले पातस्ततो नरकमृच्छति ॥ २१॥

आपोहिष्ठेति शिरसि प्रोक्षयेत्पापशान्तये ।
यस्येति मन्त्रं पादे तु सन्धिप्रोक्षणमुच्यते ॥ २२॥

हृदये मूर्ध्नि पादे च मूर्ध्नि हृत्पाद एव च ।
हृत्पादमूर्ध्नि सम्प्रोक्ष्य मन्त्रस्नानं विदुर्बुधाः ॥ २३॥

English
The Tripuṇḍraka$^{70}$ shall be drawn on the forehead with the Jābālaka mantra. If anyone enters water otherwise, he will surely go to hell. According to scholarly authorities the mantrasnāna is as follows : Repeating the mantra “Āpo hi sthā”$^{71}$ etc. water shall be sprinkled over the head for suppressing sins. Repeating the mantra “Yasya Kṣayāya”$^{72}$ etc. water shall be sprinkled over the joints in the legs. The order is as follows :—feet, head, chest; head, chest, feet and chest, feet, head for sprinkling with water thrice. (21-23.)

Sanskrit
ईषत्स्पर्शे च दोःस्वास्थ्ये राजराष्ट्रभयेऽपि च ।
अगत्या गतिकाले च मन्त्रस्नानं समाचरेत् ॥ २४॥

English
It is enough if one performs mantra snāna when one is slightly indisposed, or when there is danger from the king or when there is civil commotion, or when there is no other way or when one is about to undertake a journey. (24.)

---

Sanskrit

अपः पीत्वा तथा मध्ये पुनः प्रोक्षणमाचरेत् ॥ २५॥

English
He shall drink by way of Ācamana reciting the mantras from Sūryānuvāka in the morning or from Agni- Anuvāka in the evening and perform the ceremonial sprinkling in the middle. (25.)

---

Sanskrit

English
O brahmins, at the end of the Japa of Gāyatri mantra$^{73}$ Arghya shall be offered thrice to the sun towards east and once also thereafter. (26.)

[fn 73. Three-footed sacred mantra of the Ṛgveda well-known after its metre Gāyatri. It is addressed to the sun (savitar) and is therefore called Sivitri. Sanskrit
अथ जाते च सायाह्ने भुवि पश्चिमदिङ्मुखः ।
उद्धृत्य दद्यात् प्रातस्तु मध्याह्नेऽङ्गुलिभिस्तथा ॥ २७॥

English
The offering of Arghya in the morning is by lifting both the hands high up; that in the midday by letting off the water through the fingers and that in the evening by letting the water over the ground facing the west. (27.)

---

Sanskrit
अङ्गुलीनां च रन्ध्रेण लम्बं पश्येद् दिवाकरम् ।

English

Sanskrit
सायं मुहूर्तादर्वाक्तु कृता सन्ध्या वृथा भवेत् ।
अकालात्काल इत्युक्तो दिनेऽतीते यथाक्रमम् ॥ २९॥

दिवातीते च गायत्रीं शतं नित्ये क्रमाज्जपेत् ।

English
Sandhyā prayer performed before the prescribed time is ineffective. Hence Sandhyā shall be performed at the prescribed time. The expiatory rite for the omission of Sandhyā prayer for a day is the repetition of Gāyatri a hundred times more than the usual number of times for ten days. If the omission is for ten days or more, Gāyatri must be repeated for a hundred thousand times as atonement. (29-30.)

Sanskrit
मासातीते तु नित्ये हि पुनश्चोपनयं चरेत् ।
ईशो गौरी गुहो विष्णुर्ब्रह्मा चन्द्रश्च वै यमः ॥ ३१॥

एवंरूपांश्च वै देवांस्तर्पयेदर्थसिद्धये ।
ब्रह्मार्पणं ततः कृत्वा शुद्धाचमनमाचरेत् ॥ ३२॥

English
If one omits Sandhyā for a month one has to be re-invested with the sacred thread.$^{74}$ For the sake of prosperity deities shall be propitiated such as Īśa, Gauri, Guha.$^{75}$ Viṣṇu, Brahmā, Candra (the moon) and Yama. Thereafter the entire rite shall be dedicated to the supreme Brahman and pure Ācamana shall be performed. (31-32.)

[fn 74. It is one of the purificatory rites prescribed in the Dharmaśūtras and explained in the Grhyasūtras in which the boy is invested with the sacred thread and thus endowed with second or spiritual birth and qualified to learn the Veda by heart. A Brāhmin is initiated in the eighth year, a Kṣatriya in the eleventh, a Vaiśya in the twelfth; but the term could be delayed. Cf. MS. 2. 36-38.]

[fn 75. Guha, literally the mysterious one, is Kārttikeya, so called because of his mysterious birth. According to a legend he was the son of Śiva produced without the intervention of a woman. Śiva cost his seed into fire which was afterwards received by the Ganges: Kārttikeya was the result. He is therefore called as the son of Agni and Gangā. When born he was fostered by the six Krttikas and these offering their six breasts to the child he became six-headed.]

He shall not omit the practice of the Praṇava mantra. (33-34.)

Sanskrit

Sanskrit

Sanskrit

English

Sanskrit

Sanskrit

Sanskrit

English
Evil is of violent character and virtue is of pleasant nature. A person becomes miserable due to evil and secures happiness on account of virtue. (57.)

---

Sanskrit

Sanskrit

If any one regularly offers sufficient material means to a brahmin with four members in his family, for a hundred years he will remain in Brahmaloka. (59.)

Sanskrit

English
The rite of Cāndrāyaṇa performed a thousand times yields Brahmaloka. It is the duty of a Kṣatriya to establish and sustain a thousand families. (60.)

---
