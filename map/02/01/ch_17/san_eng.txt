### **CHAPTER SEVENTEEN - # (The Story of Guṇanidhi)**

---

Sanskrit
१७

सूत उवाच ।
इत्याकर्ण्य वचस्तस्य ब्रह्मणः स तु नारदः ।
पुनः पप्रच्छ तं नत्वा विनयेन मुनीश्वराः ॥ १॥

English
Sūta said :—  O great sages, after hearing these words of Brahmā, Nārada once again bowed to him and asked humbly. (1.)

---

Sanskrit
नारद उवाच ।
कदा गतो हि कैलासं शङ्करो भक्तवत्सलः ।
क्व वा सखित्वं तस्यासीत् कुबेरेण महात्मना ॥ २॥

किं चकार हरस्तत्र परिपूर्णः शिवाकृतिः ।
एतत्सर्वं समाचक्ष्व परं कौतूहलं मम ॥ ३॥

English
Nārada said :—  When did Śiva favourably disposed to His devotees go to Kailāsa? Where did he have the intimate acquaintance with Kubera²¹⁹ of great and noble soul? What did Śiva of auspicious form do there? Please narrate all these things to me. I am deeply interested in it. (2-3.)

[fn 219. Kubera is the son of विश्रवस् by इब्राहिम। He is the chief of the Yakṣas and a friend of Rudra. He is mythologised as having three legs and eight teeth.]
---

Sanskrit
ब्रह्मोवाच ।
श‍ृणु नारद वक्ष्यामि चरितं शशिमौलिनः ।
यथा जगाम कैलासं सखित्वं धनदस्य च ॥ ४॥

English
Brahmā said :— O Nārada, listen. I shall tell you the story of the moon-crested lord, how he went to Kailāsa and how he contracted friendship of Kubera. (4.)

---

Sanskrit
असीत् काम्पिल्यनगरे सोमयाजिकुलोद्भवः ।
दीक्षितो यज्ञदत्ताख्यो यज्ञविद्याविशारदः ॥ ५॥

English
In the city of Kāmpilya²²⁰ there was a sacrificer named Yajñadatta. Born of Somayāji family he was an adept in the performance of sacrifice. (5.)

[fn 220. The country known to Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā (xxiii, 18) and Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa (xiii, 2.8.3) can be identified with the city of Kāmpila in the Furrukhabad district, Uttara Pradeśa. It was the Southern Capital of Pāñcāladeśa in ancient India. Dr. Awasthi (Studies in Sk. P. 85) however, places it in the Ānarta Deśa, a region of the Western India.]

---

Sanskrit
वेदवेदाङ्गवित्प्राज्ञो वेदान्तादिषु दक्षिणः ।
राजमान्योऽथ बहुधा वदान्यः कीर्तिभाजनः ॥ ६॥

English
He knew Vedas and Vedāṅgas. He was a great scholar of Vedānta etc. He was honoured by the king. He was a liberal-minded donor and as such his fame had spread far and wide. (6.)

---

Sanskrit
अग्निशुश्रूषणरतो वेदाध्ययनतत्परः ।
सुन्दरो रमणीयाङ्गश्चन्द्रबिम्बसमाकृतिः ॥ ७॥

English
He assiduously maintained the sacrificial fire and was devoted to the study of the Vedas. His son (Guṇanidhi) was of a very handsome complexion and shone like the moon's disc. After the investiture with the sacred thread he learned all the eight lores²²¹ over and over again. Yet, unknown to his father he indulged in gambling. (7-8.)

[fn 221. The eight sciences included (1) the triple Veda (अवनी) (2) logic and metaphysics (आन्वीशिकी), (3) the science of Government (दण्डनीति), (4) practical arts such as agriculture, commerce, medicine etc. (शार्त), (5) ancient historical and mythological tradition (6) science of rituals (7) Logic and (8) Dharma or Law.]]
---

Sanskrit
आसीद् गुणनिधिर्नाम दीक्षितस्यास्य वै सुतः ।
कृतोपनयनः सोऽष्टौ विद्यां जग्राह भूरिशः ॥

अथ पित्रानभिज्ञातो द्यूतकर्मरतोऽभवत् । ८॥

आदायादाय बहुशो धनं मातुः सकाशतः ।
समदाद् द्यूतकारेभ्यो मैत्रीं तैश्च चकार सः ॥ ९॥

English
Ever and anon he took plenty of sums from his mother and gave them over to other gamblers with whom he contracted great intimacy. (9.)

---

Sanskrit
सन्त्यक्तब्राह्मणाचारः सन्ध्यास्नानपराङ्मुखः ।
निन्दको वेदशास्त्राणां देवब्राह्मणनिन्दकः ॥ १०॥

English
He eschewed all brahminical ways and conduct of life. He was averse to the performance of Sandhyā prayers and ceremonial ablutions. He began to speak ill of the Vedas, sacred texts, devas and brahmins. (10.)

---

Sanskrit
स्मृत्याचारविहीनस्तु गीतवाद्यविनोदभाक् ।
नटपाखण्डभाण्डैस्तु बद्धप्रेमपरम्परः ॥ ११॥

English
He did not follow the conventions and injunctions of the Smṛti code. He indulged in singing and playing. Actors, heretics etc. were his beloved friends. (11.)

---

Sanskrit
प्रेरितोऽपि जनन्या स न ययौ पितुरन्तिकम् ।
गृहकार्यान्तरव्याप्तो दीक्षितो दीक्षितायिनीम् ॥ १२॥

यदा यदैव तां पृच्छेदये गुणनिधिः सुतः ।
न दृश्यते मया गेहे कल्याणि विदधाति किम् ॥ १३॥

तदा तदेति सा ब्रूयादिदानीं स बहिर्गतः ।
स्नात्वा समर्च्य वै देवानेतावन्तमनेहसम् ॥ १४॥

अधीत्याध्ययनार्थं स द्वित्रैर्मित्रैः समं ययौ ।
एकपुत्रेति तन्माता प्रतारयति दीक्षितम् ॥ १५॥

English
Although his mother wanted him to meet his father now and then, he never went near his father. Engaged in extra-domestic activities Yajñadatta used to ask his wife “Dear good woman, what is our son Guṇanidhi doing? He is not at home.” Then the woman used to say, “He has gone out just now. So long he had been taking his bath and worshipping the deities. He has finished his Vedic studies and has just gone out in the company of two or three friends for the purpose of learning somewhere”. The poor woman in view of the fact that she had only one son deceived her husband thus. (12-15.)

---

Sanskrit
न तत्कर्म च तद्वृत्तं किञ्चिद्वेत्ति स दीक्षितः ।
सर्वं केशान्तकर्मास्य चक्रे वर्षेऽथ षोडशे ॥ १६॥

English
The simple husband did not know anything about the nefarious activities of his son or his bad conduct. All sacred rites ending with Keśa Karma$^{222}$ were performed in the sixteenth year of the son. (16.)

[fn 222. The religious ceremony Keśānta in which the hair were cut off was performed upon Brāhmins at 16 years of age, Kṣattrīyas at 22 and Vaiśyas at 24. Cf. Manu. ii. 65, Yāj. 1.36.]

---

Sanskrit
अथो स दीक्षितो यज्ञदत्तः पुत्रस्य तस्य च ।
गृह्योक्तेन विधानेन पाणिग्राहमकारयम् ॥ १७॥

English
Thereafter Yajñadatta performed the marriage rite of the son in accordance with the rules prescribed in the Gṛhya Sūtras. (17.)

---

Sanskrit
प्रत्यहं तस्य जननी सुतं गुणनिधिं मृदु ।
शास्ति स्नेहार्द्रहृदया ह्युपवेश्य स्म नारद ॥ १८॥

English
O Nārada, every day the woman with her heart melting with motherly affection used to make her son sit up and gently upbraid him. (18.)

---

Sanskrit
क्रोधनस्तेऽस्ति तनय स महात्मा पितेत्यलम् ।
यदि ज्ञास्यति ते वृत्तं त्वां च मां ताडयिष्यति ॥ १९॥

English
“Dear son, your father is surely a great man, but he is of rash temperament. If he comes to know of your activities he will beat you and will not spare me too. (19.)

---

Sanskrit
आच्छादयामि ते नित्यं पितुरग्रे कुचेष्टितम् ।
लोकमान्योऽस्ति ते तातः सदाचारैर्न वै धनैः ॥ २०॥

English
I conceal your nefarious activities from your father every day. Due to his good conduct and his affluent circumstances he is honoured by all the people. (20.)

---

Sanskrit
ब्राह्मणानां धनं तात सद्विद्या साधुसङ्गमः ।
किमर्थं न करोषि त्वं स्वरुचिं प्रीतमानसः ॥ २१॥

English
Dear child, a good learning and association with men of saintly character constitute a great asset for brahmins. How is it that you do not gladly take interest in such things? (21.)

---

Sanskrit
सच्छ्रोत्रियास्तेऽनूचाना दीक्षिताःसोमयाजिनः ।
इति रूढिमिह प्राप्तास्तव पूर्वपितामहाः ॥ २२॥

English
Your ancestors and grandfathers had all earned the reputation of being good Vedic scholars, well learned in Śāstras, and performers of sacrifices, especially Somayāgas. (22.)

---

Sanskrit
त्यक्त्वा दुर्वृत्तसंसर्गं साधुसङ्गरतो भव ।
सद्विद्यासु मनो धेहि ब्राह्मणाचारमाचर ॥ २३॥

English
Shun the company of the wicked people, associate with good men, turn your attention to good learning and strictly adhere to brahminical conventions. (23.)

---

Sanskrit

ततो न त्रपसे किं नः त्यज दुर्वृत्ततां स्वकाम् ॥ २४॥

English
Emulate your father in form, fame and traditional activity. Why don't you feel ashamed? Cast off your wickedness. (24.)

---

Sanskrit
ऊनविंशतिकोऽसि त्वमेषा षोडशवार्षिकी ।
एतां संवृणु सद्वृत्तां पितृभक्तियुतो भव ॥ २५॥

English
You are nineteen now. This girl is sixteen years old. She is a good girl. Take her. Protect her. Above all be devoted to your father. (25.)

---

Sanskrit
श्वशुरोऽपि हि ते मान्यः सर्वत्र गुणशीलतः ।
ततो न त्रपसे किं नः त्यज दुर्वृत्ततां सुत ॥ २६॥

English
You shall respect your father-in-law also, in view of his good qualities and conduct. How is it that you do not feel ashamed of wickedness? (26.)

---

Sanskrit

तेभ्योऽपि न बिभेषि त्वं शुद्धोऽस्युभयवंशतः ॥ २७॥

English
Dear son, your maternal uncles too are matchless in learning, conduct and pedigree and other things. You are not afraid even of them. Your paternal and maternal lineages are equally pure. (27.)

---

Sanskrit
पश्यैतान्प्रतिवेश्मस्थान्ब्राह्मणानां कुमारकान् ।
गृहेऽपि शिष्यान्पश्यैतान्पितुस्ते विनयोचितान् ॥ २८॥

English
See the brahmin boys of your neighbourhood. Even in our house see the disciples of your father. How humbly do they behave? (28.)

---

Sanskrit

श्रद्धां विहाय ते ताते वृत्तिलोपं करिष्यति ॥ २९॥

English
Dear son, if the king hears of your evil propensities, he will cease to respect your father and may even suspend the regular maintenance allowance. (29.)

---

Sanskrit
बालचेष्टितमेवैतद् वदन्त्यद्यापि ते जनाः ।
अनन्तरं हरिष्यन्ति युक्तां दीक्षिततामिह ॥ ३०॥

English
Till now people used to call your activities the foolish blunders of an ignorant boy. Hereafter they may take away the traditional title of Dikṣita. (30.)

---

Sanskrit
सर्वेऽप्याक्षारयिष्यन्ति तव तातं च मामपि ।
मातुश्चरित्रं तनयो धत्ते दुर्भाषणैरिति ॥ ३१॥

English
People will curse and cavil at your father and me saying such evil words as "The son has adopted the wickedness of the mother." (31.)

---

Sanskrit
पितापि ते न पापीयाञ्छ्रुतिस्मृतिपथानुगः ।
तदङ्घ्रिलीनमनसो मम साक्षी महेश्वरः ॥ ३२॥

English
Your father has never been a sinner. He strictly follows the path of the Vedas and Smṛtis. Lord Śiva is my witness for the purity of my mind that is riveted to his feet. (32.)

---

Sanskrit

English
I have not seen the face of any wicked man after my menstrual bath. Powerful indeed is Fate whence a boy like you is born of my womb !” (33.)

---

Sanskrit

English
Although constantly advised thus by his mother, the wicked boy did not abandon his evil ways. For, an idiot indulging in vice is beyond redemption. (34.)

---

Sanskrit
मृगयामद्यपैशुन्यानृतचौर्यदुरोदरैः ।
स वारदारैर्व्यसनैरेभिः कोऽत्र न खण्डितः ॥ ३५॥

English
Who is he that is not broken up by the evil influences of hunting, wine, slander, untruth, theft, gambling and prostitutes ? (35.)

---

Sanskrit
यद्यन्मध्यगृहे पश्येत्तत्तन्नीत्वा सुदुर्मतिः ।
अर्पयेद् द्यूतकाराणां सकुप्यं वसनादिकम् ॥ ३६॥

English
The wicked fellow (Guṇanidhi) used to lay his hands on whatever he could see in the house, a cloth, a base metal etc. and take it to the gambling den, there to lose the same to his brother gamblers. (36.)

---

Sanskrit

चोरयित्वैकदादाय दुरोदरकरेऽर्पयत् ॥ ३७॥

English
Once he stole a very valuable ring of his father set with precious stones and gave it to one the of gamblers. (37.)

---

Sanskrit

English
It chanced that one day the Dīkṣita saw it in the hand of the gambler. He asked the fellow—“Where did you get this ring from?” (38.)

---

Sanskrit

English
First the gambler did not say anything. When repeatedly asked he said—“O brahmin, you are unnecessarily accusing me of theft. It was your son who gave it to me. On the previous day I had won his mother’s upper garment. (39-40.)

---

Sanskrit

अन्येषां द्यूतकर्तॄणां भूरि तेनार्पितं वसु ॥ ४१॥

English
Do not think that I alone was the winner of this ring. He has lost many costly things to other gamblers as well. (41.)

---

Sanskrit

भाजनानि विचित्राणि कांस्यताम्रमयानि च ॥ ४२॥

English
He has thus given gems, metals, silk garments, vessels, golden vases, and different sorts of copper and bell metal pots. (42.)

---

Sanskrit

English
Everyday he is being bound stark naked by the gamblers. In the whole world you cannot see such a useless poor gambler as he (your son). (43.)

---

Sanskrit

English
How is it that till now, O brahmin, you have not realised that your son is a ring leader of base gamblers, very clever in misdemeanour and unfair means ? (44.)

---

Sanskrit

English
On hearing these words, the poor Dīkṣita’s head bent down with shame. He covered his face and head with a cloth and quietly slipped back into his house. (45.)

---

Sanskrit

स दीक्षितो यज्ञदत्तः श्रौतकर्मपरायणः ॥ ४६॥

English
Yajñadatta, the sacrificer, well versed in Vedic rites spoke thus to his wife who was a very chaste lady. (46.)

---

Sanskrit
यज्ञदत्त उवाच ।
दीक्षितायनि कुत्रास्ति धूर्तो गुणनिधिः सुतः ।
अथ तिष्ठतु किं तेन क्व सा मम शुभोर्मिका ॥॥ ४७॥

अङ्गोद्वर्तनकाले या त्वया मेऽङ्गुलितो हृता ।
सा त्वं रत्नमयीं शीघ्रं तामानीय प्रयच्छ मे ॥ ४८॥

English
*Yajñadatta said :—*  O mistress ! where is that gambling rogue of a son, Guṇanidhi? Or let it be. Why should I ask for him? Where is that auspicious ring which you took off at the time of applying unguents on my body? Bring it quickly and give it to me. (47-48.)

---

Sanskrit
इति श्रुत्वाथ तद्वाक्यं भीता सा दीक्षितायनी ।
प्रोवाच स्नानमध्याह्नीं क्रियां निष्पादयन्त्यथ ॥ ४९॥

व्यग्रास्मि देवपूजार्थमुपहारादिकर्मणि ।
समयोऽयमतिक्रामेदतिथीनां प्रियातिथे ॥ ५०॥

इदानीमेव पक्वान्नकरणव्यग्रया मया ।
स्थापिता भाजने क्वापि विस्मृतेति न वेद्म्यहम् ॥ ५१॥

English
The mistress was frightened at these words. While she was engaged in arranging for bath and midday sacred rites she replied—“O lord, I am busy arranging the various articles of offerings for worship. O lord, fond of guests, the guests may be unnecessarily detained. While I was busy cooking the pudding I kept the ring somewhere in some vessel just now. What a pity ! I have forgotten it. I do not know where it has been kept. (49-51.)
