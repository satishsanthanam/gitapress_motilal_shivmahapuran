### **CHAPTER EIGHTEEN - *(The Redemption of Guṇanidhi)***

---

Sanskrit
१८

ब्रह्मोवाच ।
श्रुत्वा तथा स वृत्तान्तं प्राक्तनं स्वं विनिन्द्य च ।
काञ्चिद्दिशं समालोक्य निर्ययौ दीक्षिताङ्गजः ॥ १॥

कियच्चिरं ततो गत्वा यज्ञदत्तात्मजः स हि ।
दुष्टो गुणनिधिस्तस्थौ गतोत्साहो विसर्जितः ॥ २॥

English
*Brahmā said:—*  Guṇanidhi, the son of the Dikṣita Yajñadatta, came to know of this. Regretfully he cursed himself and set off from that place. After wandering aimlessly for a long time, he, the wicked fellow, felt the abandonment keenly and losing all hopes halted at a place (1-2.)

Sanskrit
चिन्तामवाप महतीं क्व यामि करवाणि किम् ।
नाहमभ्यस्तविद्योऽस्मि न चैवातिधनोऽस्म्यहम् ॥ ३॥

देशान्तरे यस्य धनं स सद्यः सुखमेधते ।
भयमस्ति धने चौरात्स विघ्नः सर्वतो भवः ॥ ४॥

याजकस्य कुले जन्म कथं मे व्यसनं महत् ।
अहो बलीयान्हि विधिर्भावि कर्मानुसन्धयेत् ॥ ५॥

भिक्षितुं नाधिगच्छामि न मे परिचितः क्वचित् ।
न च पार्श्वे धनं किञ्चित्किमत्र शरणं भवेत् ॥ ६॥

सदानभ्युदिते भानौ प्रसूर्मे मिष्टभोजनम् ।
दद्यादद्यात्र कं याचे न चेह जननी मम ॥ ७॥

English
He thought to himself : “Where am I to go ? What shall I do ? I have not studied much, nor am I rich enough. Only a wealthy man can be happy in a foreign land, although he has to face the fear of thieves there. Of course this fear is present everywhere. I am born in the family of priests officiating in sacrifices. Why am I reduced to this wretched plight ? Fate is powerful indeed, controlling all our future actions. I cannot even beg as I have no acquaintance, no money. Where shall I seek refuge ? Everyday, even before sunrise, my mother used to feed me with sweet pudding. Today whom shall I beg ? My mother too is away from me.” (3-7.)

English

[fn 223. Śivarātri : Śiva's Night. It is a popular fast and festival held in honour of Śiva on the 14th of the dark half of the month Māgha or January-February with many solemn ceremonies observed during the day and night. In Tāntric literature it is called Kālarātri, one of the three sacred nights, the other two being Mahārātri and Moharātri.]

दम इत्यभिधानोऽभूच्छिवसेवापरायणः ।
बालोऽपि शिशुभिः साकं शिवभक्तिं चकार सः ॥ ५३॥

English
Known as Dama he was devoted to the service of Śiva. Even as a boy he carried on many acts of devotion to Śiva in the company of other children. (53.)

Sanskrit
क्रमाद्राज्यमवापाथ पितर्युपरते युवा ।
प्रीत्या प्रवर्तयामास शिवधर्मांश्च सर्वशः ॥ ५४॥

English
When his father passed away he became the king in the prime of his youth. In his kingdom he spread the ideals and tenets of Śiva lovingly. (54.)

Sanskrit
नान्यं धर्मं स जानाति दुर्दमो भूपतिर्दमः ।
शिवालयेषु सर्वेषु दीपदानादृते द्विज ॥ ५५॥

English
The king Dama was unconquerable. O brahmin, he did not stress any act of piety other than furnishing temples of Śiva with lamps in plenty. (55.)

Sanskrit
ग्रामाधीशान्समाहूय सर्वान्स विषयस्थितान् ।
इत्थमाज्ञापयामास दीपा देयाः शिवालये ॥ ५६॥

English
He called headmen of the villages in his kingdom and asked them to furnish all temples of Śiva with lamps. (56.)

Sanskrit
अन्यथा सत्यमेवेदं स मे दण्ड्यो भविष्यति ।
दीपदानाच्छिवस्तुष्टो भवतीति श्रुतीरितम् ॥ ५७॥

English
He warned them that if they defaulted they would be punished. It is declared in the Vedas that Śiva is delighted at the gift of a lamp to his temples. (57.)

Sanskrit
यस्य यस्याभितो ग्रामं यावन्तश्च शिवालयाः ।
तत्र तत्र सदा दीपो द्योतनीयोऽविचारितम् ॥ ५८॥

English
"Therefore, you headmen shall see that the temples of Śiva in your jurisdiction are properly illuminated with lamps. There is no question of hesitation in this matter. (58.)

Sanskrit

इति तद्भयतो दीपा दीप्ताः प्रतिशिवालयम् ॥ ५९॥

English
"Undoubtedly I shall get the defaulter beheaded." Thus for fear of him every temple was duly illuminated. (59.)

Sanskrit

English
With this act of piety alone, as long as he lived, the king Dama acquired ample prosperity. Finally he passed away. (60.)

Sanskrit

English
The impression of lamps persisted in his mind. He caused many lamps to be lighted. Finally he became the lord of Alakā$^{226}$ with gem-set lamps to his credit. (61.)

Sanskrit

इति ज्ञात्वा शिवे कार्यं भजनं सुसुखार्थिभिः ॥ ६२॥

English
Thus even the smallest service rendered to Śiva bears rich fruit in time. Let all persons seeking happiness realise this and continue the worship of Śiva. (62.)

Sanskrit
क्व स दीक्षितदायादः सर्वधर्मारतिः सदा ।
शिवालये दैवयोगाद्यातश्चोरयितुं वसु ॥

स्वार्थदीपदशोद्योतलिङ्गमौलितमोहरः । ६३॥

कलिङ्गविषये राज्यं प्राप्तो धर्मरतिं सदा ।
शिवालये समुद्दीप्य दीपान्प्राग्वासनोदयात् ॥ ६४॥

क्वैषा दिक्पालपदवी मुनीश्वर विलोकय ।
मनुष्यधर्मिणानेन साम्प्रतं येह भुज्यते ॥ ६५॥

English
That son of Dīkṣita never cared for any act of piety. It was to steal that he had entered the temple of Śiva. To serve his own end he had brightened the lamp there, thereby dispelling the shadow of darkness on the top of the linga. Then he became the virtuous king of Kaliṅga. O foremost of the sages where the wicked son of the Dīkṣita, and where the guardian of a quarter? Although he had been simply a man, he became the guardian of a quarter. (63-65.)
