### **CHAPTER SEVEN - (Sandhyā gets the name Arundhati and marries Vasiṣṭha)**

---

Sanskrit
७

ब्रह्मोवाच ।
वरं दत्त्वा मुने तस्मिन् शम्भावन्तर्हिते तदा ।
सन्ध्याप्यगच्छत्तत्रैव यत्र मेधातिथिर्मुनिः ॥ १॥

English
Brahmā said :—  O sage, when Śiva vanished after granting her the boons, Sandhyā too went to the place where Medhātithi was performing sacrifice. (1.)

---

Sanskrit
तत्र शम्भोः प्रसादेन न केनाप्युपलक्षिता ।
सस्मार वर्णिनं तं वै स्वोपदेशकरं तपः ॥ २॥

English
She entered the sacrificial hall without being observed by anyone, thanks to Śiva's favour. She recalled to her memory the brahmin boy who had instructed her in the procedure of penance. (2.)

---

Sanskrit
वसिष्ठेन पुरा सा तु वर्णीभूत्वा महामुने ।
उपदिष्टा तपश्चर्तुं वचनात्परमेष्ठिनः ॥ ३॥

English
O great sage, at the bidding of Brahmā, Vasiṣṭha had assumed the guise of a brahmin boy and instructed her in the rites of penance. (3.)

---

Sanskrit
तमेव कृत्वा मनसा तपश्चर्योपदेशकम् ।
पतित्वेन तदा सन्ध्या ब्राह्मणं ब्रह्मचारिणम् ॥ ४॥

समिद्धेऽग्नौ महायज्ञे मुनिभिर्नोपलक्षिता ।
हृष्टा शम्भुप्रसादेन सा विवेश विधेः सुता ॥ ५॥

English
Meditating on that Brahmacārin, her tutor in the mode of austerities, Sandhyā thought of him as her future husband, and entered the blazing sacrificial fire unobserved by the sages. She was delighted that it was by Śiva's favour that she could enter the sacrificial fire. (4-5.)

---

Sanskrit
तस्याः पुरोडाशमयं शरीरं तत्क्षणात्ततः ।
दग्धं हव्यमयं गन्धं तस्तार यदलक्षितम् ॥ ६॥

English
Her body itself had become sacrificial offering in that sacrifice. When it was burnt it could not be distinguished from the ordinary Puroḍāśā since it too had the same fragrance. (6.)

---

Sanskrit
वह्निस्तस्याः शरीरं तु दग्ध्वा सूर्यस्य मण्डलम् ।
शुद्धं प्रवेशयामास शम्भोरेवाज्ञया पुनः ॥ ७॥

English
At the bidding of Śiva, the god of fire sent forth her body to the pure zone of the sun. (7.)

---

Sanskrit
सूर्यो द्वयर्धं विभज्याथ तच्छरीरं तदा रथे ।
स्वके संस्थापयामास प्रीतये पितृदेवयोः ॥ ८॥

English
The sun severed her body into two halves and placed the same on his own chariot for the propitiation of the Pīṭṭs and the Devas. (8.)

---

Sanskrit
तदूर्ध्वभागस्तस्यास्तु शरीरस्य मुनीश्वर ।
प्रातःसन्ध्याभवत्सा तु अहोरात्रादिमध्यगा ॥ ९॥

तच्छेषभागस्तस्यास्तु अहोरात्रान्तमध्यगा ॥

सा सायमभवत्सन्ध्या पितृप्रीतिप्रदा सदा ॥ १०॥

English
O great sage, the upper half of her body became the Prātaḥ Sandhyā (dawn) which is at the beginning or in the middle of a day and night. The lower half of her body became the Sāyaṁsandhyā (dusk) which is in the middle of a day and night. The period is always pleasing to the manes. (9-10.)

---

Sanskrit
सूर्योदयात्तु प्रथमं यदा स्यादरुणोदयः ।
प्रातःसन्ध्या तदोदेति देवानां प्रीतिकारिणी ॥ ११॥

English
Before the sunrise, when the day breaks, the period is called Prātaḥsandhyā. It delights the Gods. (11.)

---

Sanskrit
अस्तं गते ततः सूर्ये शोणपद्मनिभे सदा ।
उदेति सायंसन्ध्यापि पितॄणां मोदकारिणी ॥ १२॥

English
When the sun has set and assumed the hue of a red lotus, the period of Sāyaṁsandhyā sets in. It is delightful to the manes. (12.)

---

Sanskrit
तस्याः प्राणास्तु मनसा शम्भुनाथ दयालुना ।
दिव्येन तु शरीरेण चक्रिरे हि शरीरिणः ॥ १३॥

English
Śiva the merciful, created embodied beings with her vital airs, mind and the divine body. (13.)

---

Sanskrit
मुनेर्यज्ञावसाने तु सम्प्राप्ते मुनिना तु सा ।
प्राप्ता पुत्री वह्निमध्ये तप्तकाञ्चनसुप्रभा ॥ १४॥

English
At the end of the sacrifice, the sage found his daughter in the sacrificial pit shining lustrously like heated gold. (14.)

---

Sanskrit
तां जग्राह तदा पुत्रीं मुनिरामोदसंयुतः ।
यज्ञार्थं तां तु संस्नाप्य निजक्रोडे दधौ मुने ॥ १५॥

English
With very great delight the sage took up the daughter, O sage, as though she were a sacrificial article. He bathed her and kept her on his lap. (15.)

---

Sanskrit
अरुन्धतीति तस्यास्तु नाम चक्रे महामुनिः ।
शिष्यैः परिवृतस्तत्र महामोदमवाप ह ॥ १६॥

English
The great sage gave her the name Arundhati$^{239}$. Surrounded by his disciples he celebrated the event joyously. (16.)

[fn 239. According to another version (cf Vā 70, 79-80, Bd iii, 8, iii, 8, 86-7, Liṅga 1.13, 78-80, Kūrma 1.19.20) Arundhati was the daughter of Kaśyapa, the son of Marīci who also begot on her Nārada and Parvata—two sons. We also know from this source that Nārada gave his sister Arundhati as wife to Vasiṣṭha. In the present context she is said to be the daughter of the sage Medhātithi.]
---

Sanskrit
न रुणद्धि यतो धर्मं सा कस्मादपि कारणात् ।
अतस्त्रिलोके विदितं नाम सम्प्राप तत्स्वयम् ॥ १७॥

English
The word Arundhati means “one who does not hinder sacred rites in any manner whatsoever”. She acquired this name which later on became well-known in the three worlds. (17.)

---

Sanskrit
यज्ञं समाप्य स मुनिः कृतकृत्यभाव-
मासाद्य सम्मदयुतस्तनयाप्रलम्भात् ।
तस्मिन्निजाश्रमपदे सह शिष्यवर्गै-
स्तामेव सन्ततमसौ दयते सुरर्षे ॥ १८॥

English
O celestial sage, that sage concluded the sacrifice with great contentment and was delighted at the acquisition of a daughter. He spent his days in the same hermitage along with his disciples, tending the daughter, mercifully. (18.)

---

Sanskrit
अथ सा ववृधे देवी तस्मिन्मुनिवराश्रमे ।
चन्द्रभागानदीतीरे तापसारण्यसंज्ञके ॥ १९॥

English
The divine lady grew up in the hermitage, Tāpasāraṇya, on the banks of the river—Candrabhāgā. (19.)

---

Sanskrit
सम्प्राप्ते पञ्चमे वर्षे चन्द्रभागां तदा गुणैः ।
तापसारण्यमपि सा पवित्रमकरोत्सती ॥ २०॥

English
When she reached her fifth year, the chaste lady sanctified the environs of the Tāpasāraṇya and the river Candrabhāgā, by virtue of her good qualities. (20.)

---

Sanskrit
विवाहं कारयामासुस्तस्या ब्रह्मसुतेन वै ।
वसिष्ठेन ह्यरुन्धत्या ब्रह्मविष्णुमहेश्वराः ॥ २१॥

English
Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Śiva got her marriage celebrated with Vasiṣṭha, the son of Brahmā. (21.)

---

Sanskrit
तद्विवाहे महोत्साहो बभूव सुखवर्धनः ।
सर्वे सुराश्च मुनयः सुखमापुः परं मुने ॥ २२॥

English
O sage, great festivities in the marriage ceremony increased happiness. The sages and the Gods were very happy on that account. (22.)

---

Sanskrit
ब्रह्मविष्णुमहेशानां करनिस्सृततोयतः ।
सप्तनद्यः समुत्पन्नाः शिप्राद्याः सुपवित्रकाः ॥ २३॥

English
From the water oozing from the hands of Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Śiva, the seven holy rivers Śiprā$^{240}$ and others rose and flowed. (23.)

[fn 240. Śiprā or Kṣiprā, on which Ujjain, the Capital of the Mālava country is situated rises from the Pāripātra or Pāriyātra hills. Fed by its tributaries it flows—in the Mālava Deśa.]
---

Sanskrit
अरुन्धती महासाध्वी साध्वीनां प्रवरोत्तमा ।
वसिष्ठं प्राप्य संरेजे मेधातिथिसुता मुने ॥ २४॥

English
O sage, Arundhatī, the daughter of Medhātithi, the greatest of all chaste ladies shone all the more on attaining Vasiṣṭha. (24.)

---

Sanskrit
यस्याः पुत्राः समुत्पन्नाः श्रेष्ठाः शक्त्यादयः शुभाः ।
वसिष्ठं प्राप्य तं कान्तं संरेजे मुनिसत्तम ॥ २५॥

English
O excellent sage, she secured Vasiṣṭha and bore the auspicious sons Śakti$^{241}$ etc. (25.)

[fn 241. The sage Vasiṣṭha begot 100 sons—Śakti and others, on his wife Arundhatī, here identified with Sandhyā. See Kū. P 1.19.23 : “Arundhatyām Vasiṣṭhastu Sutān utpādayac chatam”. There a slightly different Version in Mat. 200 and 201 Arundhatyām Vasiṣṭhas tu Śaktim Utpādayat Sutam. See AIHT. P. 204.]
---

Sanskrit
एवं सन्ध्याचरित्रं ते कथितं मुनिसत्तम ।
पवित्रं परमं दिव्यं सर्वकामफलप्रदम् ॥ २६॥

य इदं श‍ृणुयान्नारी पुरुषो वा शुभव्रतः ।
सर्वान्कामानवाप्नोति नात्र कार्या विचारणा ॥ २७॥

English
O excellent sage, I have narrated to you the story of Sandhyā. It is holy, sanctifying, divine and bestower of all benefits. He or she who hears this story accompanied by auspicious rites attains all cherished desires. There is no doubt about it. (26-27.)
