### **CHAPTER TWENTYONE - *(The Dalliance of Satī and Śiva)***

---

Sanskrit
२१

नारद उवाच ।
समीचीनं वचस्तात सर्वज्ञस्य तवानघ ।
महाद्भुतं श्रुतं नो वै चरितं शिवयोः शुभम् ॥ १॥

English
*Nārada said :—* O dear, your words are perfect inasmuch as you are omniscient, sinless one. The wonderfully auspicious story of Śivā and Śiva has been heard by us. (1.)

---

Sanskrit
विवाहश्च श्रुतः सम्यक् सर्वमोहापहारकः ।
परमज्ञानसम्पन्नो मङ्गलालय उत्तमः ॥ २॥

English
We have heard the detailed account of their marriage that destroys delusions, makes one endowed with true knowledge and which is excellently auspicious. (2.)

---

Sanskrit
कदाचिद्वन्यपुष्पाणि समाहृत्य मनोहराम् ।
मालां विधाय सत्यास्तु हारस्थाने स योजयत् ॥ ३॥

English
I wish to know more of the auspicious story of Śivā and Śiva. Hence having unequalled consideration for me, O intelligent one, please narrate the same. (3.)

---

Sanskrit
ब्रह्मोवाच ।
सम्यक्कारुणिकस्यैव मुने ते विचिकित्सितम् ।
यदहं नोदितः सौम्य शिवलीलानुवर्णने ॥ ४॥

English
*Brahmā said :—*  Your enquiries for the history of the merciful lord are pursued well, since you have prompted me to narrate the divine sports of Śiva. (4.)

---

Sanskrit
विवाह्य दक्षजां देवीं सतीं त्रैलोक्यमातरम् ।
गत्वा स्वधाम सुप्रीत्या यदकार्षीन्निबोध मे ॥ ५॥

English
Know from me what Śiva did with pleasure on reaching His abode after His marriage with goddess Satī, Dakṣa’s daughter and the mother of the three worlds. (5.)

---

Sanskrit
ततो हरः स सगणः स्वस्थानं प्राप्य मोदनम् ।
देवर्षे तत्र वृषभादवातरदतिप्रियात् ॥ ६॥

English
O celestial sage, after reaching His gay abode along with His Gaṇas, Śiva descended from His Bull with great pleasure. (6.)

---

Sanskrit
यथायोग्यं निजस्थानं प्रविश्य स सतीसखः ।
मुमुदेऽतीव देवर्षे भवाचारकरः शिवः ॥ ७॥

English
O celestial sage, entering His apartment in a befitting manner, along with Satī, Śiva assuming worldly conventions rejoiced very much. (7.)

---

Sanskrit
ततो विरूपाक्ष इमां प्राप्य दाक्षायणीं गणान् ।
स्वीयान्निर्यापयामास नन्द्यादीन् गिरिकन्दरात् ॥ ८॥

English
Then after approaching Satī, Śiva sent out His attendants—Nandin and others, from the cave in the mountain. (8.)

---

Sanskrit
उवाच चैतांस्तान् सर्वान्नन्द्यादीनतिसूनृतम् ।
लौकिकीं रीतिमाश्रित्य करुणासागरः प्रभुः ॥ ९॥

English
Following the manner of the people of the world, the merciful lord spoke these affable and courteous words to Nandin and others. (9.)

---

Sanskrit
महेश उवाच ।
यदाहं च स्मराम्यत्र स्मरणादरमानसाः ।
समागमिष्यथ तदा मत्पार्श्वं मे गणा द्रुतम् ॥ १०॥

English
*Lord Śiva said :—*O my attendants, with minds respectfully concentrated in thinking upon me, you shall come to me only when I remember you. (10.)

---

Sanskrit
इत्युक्ते वामदेवेन नन्द्याद्याः स्वगणाश्च ते ।
महावेगा महावीरा नानास्थानेषु संययुः ॥ ११॥

English
When Śiva said like this, Nandin and others who constituted the powerful set of attendants of quick speed left for different places. (11.)

---

Sanskrit
ईश्वरोऽपि तया सार्धं तेषु यातेषु विभ्रमी ।
दाक्षायण्या समं रेमे रहस्ये मुदितो भृशम् ॥ १२॥

English
When they went away and He was left alone with Satī, Śiva rejoiced much and sported with her. (12.)

---

English
Sometimes He gathered some sylvan flowers and wreathed a fine garland out of them which he put round her neck in the place of the necklace. (13.)

---

Sanskrit
कदाचिद्दर्पणे चैव वीक्षन्तीमात्मनः सतीम् ।
अनुगम्य हरो वक्त्रं स्वीयमप्यवलोकयत् ॥ १४॥

English
While Satī was admiring at the reflection of her face in the mirror, Śiva came behind and peeped into the reflection of His own face. (14.)

---

Sanskrit
कदाचित्कुण्डलं तस्या उल्लास्योल्लास्य सङ्गतः ।
बध्नाति मोचयत्येव सा स्वयं मार्जयत्यपि ॥ १५॥

English
Sometimes He would be sporting with her ear-rings, tying and untying and scrubbing them Himself. (15.)

---

Sanskrit
सरागौ चरणावस्याः यावकेनोज्ज्वलेन च ।
निसर्गरक्तौ कुरुते पूर्णरागौ वृषध्वजः ॥ १६॥

English
Sometimes by the application of red dye Śiva made her naturally red feet completely red. (16.)

---

Sanskrit
उच्चैरपि यदाख्येयमन्येषां पुरतो बहु ।
तत् कर्णे कथयत्यस्या हरो द्रष्टुं तदाननम् ॥ १७॥

English
Many things which could be said aloud even in the presence of many, Śiva whispered into her ears in order to see her face. (17.)

---

Sanskrit
न दूरमपि गन्तासौ समागत्य प्रयत्नतः ।
अनुबध्नाति नामाक्षी पृष्ठदेशेऽन्यमानसाम् ॥ १८॥

English
He would not go far from her, (if at all he went) he would return suddenly and close her eyes from behind and while she was thinking about something else he would ask her his name. (18.)

---

Sanskrit
अन्तर्हितस्तु तत्रैव मायया वृषभध्वजः ।
तामालिलिङ्ग भीत्या स्वं चकिता व्याकुलाभवत् ॥ १९॥

English
Sometimes Śiva would become invisible through His Māyā and suddenly embrace her when she would become terrified and agitated. (19.)

---

Sanskrit
सौवर्णपद्मकलिकातुल्ये तस्याः कुचद्वये ।
चकार भ्रमराकारं मृगनाभिविशेषकम् ॥ २०॥

English
Sometimes with musk He would make marks like bees on her breasts that resembled the buds of a golden lotus. (20.)

---

Sanskrit
हारमस्याः कुचयुगाद्वियोज्य सहसा हरः ।
न्ययोजयच्च तत्रैव स्वकरस्पर्शनं मुहुः ॥ २१॥

English
Sometimes he would take the necklace off her breasts and press them with his hands. (21.)

---

Sanskrit
अङ्गदान्वलयानूर्मान्विश्लेष्य च पुनः पुनः ।
तत्स्थानात्पुनरेवासौ तत्स्थाने प्रत्ययोजयत् ॥ २२॥

English
Sometimes he would remove the bracelets, bangles, rings from their places and fix them again one by one. (22.)

---

Sanskrit
कालिकेति समायाति सवर्णा ते सखी त्विमाम् ।
यास्यत्वस्यास्तथेक्षन्त्याः प्रोत्तुङ्गौ साहसं कुचौ ॥ २३॥

English
Even as she was looking on, sometimes he would come to her lofty breasts saying with laughter, this dark spot “Kālikā” on your breasts is your companion of the same colour as it contains the same letters as are found in your name “Kālikā”.$^{275}$ (23.)

[fn 275. The text of the second half of the verse is corrupt; hence the translation of that portion is conjectural.]
---

Sanskrit
कदाचिन्मदनोन्मादचेतनः प्रमथाधिपः ।
चकार नर्म शर्माणि तथाकृत्प्रियया मुदा ॥ २४॥

English
Sometimes when he was too much excited with love he would exchange pleasantries with his beloved. (24.)

---

Sanskrit
आहृत्य पद्मपुष्पाणि रम्यपुष्पाणि शङ्करः ।
सर्वाङ्गेषु करोति स्म पुष्पाभरणमादरात् ॥ २५॥

English
Sometimes he would gather lotuses and other beautiful flowers and decorate her with them as though with ornaments. (25.)

---

Sanskrit
गिरिकुञ्जेषु रम्येषु सत्या सह महेश्वरः ।
विजहार समस्तेषु प्रियया भक्तवत्सलः ॥ २६॥

English
In the company of his beloved Śivā, Śiva who is favourably disposed to His devotees, sported about among the mountain hedges. (26.)

---

Sanskrit
तया विना स्म नो याति नास्थितो न स्म चेष्टते ।
तया विना क्षणमपि शर्म लेभे न शङ्करः ॥ २७॥

English
Without her, he did not move anywhere, he did not stay anywhere, he did not carry on any activity without her company. Śiva was not happy without her even for a moment. (27.)

---

Sanskrit
विहृत्य सुचिरं कालं कैलासगिरिकुञ्जरे ।
अगमद्धिमवत्प्रस्थं सस्मार स्वेच्छया स्मरम् ॥ २८॥

English
After dallying among the hedges and grottos in the Kailāsa mountain for a long time he went to the Himālayan ridges where he remembered Kāma out of his own accord. (28.)

---

Sanskrit
तस्मिन्प्रविष्टे कामे तु वसन्तः शङ्करान्तिके ।
वितस्तार निजं भावं हार्दं विज्ञाय यत्प्रभोः ॥ २९॥

English
When Kāma reached the vicinity of Śiva, Spring spread all his splendour in accord with the inclination of the lord. (29.)

---

English
The trees and creepers blossomed and bloomed. Waters were covered with full blown lotuses. Bees hovered round the lotuses. (30.)

---

Sanskrit
सर्वे च पुष्पिता वृक्षा लताश्चान्याश्च पुष्पिताः ।
अम्भांसि फुल्लपद्मानि पद्माः सभ्रमरास्तथा । ३०॥

प्रविष्टे सदृतौ तत्र ववौ स मलयो मरुत् ।
सुगन्धिगन्धपुष्पेण मोदकश्च सुगन्धियुक् ॥ ३१॥

English
When that excellent season set in, the gentle Malaya breeze fragrant and delightful due to sweet smelling flowers blew all round. (31.)

---

Sanskrit
सन्ध्यार्धचन्द्रसङ्काशाः पलाशाश्च विरेजिरे ।
कामास्त्रवत्सुमनसः प्रमोदात्पादपाधरः ॥ ३२॥

English
The Palāśa flowers resembling the hue of the twilight and shaped like the crescent moon shone like the flowery arrows of Kāma at the feet of trees. (32.)

---

Sanskrit
बभुः पङ्कजपुष्पाणि सरस्सु सकलाञ्जनान् ।
सम्मोहयितुमुद्युक्ता सुमुखी वायुदेवता ॥ ३३॥

English
The lotus flowers shone in the lakes. The goddess wind endeavoured to fascinate people with her sweet face. (33.)

---

Sanskrit
नागकेशरवृक्षाश्च स्वर्णवर्णैः प्रसूनकैः ।
बभुर्मदनकेत्वाभा मनोज्ञाः शङ्करान्तिके ॥ ३४॥

English
With their flowers golden in hue, the Nāgakesara trees shone beautifully like the banners of Kāma. (34.)

---

Sanskrit
लवङ्गवल्ली सुरभिगन्धेनोद्वास्य मारुतम् ।
मोहयामास चेतांसि भृशं कामिजने पुरा ॥ ३५॥

English
Rendering the breeze fragrant with its smell the clove creeper fascinated the minds of passionate people with its sweetness. (35.)

---

Sanskrit
चारुचर्चितभृङ्गौघाः सुस्वराश्चूतशालिनः ।
बभुर्मदनबाणौघपर्यङ्कमदनावृताः ॥ ३६॥

English
The mango trees and the Śāli plants shining like mild fire shone like the open couches for the flowery arrows of Kāma. (36.)

---

Sanskrit
अम्भांसि मलहीनानि रेजुः फुल्लकुशाशयाः ।
मुनीनामिव चेतांसि प्रव्यक्तज्योतिरुद्गमम् ॥ ३७॥

English
With full-blown lotuses, the pure waters of the lakes shone like the minds of sages wherein the supreme splendour—Ātman is clearly reflected. (37.)

---

Sanskrit
तुषाराः सूर्यरश्मीनां सङ्गमादगमन् बहिः ।
प्रमत्वानीक्ष्यतेक्षाश्च सलिलीहृदयास्तदा ॥ ३८॥

English
The dew-drops as they came in contact with the rays of the sun turned in vapours like the hearts of the people turning pure in association with the good.²⁷⁶ (38.)

[fn 276. The text of the second half of the verse is corrupt; hence the translation of that portion is conjectural.]
---

Sanskrit
प्रसन्नाः सह चन्द्रेण निर्नीशारास्तदाऽभवन् ।
विभावर्यः प्रियेणैवं कामिन्यः सुमनोहराः ॥ ३९॥

English
The nights became bright with the moon devoid of mist. Lovely women shone beautifully in the company of their lovers. (39.)

---

Sanskrit
तस्मिन्काले महादेवः सह सत्या धरोत्तमे ।
रेमे स सुचिरं छन्दं निकुञ्जेषु नदीषु च ॥ ४०॥

English
In this atmosphere, on that excellent mountain, Lord Śiva sported about for a long time among the groves, hedges and streams in the company of Satī. (40.)

---

Sanskrit
तथा तेन समं रेजे तदा दाक्षायणी मुने ।
यथा हरः क्षणमपि शान्तिमाप तया विना ॥ ४१॥

English
O sage, then Satī so exercised her splendid influence on Śiva that he did not have mental peace without her even for a moment. (41.)

---

Sanskrit
सम्भोगविषये देवी सती तस्य मनःप्रिया ।
विशतीव हरस्याङ्गे पाययन्निव तद्रसम् ॥ ४२॥

English
The goddess satisfied his mind in fulness in the matter of intercourse. She seemed to enter his body. He made her drink that juice. (42.)

---

Sanskrit
तस्याः कुसुममालाभिर्भूषयन्सकलां तनुम् ।
स्वहस्तरचिताभिस्तु नवशर्माकरोच्च सः ॥ ४३॥

English
With garlands of flowers wreathed by himself he decorated her person and felt new pleasures. (43.)

---

Sanskrit
आलापैर्वीक्षितैर्हास्यैस्तथा सम्भाषणैर्हरः ।
तस्यादिदेश गिरिजां शंसतीवात्मसंविदम् ॥ ४४॥

English
With diverse conversations, glances, joking remarks and exchanges of pleasantries he instructed Śiva in the knowledge of Self. (44.)

---

Sanskrit
तद्वक्त्रचन्द्रपीयूषपानस्थिरतनुर्हरः ।
नानावैशेषिकीं तन्वीमवस्थां स कदाचन ॥ ४५॥

English
Drinking the nectar from her moon-face, Śiva stabilised his body. Sometimes he experienced exhilarating and particularly pleasing state. (45.)

---

Sanskrit
तद्वक्त्राम्बुजवासेन तत्सौन्दर्य्यैश्च नर्मभिः ।
गुणैरिव महादन्ती बद्धो नान्यविचेष्टितः ॥ ४६॥

English
Just as a huge elephant that is bound with ropes cannot have any other activity. He was also bound by the sweet fragrance of her lotus-like face, her beauty and her jocular pleasantries. (46.)

---

Sanskrit
इति हिमगिरिकुञ्जप्रस्थभागे दरीषु
प्रतिदिनमभिरेमे दक्षपुत्र्या महेशः ।
क्रतुभुजपरिमाणैः क्रीडतस्तस्य जाता
दश दश च सुरर्षे वत्सराः पञ्च चान्ये ॥ ४७॥

English
Thus in the ridges and caverns of the Himālayan mountains, the lord sported about in the company of Satī every day. According to the calculation of the devas twenty five years elapsed, O celestial sage, during which he dallied thus. (47.)
