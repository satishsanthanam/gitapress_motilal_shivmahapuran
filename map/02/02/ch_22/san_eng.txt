### **CHAPTER TWENTYTWO - *(The dalliance of Śivā and Śivā on the Himālayas)* - *Brahmā said:—***

---

Sanskrit
२२

ब्रह्मोवाच ।
कदाचिदथ दक्षस्य तनया जलदागमे ।
कैलासक्ष्माभृतः प्राह प्रस्थस्थं वृषभध्वजम् ॥ १॥

English
Once at the advent of clouds, Dakṣa’s daughter said to Śiva who was halting on the ridge of Kailāsa mountain. (1.)

---

Sanskrit
सत्युवाच ।
देवदेव महादेव शम्भो मत्प्राणवल्लभ ।
श‍ृणु मे वचनं नाथ श्रुत्वा तत्कुरु मानद ॥ २॥

English
*Satī said:—*  O lord of devas, O Śiva my dear husband, please hear my words and do accordingly, O bestower of honour. (2.)

---

Sanskrit
घनागमोऽयं सम्प्राप्तः कालः परमदुःसहः ।
अनेकवर्णमेघौघाः सङ्गीताम्बरदिक्चयाः ॥ ३॥

English
The most unbearable season of the advent of clouds has arrived with clusters of clouds of diverse hues, and their music reverberating in the sky and the various quarters. (3.)

---

Sanskrit
विवान्ति वाता हृदयं हारयन्तीति वेगिनः ।
कदम्बरजसा धौताः पाथोबिन्दुविकर्षणाः ॥ ४॥

English
The speedy gusts of wind scattering sprays of water mingled with nectarine drops from the Kadamba flowers captivate the heart as they blow. (4.)

---

Sanskrit
मेघानां गर्जितैरुच्चैर्धारासारं विमुञ्चताम् ।
विद्युत्पताकिनां तीव्रैः क्षुब्धं स्यात्कस्य नो मनः ॥ ५॥

English
Whose mind will not be agitated by the loud and forceful rumblings of the clouds that release a heavy downpour and have the beams of lightning for their ensign? (5.)

---

Sanskrit
न सूर्यो दृश्यते नापि मेघच्छन्नो निशापतिः ।
दिवापि रात्रिवद्भाति विरहिव्यसनाकरः ॥ ६॥

English
Covered by the clouds neither the sun nor the moon is visible. Even the day appears like the night and it distresses those who are separated from their lovers. (6.)

---

Sanskrit
मेघा नैकत्र तिष्ठन्तो ध्वनन्तः पवनेरिताः ।
पतन्त इव लोकानां दृश्यन्ते मूर्ध्नि शङ्कर ॥ ७॥

English
O Śiva, tossed about by the gusts of wind the clouds do not remain steady in any place, they rumble and appear as if they would fall on the heads of the people. (7.)

---

Sanskrit
वाताहता महावृक्षा नर्तन्त इव चाम्बरे ।
दृश्यन्ते हर भीरूणां त्रासदाः कामुकेप्सिताः ॥ ८॥

English
Huge trees struck down by the wind appear to dance in the sky, terrifying the cowards and delighting the lover, O Śiva. (8.)

---

Sanskrit
स्निग्धनीलाञ्जनस्याशु सदिवौघस्य पृष्ठतः ।
बलाकराजीवात्युच्चैर्यमुनापृष्ठफेनवत् ॥ ९॥

English
Flocks of cranes above the clouds glossy and blue like the collyrium shine like foams on the surface of Yamunā. (9.)

---

Sanskrit
क्षपायामीश वलयो दृश्यन्ते करभाश्रिताः ।
अम्बुधाविव सन्दीप्तपावको वडवामुखः ॥ १०॥

English
During the close of the nights the circle of lightning appears like the blazing submarine$^{277}$ fire in the ocean. (10.)

[fn 277. Baḍavāmukha variously called Baḍavāṇala, Aurva etc. is a submarine fire, represented as a flame with a horse’s head. According to Paurāṇic Mythology it devours all things including the Gods, Asuras, and Rākṣasas at the dissolution of the Universe.]
---

Sanskrit
प्रारोहन्तीह सस्यानि मन्दरे प्राङ्गणेष्वपि ।
किमन्यत्र विरूपाक्ष सस्योद्भूतिं वदाम्यहम् ॥ ११॥

English
O odd-eyed Śiva, here even in the courtyards of the temples, plants grow; need I mention the growth of plants elsewhere? (11.)

---

Sanskrit
श्यामलै राजतै रक्तैर्विशदोऽयं हिमाचलः ।
मन्दराश्रयमेघौघः पत्रैर्दुग्धाम्बुधिर्यथा ॥ १२॥

English
With the clusters of clouds dark, silvery and red in colour clinging to the Mandara mountain (peak), Himālaya appears as the ocean of milk with the birds of diverse colours. (12.)

---

Sanskrit
असमश्रीश्च कुटिलं भेजे यस्याथ किंशुकान् ।
उच्चावचान् कलौ लक्ष्मीर्गन्ता सन्त्यज्य सज्जनान् ॥ १३॥

English
Unrivalled splendour has resorted to the Kimśuka flowers devoid of odour, as Lakṣmī (the Goddess of fortune) abandons good people and resorts to the crooked, whether of high or low birth. (13.)

---

Sanskrit
मन्दराचलमेघानां शब्देन हृषिता मुहुः ।
केकायन्ते प्रतिवने सततं पृष्ठसूचकम् ॥ १४॥

English
The peacocks are delighted at the sound of the cloud over the Mandara mountain. Their gleeful cackles and out-stretched tails indicate the incessant pleasure of their heart. (14.)

---

Sanskrit
मेघोत्सुकानां मधुरश्चातकानां मनोहरः ।
धारासारशरैस्तापं पेतुः प्रतिपथोद्गतम् ॥ १५॥

English
The sweet and delightful sounds of the Cātaka birds that are fond of clouds fall upon the way-farers like the arrows of rain-showers causing incessant pain. (15.)

---

Sanskrit
मेघानां पश्य मद्देहे दुर्नयं करकोत्करैः ।
ये छादयन्त्यनुगते मयूरांश्चातकांस्तथा ॥ १६॥

English
See the wickedness perpetrated by the clouds on my body. They are pelting it with hailstones. But they cover and protect the peacocks and Cātakas who are their followers. (16.)

---

Sanskrit
शिखिसारङ्गयोर्दृष्ट्वा मित्रादपि पराभवम् ।
हर्षं गच्छन्ति गिरिश विदूरमपि मानसम् ॥ १७॥

English
On seeing the distress of peacocks and deer from even their friend (sun), the swans go even to the distant Mānasa lake on the top of the mountain. (17.)

---

Sanskrit
एतस्मिन्विषमे काले नीडं काकाश्चकोरकाः ।
कुर्वन्ति त्वां विना गेहान् कथं शान्तिमवाप्स्यसि ॥ १८॥

English
In this troublesome time, even crows and Cakora birds build their nests. But you don't. Without a home how will you be happy? (18.)

---

Sanskrit
महतीवाद्य नो भीतिर्मां मेघोत्था पिनाकधृक् ।
यतस्व यस्माद्वासाय माचिरं वचनान्मम ॥ १९॥

English
O Pināka-bearer<sup>278</sup> Śiva, let not the great fear originating from clouds befall us. Hence endeavour for a residence. Do not delay. Heed my words. (19.)

[fn 278. Pināka-dhrk. It is the name of Śiva derived from wielding a staff, bow or trident.]

---

Sanskrit
कैलासे वा हिमाद्रौ वा महाकोश्यामथ क्षितौ ।
तत्रोपयोग्यं संवासं कुरु त्वं वृषभध्वज ॥ २०॥

English
O bull-emblemed God, either in Kailāsa or in the Himālayas or in Mahākāśī on the earth you make a befitting habitation. (20.)

---

Sanskrit
ब्रह्मोवाच ।
एवमुक्तस्तया शम्भुर्दाक्षायण्या तथासकृत् ।
सञ्जहास च शीर्षस्थचन्द्ररश्मिस्मितालयम् ॥ २१॥

English
*Brahmā said :—*  Thus advised by Satī frequently Śiva laughed provoking a smile from the moon on his head by way of its beams. (21.)

---

Sanskrit
अथोवाच सतीं देवीं स्मिताभिन्नौष्ठसम्पुटः ।
महात्मा सर्वतत्त्वज्ञस्तोषयन्परमेश्वरः ॥ २२॥

English
Then the high-souled lord Śiva who knew all the principles spoke to Sati with a smile breaking his lips asunder and consoling her. (22.)

---

Sanskrit
ईश्वरः उवाच ।
यत्र प्रीत्यै मया कार्यो वासस्तव मनोहरे ।
मेघास्तत्र न गन्तारः कदाचिदपि मत्प्रिये ॥ २३॥

English
*Śiva said :—*  “O my beloved, beautiful woman, clouds will not reach the place where I have to make an abode for you. (23.)

---

Sanskrit
मेघा नितम्बपर्यन्तं सञ्चरन्ति महीभृतः ।
सदा प्रालेयसानोस्तु वर्षास्वपि मनोहरे ॥ २४॥

English
O comely lass ! even in the rainy seasons the clouds move about in the side ridges alone of the Himālayas. (24.)

---

Sanskrit
कैलासस्य तथा देवि पादगाः प्रायशो घनाः ।
सञ्चरन्ति न गच्छन्ति तत ऊर्ध्वं कदाचन ॥ २५॥

English
O gentle lady, the clouds usually come only upto the foot of Kailāsa. They never go above it. (25.)

---

Sanskrit
सुमेरोर्वा गिरेरूर्ध्वं न गच्छन्ति बलाहकाः ।
जम्बूमूलं समासाद्य पुष्करावर्तकादयः ॥ २६॥

English
The clouds never go above the mountain Sumeru. The clouds Puṣkara, Āvartaka etc reach the foot of Jambu (and return). (26.)

---

Sanskrit
इत्युक्तेषु गिरीन्द्रेषु यस्योपरि भवेद्धि ते ।
मनोरुचिर्निवासाय तमाचक्ष्व द्रुतं हि मे ॥ २७॥

English
Of these mountains I have mentioned you can choose one for residence as you desire. Please tell me quickly where you wish to reside. (27.)

---

Sanskrit
स्वेच्छाविहारैस्तव कौतुकानि
सुवर्णपक्षानिलवृन्दवृन्दैः ।
शब्दोत्तरङ्गैर्मधुरस्वनैस्तै-
र्मुदोपगेयानि गिरौ हिमोत्थे ॥ २८॥

English
On the Himālayan mountains, songs exciting your curiosity and enthusiastic gaiety shall be sung by clusters and swarms of bees with sweet humming sounds as they play about as they please. (28.)

---

Sanskrit
सिद्धाङ्गनास्ते रचितासना भुवं
काङ्क्षन्ति चैवोपहृतं सकौतुकम् ।
स्वेच्छाविहारे मणिकुट्टिमे गिरौ
कुर्वन्ति चेष्यन्ति फलादिदानकैः ॥ २९॥

English
On that mountain at the time when you wish to sport about, the Siddha women will gaily offer you a seat on the jewel-studded platform and gladly present you with fruits and other gifts. (29.)

---

Sanskrit
फणीन्द्रकन्या गिरिकन्यकाश्च
या नागकन्याश्च तुरङ्गमुख्याः ।
सर्वास्तु तास्ते सततं सहायतां
समाचरिष्यन्त्यनुमोदविभ्रमैः ॥ ३०॥

English
The daughters of the king of serpents, the mountain damsels, the Nāga ladies and the Turaṅga-Mukhīs will assist you in their excited flutter in congratulating you. (30.)

---

Sanskrit
रूपं तदेवमतुलं वदनं सुचारु
दृष्ट्वाङ्गना निजवपुर्निजकान्तिसह्यम् ।
हेला निजे वपुषि रूपगणेषु नित्यं
कर्तार इत्यनिमिषेक्षणचारुरूपाः ॥ ३१॥

English
Seeing your face of unequalled splendour and beauty and your body of uncommon lustre, the celestial ladies there, despising their own beauty and lacking in interest in their own qualities will begin to stare at you with winkless eyes. (31.)

---

Sanskrit
या मेनका पर्वतराज जाया
रूपैर्गुणैः ख्यातवती त्रिलोके ।
सा चापि ते तत्र मनोऽनुमोदं
नित्यं करिष्यत्यनुनाथनाद्यैः ॥ ३२॥

English
Menakā,$^{279}$ the wife of the king of mountains famous in the three worlds for her beauty and good qualities will delight you very much through words of entreaties. (32.)

[fn 279. Menakā or Menā. She is the wife of Himavat and mother of Pārvatī and Gaṅgā and of a son named Maināka.]
---

Sanskrit
पुरस्थवर्गैर्गिरिराजवन्द्यैः
प्रीतिं विचिन्वद्भिरुदाररूपाम् ।
शिक्षा सदा ते खलु शोचितापि
कार्यान्वहं प्रीतियुता गुणाद्यैः ॥ ३३॥

English
The honourable ladies of Himālaya’s harem will cause immense pleasure to your gracious Self. They will impart you useful instruction, though you need none, with pleasure every day. (33.)

---

Sanskrit
विचित्रैः कोकिलालापमोदैः कुञ्जगणावृतम् ।
सदा वसन्तप्रभवं गन्तुमिच्छसि किं प्रिये ॥ ३४॥

नानाबहुजलापूर्णसरः शीतसमावृतम् ।
पद्मिनीशतशोयुक्तमचलेन्द्रं हिमालयम् ॥ ३५॥

English
O beloved, do you wish to go to the Himālayas, the king of mountains wherein there is spring for ever, which abounds in hedges and groves where the cuckoos coo in diverse pleasing ways and which contains many lakes filled with cool water and hundreds of lotuses. (34-35.)

---

Sanskrit
सर्वकामप्रदैर्वृक्षैः शाद्वलैः कल्पसंज्ञकैः ।
सक्षणं पश्य कुसुमान्यथाश्वकरिगोव्रजम् ॥ ३६॥

English
It is of full grassy plains and trees that yield everything one desires and hence on a par with Kalpa$^{280}$ trees. You can see plenty of flowers, horses, elephants and cows there. (36.)

[fn 280. Kalpa-Vṛkṣa. One of the five trees of Indra's paradise fabled to fulfil all desires, the other four being मन्द्रार, पारिजातक, सन्तान and हुरिचन्द्रन ।]
---

Sanskrit
प्रशान्तश्वापदगणं मुनिभिर्यतिभिर्वृतम् ।
देवालये महामाये नानामृगगणैर्युतम् ॥ ३७॥

English
There in the Himālayas even the beasts of prey are calm. It is the abode of many sages and ascetics. It is an abode of devas and many deer move about in it. (37.)

---

Sanskrit
स्फाटिकैः स्वर्णवप्राद्यै राजतैश्च विराजितम् ।
मानसादिसरोरङ्गैरभितः परिशोभितम् ॥ ३८॥

English
It shines with ramparts of crystals, gold and silver. It is lustrous with the lakes—Mānasa and others. (38.)

---

Sanskrit
हिरण्मयै रत्ननालैः पङ्कजैर्मुकुलैर्वृतम् ।
शिशुमारैस्तथासङ्ख्यैः कच्छपैर्मकरैः करैः ॥ ३९॥

English
It abounds in buds and full-blown lotuses with golden stalks studded with gems. Crocodiles, sharks and tortoises abound in the lakes. (39.)

---

Sanskrit
निषेवितं मञ्जुलैश्च तथा नीलोत्पलादिभिः ।
देवेशि तस्मान्मुक्तैश्च सर्वगन्धैश्च कुङ्कुमैः ॥ ४०॥

लसद्गन्धजलैः शुभ्रैरापूर्णैः स्वच्छकान्तिभिः ।
शाद्वलैस्तरुणैस्तुङ्गैस्तीरस्थैरुपशोभितम् ॥ ४१॥

English
O Goddess of devas, there are many beautiful blue lotuses emitting sweet fragrance. On the banks there are many grass lands, small and big trees and the saffron flowers increasing the fragrance of the waters with which the lakes are full. (40-41.)

---

Sanskrit
नृत्यद्भिरिव शाखोटैर्वर्जयन्तं स्वसम्भवम् ।
कामदेवैः सारसैश्च मत्तचक्राङ्गशोभितैः ॥ ४२॥

English
The Apricot tree seems to dance with their oscillating branches. They seem to be fanning the self-born god of love. There are Sārasa birds and the intoxicated Cakravāka birds heightening its beauty. (42.)

---

Sanskrit
मधुराराविभिर्मोदकारिभिर्भ्रमरादिभिः ।
शब्दायमानं च मुदा कामोद्दीपनकारकम् ॥ ४३॥

वासवस्य कुबेरस्य यमस्य वरुणस्य च ।
अग्नेः कोणपराजस्य मारुतस्य परस्य च ॥ ४४॥

पुरीभिः शोभिशिखरं मेरोरुच्चैः सुरालयम् ।
रम्भाशचीमेनकादिरम्भोरुगणसेवितम् ॥ ४५॥

English
The different parts of the mountain Meru seem to be echoing the pleasing sweet sounds of bees etc. which cause the incitement of love of the guardians of the quarters viz. Indra, Kubera, Yama, Varuṇa, Agni, Nirṛti, Marut (Wind)$^{281}$ and the Supreme lord (Īśa). Heaven, the abode of the Devas is stationed on the summits of the Meru wherein the cities of the guardians of the quarters are also situated. They are brilliant. Beautiful celestial damsels, Rambhā, Śacī, Menakā$^{282}$ and others heighten their glory. (43-45.)

[fn 282. Reference is to Indra, Kubera, Yama, Varuṇa, Agni, Nirṛti, Vāyu and Īśāna who are the lords of eight quarters.]
---

Sanskrit
किं त्वमिच्छसि सर्वेषां पर्वतानां हि भूभृताम् ।
सारभूते महारम्ये संविहर्तुं महागिरौ ॥ ४६॥

English
Do you wish to sport about on this great mountain which is very beautiful and which appears to contain the essence of all mountains ? (46.)

---

Sanskrit
तत्र देवी सखियुता साप्सरोगणमण्डिता ।
नित्यं करिष्यति शची तव योग्यां सहायताम् ॥ ४७॥

English
There, the Queen Śacī attended by her chaperons and celestial damsels will assist you always. (47.)

---

Sanskrit
अथवा मम कैलासे पर्वतेन्द्रे सदाश्रये ।
स्थानमिच्छसि वित्तेशपुरीपरिविराजिते ॥ ४८॥

English
Or do you wish to have an abode in my own Kailāsa, the great mountain affording shelter to the good and enhanced in beauty by the luminous city of Kubera ?$^{283}$ (48.)

[fn 283. Rambhā, Śacī, Menakā etc are the heavenly nymphs famous for their personal charms. They are skilful in winning over the minds even of the ascetics practising hard austerities.]

---

Sanskrit
गङ्गाजलौघप्रयते पूर्णचन्द्रसमप्रभे ।
दरीषु सानुषु सदा ब्रह्मकन्याभ्युदीरिते ॥ ४९॥

नानामृगगणैर्युक्ते पद्माकरशतावृते ।
