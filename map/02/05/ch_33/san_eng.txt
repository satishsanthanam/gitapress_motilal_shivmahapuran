### **CHAPTER THIRTYTHREE - *(March of The Victorious Lord Śiva)***

---

Sanskrit
३३

सनत्कुमार उवाच ।
तस्य दूतस्य तद्वाक्यमाकर्ण्य सुरराट् ततः ।
सक्रोधः प्राह गिरिशो वीरभद्रादिकान्गणान् ॥ १॥

English
*Sanatkumāra said:—*  On hearing those words of the emissary, the infuriated emperor of the gods, Śiva spoke to Vīrabhadra and other Gaṇas. (1.)

---

Sanskrit
रुद्र उवाच ।
हे वीरभद्र हे नन्दिन् क्षेत्रपालष्टभैरवाः ।
सर्वे गणाश्च सन्नद्धाः सायुधा बलशालिनः ॥ २॥

कुमाराभ्यां सहैवाद्य निर्गच्छन्तु ममाज्ञया ।
स्वसेनया भद्रकाली निर्गच्छतु रणाय च ॥

शङ्खचूडवधार्थाय निर्गच्छाम्यद्य सत्वरम् ॥ ३॥

English
*Śiva said.*  “O Vīrabhadra, O Nandin, O eight Bhairavas,* the frontier guards,²⁵² let the Gaṇas start along with my sons at my bidding. Let those strong ones be ready and fully equipped with weapons. Let Bhadrakālī start with her army for the war. I start just now for slaying Śaṅkhacūḍa”. (2-3.)

[fn ²⁵². Kṣetrapālas are tutelary deities whose number is given as fortynine.]
---

Sanskrit

सर्वे वीरगणास्तस्यानुययुः सम्प्रहर्षिताः ॥ ४॥

English
*Sanatkumāra said:—* Having ordered thus, lord Śiva started along with his army. His delighted heroic Gaṇas followed him. (4.)

English

English
The leading chiefs of the Gaṇas were Virabhadra, Nandin, Mahākāla, Subhadraka, Viśālākṣa, Bāṇa, Piṅgalākṣa, Vikampana, Virūpa, Vikṛti, Maṇibhadra, Bāskala, Kapila, Dīrghadamṣṭra, Vikara, Tāmralocana, Kālaṅkara, Balibhadra, Kālajihva, Kuṭicara, Balonmatta, Raṇaślāghya, Durjaya, Durgama and others. I shall enumerate the number of Gaṇas they had. Listen attentively. (6-9.)

Sanskrit
चन्द्रभागानदीतीरे वटमूले मनोहरे ।
तत्र तस्थौ महादेवो देवनिस्तारहेतवे ॥ ४९॥

English
The great god stationed himself at the foot of a beautiful Banyan tree on the banks of the river Candrabhāgā<sup>259</sup> for the emancipation of the gods. (49.)

[fn 259. Candrabhāgā, (Mod. Chenab, Ptolemy's Sandabaga) rises from the foot of the Himalayas in two rivulets Chandrā and Bhāgā which join at Tandi. The joint stream is known as Chandrabhāgā. See Note 237 P. 235.]
