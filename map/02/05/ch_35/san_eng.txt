### **CHAPTER THIRTYFIVE - (The conversation between Śiva and the emissary of Śaṅkhacūḍa)**

---

Sanskrit
३५

सनत्कुमार उवाच ।
तत्र स्थित्वा दानवेन्द्रो महान्तं दानवेश्वरम् ।
दूतं कृत्वा महाविज्ञं प्रेषयामास शङ्करम् ॥ १॥

English
*Sanatkumāra said:—* Stationing himself there, the lord of Dānavas sent a leading Dānava of great knowledge as his emissary to Śiva. (1.)

---

Sanskrit
स तत्र गत्वा दूतश्च चन्द्रभालं ददर्श ह ।
वटमूले समासीनं सूर्यकोटिसमप्रभम् ॥ २॥

English
The emissary went there and saw the moon-crested lord Śiva, of the refulgence of a crore suns, seated at the root of the Banyan tree. (2.)

---

Sanskrit
कृत्वा योगासनं दृष्ट्या मुद्रायुक्तं च सस्मितम् ।
शुद्धस्फटिकसङ्काशं ज्वलन्तं ब्रह्मतेजसा ॥ ३॥

English
He saw him sitting in a yogic pose, showing the mystic gesture with his eyes, with a smiling face and body as pure as crystal and blazing with transcendent splendour. (3.)

---

Sanskrit
त्रिशूलपट्टिशधरं व्याघ्रचर्माम्बरावृतम् ।
भक्तमृत्युहरं शान्तं गौरीकान्तं त्रिलोचनम् ॥ ४॥

तपसां फलदातारं कर्त्तारं सर्वसम्पदाम् ।
आशुतोषं प्रसन्नास्यं भक्तानुग्रहकारकम् ॥ ५॥

विश्वनाथं विश्वबीजं विश्वरूपं च विश्वजम् ।
विश्वेस्वरं विश्वकरं विश्वसंहारकारणम् ॥ ६॥

कारणं कारणानां च नरकार्णवतारकम् ।
ज्ञानप्रदं ज्ञानबीजं ज्ञानानन्दं सनातनम् ॥ ७॥

English
Śiva held the trident and the iron club. He was clad in the hide of the tiger. The emissary saw the three-eyed lord of Pārvatī, the enlivener of the life of the devotees, the quiet Śiva, the dispenser of the fruits of penance, the creator of riches, quick in being propitiated, eager to bless the devotees and beaming with pleasure in his face. He saw the lord of the universe, the seed of the universe, identical with the universe and of universal form, born of all, lord of all, creator of all, the cause of the annihilation of the universe, the cause of causes, the one who enables devotees to cross the ocean of hell, the bestower of knowledge, the seed of knowledge, knowlege-bliss and eternal. (4-7.)

---

Sanskrit
अवरुह्य रथाद् दूतस्तं दृष्ट्वा दानवेश्वरः ।
शङ्करं सकुमारं च शिरसा प्रणनाम सः ॥ ८॥

English
On seeing him, the messenger, the leader of Dānavas, descended from his chariot and bowed to him as well as to Kumāra. (8.)

---

Sanskrit
वामतो भद्रकालीं च स्कन्दं तत्पुरतः स्थितम् ।
लोकाशिषं ददौ तस्मै काली स्कन्दश्च शङ्करः ॥ ९॥

English
He saw Bhadrakālī to his left and Kārttikeya standing before him. Kālī, Kārttikeya and Śiva offered him the conventional benediction. (9.)

---

Sanskrit
अथासौ शङ्खचूडस्य दूतः परमशास्त्रवित् ।
उवाच शङ्करं नत्वा करौ बद्ध्वा शुभं वचः ॥ १०॥

English
This emissary of Śaṅkhacūḍa, had full knowledge of the sacred texts. He joined his palms in reverence and bowing to him spoke the auspicious words. (10.)

---

Sanskrit
दूत उवाच ।
शङ्खचूडस्य दूतोऽहं त्वत्सकाशमिहागतः ।
वर्तते ते किमिच्छाद्य तत्त्वं ब्रूहि महेश्वर ॥ ११॥

English
The Emissary said :—  “O lord, I am the emissary of Śaṅkhacūḍa and have come to you. What is it that you desire? Please tell me.” (11.)

---

Sanskrit
सनत्कुमार उवाच ।
इति श्रुत्वा च वचनं शङ्खचूडस्य शङ्करः ।
प्रसन्नात्मा महादेवो भगवांस्तमुवाच ह ॥ १२॥

English
*Sanatkumāra said:—* On hearing these words of Śaṅkhacūḍa, lord Śiva became delighted and spoke. (12.)

---

Sanskrit
महादेव उवाच ।
श‍ृणु दूत महाप्राज्ञ वचो मम सुखावहम् ।
कथनीयमिदं तस्मै निर्विवादं विचार्य च ॥ १३॥

English
*Lord Śiva said:—* O messenger of great intellect, listen to my words conducive to happiness. After pondering over this, without disputation, this shall be mentioned to him. (13.)

---

Sanskrit
विधाता जगतां ब्रह्मा पिता धर्मस्य धर्मवित् ।
मरीचिस्तस्य पुत्रश्च कश्यपस्तत्सुतः स्मृतः ॥ १४॥

English
Brahmā is the creator of the worlds and father of Dharma. He knows virtue. Marīci is his son. Kaśyapa is Marīci's son. (14.)

---

Sanskrit
दक्षः प्रीत्या ददौ तस्मै निजकन्यास्त्रयोदश ।
तास्वेका च दनुः साध्वी तत्सौभाग्यविवर्द्धिनी ॥ १५॥

English
Dakṣa gave him his thirteen daughters, with pleasure. Among them the chaste lady Danu increased his fortune to a great extent. (15.)

---

Sanskrit
चत्वारस्ते दनोः पुत्रा दानवास्तेजसोल्बणाः ।
तेष्वेको विप्रचित्तिस्तु महाबलपराक्रमः ॥ १६॥

English
Danu gave birth to four sons called Dānavas. They were vigorous and powerful. Vipracitti of great strength and valour was one of them. (16.)

---

Sanskrit
तत्पुत्रो धार्मिको दम्भो दानवेन्द्रो महामतिः ।
तस्य त्वं तनयः श्रेष्ठो धर्मात्मा दानवेश्वरः ॥ १७॥

English
His son, the virtuous Dambha of great intellect was the ruler of Dānavas. You are his excellent son, a pious soul, and the lord of Dānavas. (17.)

---

Sanskrit
पुरा त्वं पार्षदो गोपो गोपेष्वेव च धार्मिकः ।
अधुना राधिकाशापाज्जातस्त्वं दानवेश्वरः ॥ १८॥

English
In previous birth you were a cowherd and an attendant of Kṛṣṇa. Among the cowherds you were virtuous. As a result of Rādhā's curse, you are born as Dānava and have become the king of Dānavas. (18.)

---

Sanskrit
दानवीं योनिमायातस्तत्त्वतो न हि दानवः ।
निजवृत्तं पुरा ज्ञात्वा दैववैरं त्यजाधुना ॥ १९॥

English
You are casually born as a Dānava. You are really no Dānava. Realising your previous birth you leave off your inimical attitude to the gods. (19.)

---

Sanskrit
द्रोहं न कुरु तैः सार्द्धं स्वपदं भुङ्क्ष्व सादरम् ।
नाधिकं सविकारं च कुरु राज्यं विचार्य च ॥ २०॥

English
Don't be malicious towards them. You can enjoy your kingdom zealously. Do not try to expand your kingdom nor spoil it. (20.)

---

Sanskrit
देहि राज्यं च देवानां मत्प्रीतिं रक्ष दानव ।
निजराज्ये सुखं तिष्ठ तिष्ठन्तु स्वपदे सुराः ॥ २१॥

English
O Dānava, return their kingdom to the gods. Maintain my affection. Stay in your kingdom happily. Let the gods stay in their region. (21.)

---

Sanskrit
अलं भूतविरोधेन देवद्रोहेण किं पुनः ।
कुलीनाः शुद्धकर्माणः सर्वे कश्यपवंशजाः ॥ २२॥

English
Do not offend people. Don't be malicious to the gods. The descendants of Kaśyapa are noble and indulge in pure activities. (22.)

---

Sanskrit
यानि कानि च पापानि ब्रह्महत्यादिकानि च ।
ज्ञातिद्रोहजपापस्य कलां नार्हन्ति षोडशीम् ॥ २३॥

English
Whatever sin is there in the world, even including that of slaughter of a brahmin, does not merit even a sixteenth part of the sin accruing from the offence towards kinsmen. (23.)

---

Sanskrit
सनत्कुमार उवाच ।
इत्यादिबहुवार्त्तां च श्रुतिस्मृतिपरां शुभाम् ।
प्रोवाच शङ्करः तस्मै बोधयन् ज्ञानमुत्तमम् ॥ २४॥

English
*Sanatkumāra said:—* These and many such words of advice, auspiciously based on injunctions of Śruti and Siṁṭi, Śiva said to him enlightening him in an excellent manner. (24.)

---

Sanskrit
शिक्षितः शङ्खचूडेन स दूतस्तर्कवित्तमः ।
उवाच वचनं नम्रो भवितव्यविमोहितः ॥ २५॥

English
The emissary who had been well instructed by Śaṅkhacūḍa who knew his duties well but who had been deluded by destiny spoke these words humbly. (25.)

---

Sanskrit
दूत उवाच ।
त्वया यत्कथितं देव नान्यथा तत्तथा वचः ।
तथ्यं किञ्चिद्यथार्थं च श्रूयतां मे निवेदनम् ॥ २६॥

English
*The messenger said:—* O lord, what has been narrated by you is true. It cannot be otherwise. But let my submission based on certain factual elements be heard. (26.)

---

Sanskrit
ज्ञातिद्रोहे महत्पापं त्वयोक्तमधुना च यत् ।
तत्किमीशासुराणां च न सुराणां वद प्रभो ॥ २७॥

English
O lord Śiva, verily a great sin has been cited as the result of offence to kinsmen by you now. But does it concern only Asuras and not the gods? Please tell me. (27.)

---

Sanskrit
सर्वेषामिति चेत्तद्वै तदा वच्मि विचार्य च ।
निर्णयं ब्रूहि तत्राद्य कुरु सन्देहभञ्जनम् ॥ २८॥

English
If it applies to all alike, I shall consider it and let you know. Please tell me your decision at the outset and clear my doubts. (28.)

---

Sanskrit
मधुकैटभयोर्दैत्यवरयोः प्रलयार्णवे ।
शिरश्छेदं चकारासौ कस्माच्चक्री महेश्वर ॥ २९॥

English
O lord Śiva, why did the discus-bearing lord Viṣṇu sever the heads of Madhu and Kaitabha$^{269}$ the excellent Daityas in the ocean of dissolution ? (29.)

[fn 269. The Asuras Madhu and Kaitabha born of the ears of Viṣṇu in the ocean at the end of a kalpa rushed against Brahmā who appealed for help to Viṣṇu. Viṣṇu extended his arms, seized the Asuras and slew them with his might]


---

Sanskrit
त्रिपुरैःसह संयुद्धं भस्मत्वकरणं कुतः ।
भवाञ्चकार गिरिश सुरपक्षीति विश्रुतम् ॥ ३०॥

English
Your Majesty too, O Śiva, is famous as a partisan of the gods. Why did you fight with the Tripuras$^{270}$ and reduce them to ashes ? (30.)

[fn 270. For the destruction of Tripuras see ŚP RS. V. Ch. 10]


---

Sanskrit
गृहीत्वा तस्य सर्वस्वं कुतः प्रस्थापितो बलिः ।
सुतलादि समुद्धर्तुं तद्द्वारे च गदाधरः ॥ ३१॥

English
After divesting him of every thing why was Bali packed off to Sutala and other regions$^{271}$ ? Did Viṣṇu go to his threshold as his uplifter ? (31.)

[fn 271. It refers to the legend of Viṣṇu who assuming the form of a dwarf craved from Bali the boon of three steps of ground and then stepping over heaven and earth in two strides left the nether region for Bali's abode.]


---

Sanskrit

English
Why was Hiraṇyākṣa$^{272}$ harassed by the gods along with his brother ? Why were Śumbha$^{273}$ and other Asuras subjected to fall by the gods ? (32.)

[fn 272. Hiraṇyākṣa and Hiraṇyakasipu were slain by Viṣṇu in his Boar and Man-lion incarnations respectively.]

[fn 273. Śumbha, Niṣumbha and other Asuras were killed by the Goddess Durgā at the instance of the gods.]
---

Sanskrit

English
Formerly when the ocean was churned, the nectar was drunk off by the gods.<sup>274</sup> All the strain and stress was ours but the gods reaped the fruit of our endeavour. (33.)

[fn 274. It refers to the churning of the nectar from the ocean by the joint endeavour of the gods and Asuras. The gods drank the nectar while the Asuras were deceived by Viṣṇu and deprived of their legitimate right to drink the same.]
---

Sanskrit
क्रीडाभाण्डमिदं विश्वं कालस्य परमात्मनः ।
स ददाति यदा यस्मै तस्यैश्वर्यं भवेत्तदा ॥ ३४॥

English
The entire universe is but an object of sport of Kāla the supreme soul. Whomsoever and whensoever he pleases to bestow the riches he attains them. (34.)

---

Sanskrit

English
The enmity of the gods and the Dānavas is perpetual and sparked off due to some reason or other. By turns, subject to the whims of Kāla they enjoy victory or defeat. (35.)

---

Sanskrit

English
Interference on your part in the dispute between the two is futile. This does not behove you, the lord who are equally in touch with both. (36.)

---

Sanskrit

English
Your rivalry to us is excessively shameful since you are lord unto the gods as well as to the Asuras. You are the supreme soul. (37.)

---

Sanskrit

English
In the event of your victory your fame is not enhanced. In the event of your defeat you suffer a great loss. Let this disadvantage be pondered over. (38.)

---

Sanskrit

English
*Sanatkumāra said:—* On hearing these words, the three-eyed lord laughed and spoke sweetly to the leading Dānava what seemed proper: (39.)

---

Sanskrit

English
*Lord Śiva said:—* We are subservient to our devotees. We are never independent. We carry out their tasks at their wish. We are not the partisans of any one in particular. (40.)

---

Sanskrit

English
Formerly the fight of Viṣṇu with the excellent Daityas Madhu and Kaiṭabha in the ocean of dissolution was due to the prior request of Brahmā. (41.)

---

Sanskrit

English
For the sake of Prahlāda, at the request of gods, Hiraṇyakaśipu was slain by him acting in the interest of his devotees. (42.)

---

Sanskrit

English
Formerly I fought with the Tripuras and reduced them to ashes, only at the request of the gods. It is well known. (43.)

---

Sanskrit

English
Formerly Pārvatī, the Mother of all, the goddess of all, fought with Śumbha and others and killed them only at the request of the gods. (44.)

---

Sanskrit

English
Even today, the gods have sought refuge in Brahmā. And he along with the gods and the lord Viṣṇu has sought refuge in me. (45.)

---

Sanskrit

सुराणामीश्वरो दूत युद्धार्थमगमं खलु ॥ ४६॥

English
O Emissary, paying heed to the request of Viṣṇu, Brahmā and others, I, though lord of all, have come here in the battle of the gods. (46.)

---

Sanskrit

English
Really you are the foremost of the comrades of Kṛṣṇa, the great soul. Those Daityas who had been formerly killed are not on a par with you. (47.)

---

Sanskrit

English
What is there excessively shameful in my fight with you, O king ? I the lord have been urged humbly to carry out the task of the gods. (48.)

---

Sanskrit

English
Go to Śaṅkhacūḍa and tell him what I have said. Let him do what is proper. I shall carry out the task of the gods. (49.)

---

Sanskrit
इत्युक्त्वा शङ्करस्तत्र विरराम महेश्वरः ।
उत्तस्थौ शङ्खचूडस्य दूतोऽगच्छत्तदन्तिकम् ॥ ५०॥

English
*Sanatkumāra said:—* On saying this, Śiva the great god, stopped. The emissary stood up and returned to Śaṅkhacūḍa. (50.)
