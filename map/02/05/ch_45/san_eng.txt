### **CHAPTER FORTYFIVE - (The beginning of the war and the conversation with the messengers)**

---

Sanskrit
४५

सनत्कुमार उवाच ।
गतस्ततो मत्तगजेन्द्रगामी
पीत्वा सुरां घूर्णितलोचनश्च ।
महानुभावो बहुसैन्ययुक्तः
प्रचण्डवीरो वरवीरयायी ॥ १॥

ददर्श दैत्यः स्मरबाणविद्धो
गुहां ततो वीरकरुद्धमार्गाम् ।
स्निग्धं यथा वीक्ष्य पतङ्गसंज्ञः
दशाप्रदीपं च कृमिर्ह्युपेत्य ॥ २॥

तथा प्रदर्श्याशु पुनः पुनश्च
सम्पीड्यमानोऽपि स वीरकेण ।
बभूव कामाग्निसुदग्धदेहोऽ
न्धको महादैत्यपतिः स मूढः ॥ ३॥

English
Sañatkumāra said:—  Andhaka, the great Daitya king, deluded and smitten by Kāma's arrows drank wine and started from his palace. He walked like an elephant in rut. His eyes were roving. He was accompanied by many of his soldiers. He was fierce and walked majestically like heroes. He saw the cavern guarded by Vīraka, standing at the entrance. He exhibited the characteristic reactions of a moth approaching a lamp and glancing at it eagerly and lovingly. Already the burning fire of passion had scorched him and therefore afflicting hits from Vīraka had no effect on him. (1-3.)

---

Sanskrit
पाषाणवृक्षाशनितोयवह्नि-
भुजङ्गशस्त्रास्त्रबिभीषिकाभिः ।
सम्पीडितोऽसौ न पुनः प्रपीड्यः
पृष्टश्च कस्त्वं समुपागतोऽसि ॥ ४॥

English
He was attacked with stones, trees, thunderbolts, water, fire and serpents. He was threatened with weapons and missiles. He was afflicted by Vīraka repeatedly but ineffectively and asked. "Who are you? Why have you come here?" (4.)

---

Sanskrit
निशम्य तद् गां स्वमतं स तस्मै
चकार युद्धं स तु वीरकेण ।
मुहूर्तमाश्चर्यवदप्रमेयं
सङ्ख्ये जितो वीरतरेण दैत्यः ॥ ५॥

English
On hearing his words, Andhaka made no reply but began to fight Vīraka when surprisingly and unbelievably he was defeated by Vīraka in the battle. (5.)

---

Sanskrit
ततस्तु सङ्ग्रामशिरो विहाय
क्षुत्क्षामकण्ठस्तृषितो गतोऽभूत् ।
चूर्णीकृते खड्गवरे च खिन्ने
पलायमानो गतविस्मयः सः ॥ ६॥

English
When his sword was shattered to pieces he fled from the battlefield divested of his conceited pride. His throat was parched with hunger and thirst. He was aggrieved. (6.)

---

Sanskrit
चक्रुस्तदाजिं सह वीरकेण
प्रह्लादमुख्या दितिजप्रधानाः ।
लज्जाङ्कुशाकृष्टधियो बभूवुः
सुदारुणाः शस्त्रशतैरनेकैः ॥ ७॥

English
Prahlāda and other important Daityas then fought with Vīraka. Though they were terrible themselves they were defeated by hundreds of weapons. Finally their minds were held in check by the goad of shame. (7.)

---

Sanskrit
विरोचनस्तत्र चकार युद्धं
बलिश्च बाणश्च सहस्रबाहुः ।
भजिः कुजम्भस्त्वथ शम्बरश्च
वृत्रादयश्चाप्यथ वीर्यवन्तः ॥ ८॥

English
Virocana, Bali, Bāṇa, Sahasrabāhu, Bhaji, Kujambha, Śambara, Vṛtra and others of great valour, fought there.<sup>302</sup> (8.)

[fn 302. It is incomprehensible how four generations of the Asuras represented by हिरण्यकशिपु, प्रह्लाद, विरोचन and बलि could be contemporaneous, and fight together in the battle against the Asuras.]
---

Sanskrit
ते युद्ध्यमाना विजिताः समन्ताद्
द्विधाकृता वै गणवीरकेण ।
शेषे हतानां बहुदानवाना-
मुक्तं जयत्येव हि सिद्धसङ्घैः ॥ ९॥

English
These were defeated by the Gaṇa Vīraka in the course of the battle and split into two. At the end of the fight when many Dānavas were killed, the Gaṇas of Siddhas shouted “Victory.” (9.)

---

Sanskrit
भेरुण्डजानाभिनयप्रवृत्ते
मेदोवसामांससुपूयमध्ये ।
क्रव्यादसङ्घातसमाकुले तु
भयङ्करे शोणितकर्दमे तु ॥ १०॥

भग्नैस्तु दैत्यैर्भगवान् पिनाकी
व्रतं महापाशुपतं सुघोरम् ।
प्रिये मया यत्कृतपूर्वमासी-
द्दाक्षायणीं प्राह सुसान्त्वयित्वा ॥ ११॥

English
When packs of Jackals began to dance in the midst of the putrefying suets, fat and flesh, when beasts of prey, ghosts and spirits began to roam in the terrible slough of slushy blood, when the Daityas were smashed thus, the trident-bearing lord consoled Pārvatī and said. *Śiva said:—* “O beloved, formerly I had performed the great Vrata called Mahāpāśupata. (10-11.)

---

Sanskrit
शिव उवाच ।
तस्माद्बलं यन्मम तत्प्रणष्टं
मर्त्यैरमर्त्यस्य यतः प्रपातः ।
पुण्यक्षयाही ग्रह एव जातो
दिवानिशं देवि तव प्रसङ्गात् ॥ १२॥

English
The strength that I derived therefrom is exhausted whence this fall of the immortals at the hands of the mortals. O goddess, merit has declined due to the physical contact with you. (12.)

---

Sanskrit
उत्पाद्य दिव्यं परमाद्भुतं तु
पुनर्वरं घोरतरं च गत्वा ।
तस्माद् व्रतं घोरतरं चरामि
सुनिर्भया सुन्दरि वै विशोका ॥ १३॥

English
I will create a wonderfully divine and terrible forest and going there I shall perform still more severe vrata whereby, O beautiful lady, you shall be free from fear and sorrow.” (13.)

---

Sanskrit
सनत्कुमार उवाच ।
एतावदुक्त्वा वचनं महात्मा
उत्पाद्य घोषं शनकैश्चकार ।
स तत्र गत्वा व्रतमुग्रदीप्तो
गतो वनं पुण्यतमं सुघोरम् ॥ १४॥

English
*Sanatkumāra said:—* After saying this, the noble soul went to a holy and terrible forest. He proclaimed loudly his intention and performed penance highly illuminated. (14.)

---

Sanskrit
चर्तुं हि शक्यं तु सुरासुरैर्य-
न्न तादृशं वर्षसहस्रमात्रम् ।
सा पार्वती मन्दरपर्वतस्था
प्रतीक्ष्यमाणाऽऽगमनं भवस्य ॥ १५॥

English
Śiva performed penance for a thousand years the like of which could not be performed by the gods or Asuras. Pārvatī stayed behind in Mandara mountain awaiting for the return of the lord. (15.)

---

Sanskrit
पतिव्रता शीलगुणोपपन्ना
एकाकिनी नित्यमथो विभीता ।
गुहान्तरे दुःखपरा बभूव
संरक्षिता सा सुतवीरकेण ॥ १६॥

English
The chaste lady, endowed with good conduct remained alone in that cavern. She was terrified and distressed. Of course she was guarded by her son Vīraka. (16.)

---

Sanskrit
ततः स दैत्यो वरदानमत्तः
तैर्योधमुख्यैः सहितो गुहां ताम् ।
विभिन्नधैर्यः पुनराजगाम
शिलीमुखैर्मारसमुद्भवैश्च ॥ १७॥

English
Then the Daitya whose mental steadiness had been shattered by the arrows of Kāma, became bold and haughty due to the boons that had been granted to him. He came to the cavern accompanied by his soldiers. (17.)

---

Sanskrit
अत्यद्भुतं तत्र चकार युद्धं
हित्वा तदा भोजनपाननिद्राः ।
रात्रिं दिवं पञ्चशतानि पञ्च
क्रुद्धः ससैन्यैः सह वीरकेण ॥ १८॥

English
Forsaking food, drink and sleep, the infuriated Daitya accompanied by his army fought with Vīraka a very wonderful battle for five hundred, five days and nights. (18.)

---

Sanskrit
खड्गैः सकुन्तैः सह भिन्दिपालैः
गदाभुशुण्डीभिरथो प्रकाण्डैः ।
शिलीमुखैरर्द्धशशीभिरुग्रै-
र्वितस्तिभिः कूर्ममुखैर्ज्वलद्भिः ॥ १९॥

नाराचमुख्यैर्निशितैश्च शूलैः
परश्वधैस्तोमरमुद्गरैश्च ।
खड्गैर्गुडैः पर्वतपादपैश्च
दिव्यै रथास्त्रैरपि दैत्यसङ्घैः ॥ २०॥

नदीधितिर्भिन्नतनुः पपात
द्वारं गुहायाः पिहितं समस्तम् ।
तैरायुधैर्दैत्यभुजप्रयुक्तै-
र्गुहामुखे मूर्छित एव पश्चात् ॥ २१॥

English
Various weapons were used by the Daityas—Swords, javelins, slings, maces, sharp missiles, arrows with crescent-shaped tips, arrows with prolonged iron pikes, tortoise-shaped heads with blazing steel pointed hooks, sharp spear, axes, iron clubs of diverse sorts, iron balls, rocks, branches of trees and various divine missiles. Viraka was attacked with these weapons and he fainted at the entrance to the cavern. His body was pierced by the sharp weapons hurled by the Daityas. The various weapons barred the entrance to the cave. (19-21.)

Sanskrit
आच्छादितं वीरकमस्त्रजालै-
र्दैत्यैश्च सर्वैस्तु मुहूर्तमात्रम् ।
अपावृतं कर्तुमशक्यमासी-
न्निरीक्ष्य देवी दितिजान् सुघोरान् ॥ २२॥

English
Viraka was covered by the weapons and could not be extricated. The goddess Pārvatī was afraid at the sight. From within the cave she remembered Brahmā and Viṣṇu. (22-23.)

---

Sanskrit

English
Thus remembered by the goddess, Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Indra and others assumed female forms and came there.$^{303}$ (24.)

[fn 303. The Śakti or Energy of a god is represented as his female counterpart. Each energy is personified and functions individually. The Purāṇas depict the gods and their Energies fighting against the Asuras. In fact the god and his Energy are identical. There is no characteristic mark distinguishing the two.]

Sanskrit

English
Sages of great dignity, Siddhas, Nāgas and Guhyakas became women and entered the cavern where Pārvatī was staying. (25.)

English
Since it was not customary to enter the harem of kings they assumed female form of wonderful features and entered the cavern of Pārvatī for heroic activities. (26-27.)

Sanskrit

Meanwhile Viraka of wonderfully fierce valour regained his consciousness and stood up. He seized the weapons of the warriors and hit the Daityas with them. (29.)

Brāhmī stood opposed to the Daityas with the staff in her hand. Gaurī became very furious. Nārāyaṇī held conch, mace, sword, discus and bow in her hands. (30.)

तमागतं प्रेक्ष्य महेश्वरं च ।
चक्रुर्महायुद्धमतीवमात्रं
नार्यः प्रहृष्टाः सह वीरकेण ॥ ४२॥

English
On seeing lord Śiva arrived after the lapse of a thousand years, the delighted women in the company of Viraka fought a very great battle. (42.)

Sanskrit
प्रणम्य गौरी गिरिशं च मूर्ध्ना
सन्दर्शयन् भर्तुरतीव शौर्यमम् ।
गौरी प्रयुद्धं च चकार हृष्टा
हरस्ततः पर्वतराजपुत्रीम् ॥ ४३॥

कण्ठे गृहीत्वा तु गुहां प्रविष्टो
रमासहस्राणि विसर्जितानि ।
गौरी च सम्मानशतैः प्रपूज्य
गुहामुखे वीरकमेव स्थापयन् ॥ ४४॥

English
Pārvatī bowed her head to Śiva. She exhibited great valour to her lord. The delighted Pārvatī fought a terrific battle. Śiva embraced her and then entered the cavern. The numerous women that had gathered were dismissed. Pārvatī honoured Vīraka by hundreds of gifts and appointed him as the keeper of the gate. (43-44.)

Sanskrit
ततो न गौरीं गिरिशं च दृष्ट्वा-
सुरेश्वरो नीतिविचक्षणो हि ।
द्रुतं स्वदूतं विधसाख्यमेव
स प्रेषयामास शिवोपकण्ठम् ॥ ४५॥

English
Then the Asura chief, very clever in statesmanship, unable to see either Pārvatī or Śiva sent his emissary Vighasa immediately to Śiva. (45.)

Sanskrit
तैस्तैः प्रहारैरपि जर्जराङ्ग-
स्तस्मिन् रणे देवगणेरितैर्यः ।
जगाद वाक्यं तु सगर्वमुग्रं
प्रविश्य शम्भुं प्रणिपत्य मूर्ध्ना ॥ ४६॥

English
He was one whose limbs were shattered by the weapons hurled by the gods and the Gaṇas. He entered the cavern, bowed to Śiva and spoke these words haughtily. (46.)

Sanskrit
दूत उवाच ।
सम्प्रेषितोऽहं विविशे गुहां तु
ह्येषोऽन्धकस्त्वां समुवाच वाक्यम् ।
नार्या न कार्यं तव किञ्चिदस्ति-
विमुञ्च नारीं तरुणीं सुरूपाम् ॥ ४७॥

English
*The messenger said:—*  “I have been sent by him and so I have entered this cavern. You have nothing to do with a woman. Surrender this young and beautiful lady. (47.)

Sanskrit
प्रायो भवांस्तापसस्तज्जुषस्व
क्षान्तं मया यत्कमनीयमन्तः ।
मुनिर्विरोधव्य इति प्रचिन्त्य
न त्वं मुनिस्तापस किं तु शत्रुः ॥ ४८॥

English
Usually you are an ascetic. Carry on that. Thinking “Should a sage be offended?” I have observed forbearance within my tender mind. But O sage, you are not a real ascetic but only my enemy. (48.)

Sanskrit
अतीव दैत्येषु महाविरोधी
युध्यस्व वेगेन मया प्रमथ्य ।
नयामि पातालतलानुरूपं
यमक्षयं तापस धूर्त्त हि त्वाम् ॥ ४९॥

English
You are extremely inimical to the Daityas. Show your might in fighting with me. O wicked ascetic, I shall send you to Yama’s abode befitting the nether worlds.” (49.)

Sanskrit
सनत्कुमार उवाच ।
एतद्वचो दूतमुखान्निशम्य
कपालमाली तमुवाच कोपात् ।
ज्वलन्विषादेन महांस्त्रिनेत्रः
सतां गतिर्दुष्टमदप्रहर्ता ॥ ५०॥

English
*Sanatkumāra said:—* On hearing these words conveyed by the emissary, the great three-eyed lord, the goal of the good, the destroyer of the pride of the wicked, the wearer of skull-garlands spoke furiously burning with grief. (50.)

Sanskrit
शिव उवाच ।
व्यक्तं वचस्ते तदतीव चोग्रं
प्रोक्तं हि तत्त्वं त्वरितं प्रयाहि ।
कुरुष्व युद्धं हि मया प्रसह्य
यदि प्रशक्तोऽसि बलेन हि त्वम् ॥ ५१॥

English
*Śiva said:—* Manifestly your words are fierce. Hence hasten. Fight with me if you have the might. (51.)

Sanskrit
यः स्यादशक्तो भुवि तस्य कोऽर्थो
दारैर्धनैर्वा सुमनोहरैश्च ।
आयान्तु दैत्याश्च बलेन मत्ता
विचार्यमेवं तु कृतं मयैतत् ॥ ५२॥

English
Of what avail are the wives and riches, be they ever so beautiful, to a feeble man in the world? Let the haughty Daityas proud of their strength come. I have already thought of this and acted accordingly. (52.)

Sanskrit
शरीरयात्रापि कुतस्त्वशक्तेः
कुर्वन्तु यद्यद्विहितं तु तेषाम् ।
ममापि यद्यत्करणीयमस्ति

English
—How can a feeble man maintain even his physical body? Let them do whatever they are ordained to do. I shall also do whatever I have to do. There is no doubt in this. (53.)
