### **CHAPTER FORTYEIGHT - (Swallowing of Śukra)**

---

Sanskrit
४८

व्यास उवाच ।
शुक्रे निगीर्णे रुद्रेण किमकार्षुश्च दानवाः ।
अन्धकेशा महावीरा वद तत्त्वं महामुने ॥ १॥

English
*Vyāsa said:—*  O Great sage, when Bhārgava was swallowed by Rudra what did the heroic Dānavas headed by Andhaka, do? Please narrate. (1.)

---

Sanskrit
सनत्कुमार उवाच ।
काव्ये निगीर्णे गिरिजेश्वरेण
दैत्या जयाशारहिता बभूवुः ।
हस्तैर्विमुक्ता इव वारणेन्द्राः
श‍ऋङ्गैर्विहीना इव गोवृषाश्च ॥ २॥

English
*Sanatkumāra said:—* When Bhārgava was swallowed by the lord of Pārvatī, the Daityas gave up hopes of victory, like lordly elephants bereft of trunks or cows and bulls devoid of horns. (2.)

---

Sanskrit
शिरो विहीना इव देहसङ्घा
द्विजा यथा चाध्ययनेन हीनाः ।
निरुद्यमाः सत्त्वगणा यथा वै
यथोद्यमा भाग्यविवर्जिताश्च ॥ ३॥

English
They were as futile as headless trunks, as brahmins who have forsaken the study of the Vedas, as the living beings who have given up efforts and as the efforts of those who are not favoured by good fortune. (3.)

---

Sanskrit
पत्या विहीनाश्च यथैव योषा
यथा विपक्षाः खलु पक्षिणौघाः ।
आयूंषि हीनानि यथैव पुण्यै-
र्व्रतैर्विहीनानि यथा श्रुतानि ॥ ४॥

English
They were lustreless and ineffectual like women devoid of husbands, like the arrows devoid of feather, like longevity devoid of meritorious actions and like learning in the Vedas without the observance of sacred rites. (4.)

---

English
They were as powerless as innumerable activities rendered fruitless without the support of wealth, or as Kṣatriyas devoid of heroism or as the assemblage of virtue without truth. (5.)

---

Sanskrit
नन्दिना च हृते शुक्रे गिलिते च विषादिना ।
विषादमगमन्दैत्या यतमानरणोत्सवाः ॥ ६॥

English
When Bhārgava was taken away by Nandin and swallowed by Śiva as he had swallowed poison, the Daityas became grief-stricken and their pride and jubilation for battle became curbed. (6.)

---

Sanskrit
तान् वीक्ष्य विगतोत्साहानन्धकः प्रत्यभाषत ।
दैत्यांस्तु हुण्डहुण्डादीन्महाधीरपराक्रमः ॥ ७॥

English
On seeing Tuhuṇḍa, Huṇḍa and other Daityas, devoid of enthusiasm, the courageous and valorous Andhaka replied. (7.)

---

Sanskrit
अन्धक उवाच ।
कविं विक्रम्य नयता नन्दिना वञ्चिता वयम् ।
तनूर्विना कृताः प्राणाः सर्वेषामद्य नो ननु ॥ ८॥

English
Andhaka said :—  By seizing Bhārgava forcibly from our midst we have been duped by Nandin. Our bodies have been rendered lifeless. (8.)

---

Sanskrit
धैर्यं वीर्यं गतिः कीर्तिः सत्त्वं तेजः पराक्रमः ।
युगपन्नो हृतं सर्वमेकस्मिन् भार्गवे हृते ॥ ९॥

English
With the taking away of Bhārgava from us, our courage, valour, achievement, fame, strength, splendour and exploit have been simultaneously taken away. (9.)

---

Sanskrit
धिगस्मान् कुलपूज्यो यैरेकोऽपि कुलसत्तमः ।
गुरुः सर्वसमर्थश्च त्राता त्रातो न चापदि ॥ १०॥

English
Fie upon us by whom the sole and excellent preceptor of the family worthy of the respect of the whole race, the virtual protector of all of us and very efficient, has not been saved at the time of adversity. (10.)

---

Sanskrit
तद्यूयमविलम्ब्येह युध्यध्वमरिभिः सह ।
वीरैस्तैः प्रमथैर्वीराः स्मृत्वा गुरुपदाम्बुजम् ॥ ११॥

English
Hence do not waste time. Fight with the enemy, the heroic Pramathas, after remembering the lotus-like feet of the preceptor. (11.)

---

Sanskrit
गुरोः काव्यस्य सुखदौ स्मृत्वा चरणपङ्कजौ ।
सूदयिष्याम्यहं सर्वान् प्रमथान् सह नन्दिना ॥ १२॥

English
After remembering the beneficent lotus-like feet of the preceptor, I shall slay all the Pramathas along with Nandin. (12.)

---

Sanskrit
अद्यैतान् विवशान् हत्वा सहदेवैः सवासवैः ।
भार्गवं मोचयिष्यामि जीवं योगीव कर्मतः ॥ १३॥

English
After killing these along with the gods including Indra I shall obtain the release of Bhārgava like the Yogin who releases the soul from the bondage of action. (13.)

---

Sanskrit
स चापि योगी योगेन यदि नाम स्वयं प्रभुः ।
शरीरात्तस्य निर्गच्छेदस्माकं शेषपालकः ॥ १४॥

English
Bhārgava too is a lordly Yogin. If he himself comes out of Śiva’s body, the rest of us are saved. (14.)

---

Sanskrit
सनत्कुमार उवाच ।
इत्यन्धकवचः श्रुत्वा दानवा मेघनिस्स्वनाः ।
प्रमथान् निर्दयाः प्राहुर्मर्तव्ये कृतनिश्चयाः ॥ १५॥

English
*Sanatkumāra said:—* On hearing the words of Andhaka, the powerful Dānavas, fearlessly roaring like the rumbling clouds, spoke after resolving to do what was then to be thought of. (15.)

---

Sanskrit
सत्यायुषि न नो जातु शक्ताः स्युः प्रमथा बलात् ।
असत्यायुषि किं गत्वा त्यक्त्वा स्वामिनमाहवे ॥ १६॥

English
*The Dānavas said:—*  If we are destined to live, the Pramathas cannot overwhelm us. If it is otherwise what avails running from the battle ground leaving our master behind. (16.)

---

Sanskrit
ये स्वामिनं विहायातो बहुमानधना जनाः ।
यान्ति ते यान्ति नियतमन्धतामिस्रमालयम् ॥ १७॥

English
Those who leave their masters and run away professing to be honoured and desiring to be rich will surely fall into hell Andhatāmisra. (17.)

---

Sanskrit
अयशस्तमसा ख्यातिं मलिनीकृत्य भूरिशः ।
इहामुत्रापि सुखिनो न स्युर्भग्ना रणाजिरे ॥ १८॥

English
After sullying their fame with the darkness of ignominy they will forfeit their pleasure both here and hereafter. They will be slain in the battle. (18.)

---

Sanskrit
किं दानैः किं तपोभिश्च किं तीर्थपरिमज्जनैः ।
धरातीर्थे यदि स्नानं पुनर्भवमलापहे ॥ १९॥

English
Of what avail are charitable gifts, austerities and dips in holy waters if one takes bath in the holy tract of the battlefield that removes the dirt of re-birth? (19.)

---

Sanskrit
सम्प्रधार्येति तद्वाक्यं दैत्यास्ते दनुजास्तथा ।
ममन्थुः प्रमथानाजौ रणभेरीं निनाद्य च ॥ २०॥

English
*Sanatkumārā said:—*  After saying these words and deciding in accordance with them, those Daityas and Dānavas pounded the Pramathas in the battle. They sounded the war-drums. (20.)

---

Sanskrit
तत्र बाणासिवज्रौघैः कठिनैश्च शिलामयैः ।
भुशुण्डिभिन्दिपालैश्च शक्ति भल्लपरश्वधैः ॥ २१॥

खट्वाङ्गैः पट्टिशैः शूलैर्लकुटैर्मुसलैरलम् ।
परस्परमभिघ्नन्तः प्रचक्रुः कदनं महत् ॥ २२॥

English
By means of arrows, swords, thunderbolts, hard rocks, Bhuśuṇḍis, Bhindipālas and other missiles, spears, javelins, axes, skull-headed clubs, tridents, maces, staffs and other weapons they bit one another and wrought a great havoc. (21-22.)

---

Sanskrit
कार्मुकाणां विकृष्टानां पततां च पतत्त्रिणाम् ।
भिन्दिपालभुशुण्डीनां क्ष्वेडितानां रवोऽभवत् ॥ २३॥

English
There was a great noise everywhere, produced by the drawn bows, flying arrows, the missiles, Bhuśuṇḍis, Bhindipālas and leonine roars. (23.)

---

Sanskrit
रणतूर्य्यनिनादैश्च गजानां बहुबृंहितैः ।
ह्रेषारवैर्हयानांश्च महान्कोलाहलोऽभवत् ॥ २४॥

English
There was great tumult caused by the war drums, trumpets, the trumpeting sounds of elephants and the neighing sounds of the horses. (24.)

---

Sanskrit
अतिस्वनैरवापूरि द्यावाभूम्योर्यदन्तरम् ।
अभीरूणां च भीरूणां महारोमोद्गमोऽभवत् ॥ २५॥

English
The vast space between heaven and earth was filled with loud reports causing horripilations to the courageous as well as the cowardly. (25.)

---

Sanskrit
गजवाजिमहारावस्फुटशब्दग्रहाणि च ।
भग्नध्वजपताकानि क्षीणप्रहरणानि च ॥ २६॥

रुधिरोद्गारचित्राणि व्यश्वहस्तिरथानि च ।
पिपासितानि सैन्यानि मुमूर्च्छुरुभयत्र वै ॥ २७॥

English
The thirsty armies on either side fainted with their ears bursting by the loud sounds of elephants and horses, with the flagstaff and banners broken and torn, with their weapons exhausted, vomiting diverse kinds of blood and bereft of horses, elephants and chariots. (26-27.)

---

Sanskrit

बलेन जघ्नुरसुरान्सर्वान्प्रापुर्जयं मुने ॥ २८॥

English
O sage, the heroic Nandin and other Pramathas slew all Asuras and won the victory. (28.)

---

Sanskrit

दुद्राव रथमास्थाय स्वयमेवान्धको गणान् ॥ २९॥

English
On seeing his army being shattered here and there Andhaka rushed at the Gaṇas driving in his chariot. (29.)

---

Sanskrit

English
Like the mountains hit with the thunderbolt by Indra, the Pramathas perished. Like the clouds devoid of water they sank low. (30.)

---

Sanskrit

English
Glancing at the in-coming or out-going Pramathas, far off or at close quarters, Andhaka hit them severally with as many arrows as the hair on their bodies. (31.)

---

Sanskrit

English
On seeing the army shattered and smashed by the powerful Andhaka, Skanda, Vināyaka, Nandin, Somanandin and other heroic and powerful Pramathas, and Śiva's personal Gaṇas became furious and fought in diverse ways and very valorously. (32-33.)

---

Sanskrit

वीरेण नैगमेयेन वैशाखेन बलीयसा ॥ ३४॥

English
By Vināyaka, Skanda, Nandin, Somanandin, Vīraka, Naigameya, the powerful Vaiśākha and other terrible Gaṇas, Andhaka was rendered blind as they showered tridents, spears and arrows incessantly. (34-35.)

Sanskrit

तेन शब्देन महता शुक्रः शम्भूदरे स्थितः ॥ ३६॥

Then a great tumult arose in the midst of the armies of Pramathas and Asuras. At that great noise, Bhārgava who was within the belly of Śiva began to wander seeking an outlet like the unaboded wind. In Śiva's body he observed seven worlds$^{309}$ including Pātāla. (36-37.)

[fn 309. The seven regions below the earth are called “atala, vitala, sutala, rasātala, tala, talātala” and “pātāla”. Cp. Umā S. ch. 15 V. 22-23.]

English
Wandering round and round in the belly of Śiva for a hundred years he failed to see any outlet as a wicked person fails to see a vulnerable point in a good person.

40-41 Taking recourse to the Yoga of Śiva he repeated the following mantra and assumed the form of Śiva's semen. He thus emerged out of the belly of Śiva through his penis. Thereafter he bowed to Śiva and was accepted as a son by Pārvatī. He was made a lord of the Gaṇas. (39.)
