### **CHAPTER FIFTYEIGHT - *(Dundubhi Nirhrāda is slain)***

---

Sanskrit
५८

सनत्कुमार उवाच ।
श‍ृणु व्यास प्रवक्ष्यामि चरितं शशिमौलिनः ।
यथा दुन्दुभिनिर्ह्रादमवधीद्दितिजं हरः ॥ १॥

English
*Sanatkumāra said:—* O Vyāsa, listen. I shall narrate the story of the moon-crested lord Śiva how he slew the Daitya Dundubhi-nirhrāda. (1.)

---

Sanskrit
हिरण्याक्षे हते दैत्ये दितिपुत्रे महाबले ।
विष्णुदेवेन कालेन प्राप दुखं महद्दितिः ॥ २॥

English
When the Daitya Hiranyākṣa, son of Diti,³⁴¹ of great strength was killed by Viṣṇu, Diti remained griefstricken for a long time. (2.)

[fn ³⁴¹. Diti was the daughter of Dakṣa and wife of Kaśyapa. Her sons obtained the designation 'Daitya' after her name.]


---

Sanskrit
दैत्यो दुन्दुभिनिर्ह्रादो दुष्टः प्रह्लादमातुलः ।
सान्त्वयामास तां वाग्भिर्दुःखितां देवदुःखदः ॥ ३॥

English
The wicked Daitya named Dundubhinirhrāda, the uncle of Prahlāda, the oppressor of the gods, consoled the dejected mother with the words. (3.)

---

Sanskrit
अथ दैत्यः स मायावी दितिमाश्वास्य दैत्यराट् ।
देवाः कथं सुजेयाः स्युरित्युपायमचिन्तयत् ॥ ४॥

English
After consoling Diti, the king of Daityas, an expert in using Māyā began to think of the ways and means of conquering the gods easily. (4.)

---

Sanskrit
देवैश्च घातितो वीरो हिरण्याक्षो महासुरः ।
विष्णुना च सह भ्रात्रा सच्छलैर्दैत्यवैरिभिः ॥ ५॥

English
"The great Asura Hiranyākṣa along with his brother has been killed through Viṣṇu by the gods, the enemies of Daityas, employing deceitful means. (5.)

---

Sanskrit
किं बलाश्च किमाहारा किमाधारा हि निर्जराः ।
मया कथं सुजेयाः स्युरित्युपायमचिन्तयत् ॥ ६॥

English
What is the strength of the gods? What is their diet? What is their support? How can the gods be easily vanquished by me?" Thinking like this he tried to find out the ways and means. (6.)

---

Sanskrit
विचार्य बहुशो दैत्यस्तत्त्वं विज्ञाय निश्चितम् ।
अवश्यमग्रजन्मानो हेतवोऽत्र विचारतः ॥ ७॥

English
Thinking deeply in diverse ways the Daitya came to the conclusion that the brāhmaṇas were the cause of the trouble.³⁴² (7.)

[fn ³⁴². The anti-Brāhmaṇa activities of the Daityas mentioned here and elsewhere in the Purāṇas were due to the fact that the Brāhmaṇas performed sacrifices wherein offerings were made to the gods. But this tradition of Brāhmaṇa—Daitya animosity is of late origin. Originally Daityas were devoted to Brāhmaṇas. The Bhārgavas were purohitas to Hiranyakasipu. Vasiṣṭha was his hotṛ. Vṛtra and Namuci, the two famous Dānavas were Brāhmaṇas themselves. For details see AIHT. Ch XXVI.]
---

Sanskrit
ब्राह्मणान्हन्तुमसकृदन्वधावत वै ततः ।
दैत्यो दुन्दुभिनिर्ह्रादो देववैरी महाखलः ॥ ८॥

English
The Daitya Dundubhinirhrāda, the most wicked enemy of the gods, ran after the brahmins to kill them. (8.)

---

Sanskrit
यतः क्रतुभुजो देवाः क्रतवो वेदसम्भवाः ।
ते वेदा ब्राह्मणाधारास्ततो देवबलं द्विजाः ॥ ९॥

English
Since the gods maintain themselves on sacrifices, sacrifices are born of the Vedas and the Vedas are the custody of the brāhmaṇas, so the brāhmaṇas constitute the strength of the gods. (9.)

---

Sanskrit
निश्चितं ब्राह्मणाधाराः सर्वे वेदाः सवासवाः ।
गीर्वाणा ब्राह्मणबला नात्र कार्या विचारणा ॥ १०॥

English
Certainly the gods including Indra are supported by the brāhmaṇas. The gods gain their strength from the brāhmaṇas. There is no doubt about this. (10.)

---

Sanskrit
ब्राह्मणा यदि नष्टाः स्युर्वेदा नष्टास्ततः स्वयम् ।
अतस्तेषु प्रणष्टेषु विनष्टाः सततं सुराः ॥ ११॥

English
If the brāhmaṇas are destroyed the Vedas will perish. If they are destroyed the gods will also perish. (11.)

---

Sanskrit
यज्ञेषु नाशं गच्छत्सु हताहारास्ततः सुराः ।
निर्बलाःसुखजय्याः स्युर्निर्जितेषु सुरेष्वथ ॥ १२॥

अहमेव भविष्यामि मान्यस्त्रिजगतीपतिः ।
आहरिष्यामि देवानामक्षयाः सर्वसम्पदः ॥ १३॥

English
If the sacrifices are destroyed, the gods will lose their food. They will grow weaker and be easily conquered. When the gods are conquered I shall become the sole honourable lord of the three worlds. I shall then confiscate the everlasting riches of the gods. (12-13.)

---

Sanskrit
निर्वेक्ष्यामि सुखान्येव राज्ये निहतकण्टके ।
इति निश्चित्य दुर्बुद्धिः पुनश्चिन्तितवान्खलः ॥ १४॥

English
I shall enjoy happiness in my kingdom freed of thorns.” After thinking like this the wicked Daitya thought again. (14.)

---

Sanskrit
द्विजाः क्व सन्ति भूयांसो ब्रह्मतेजोऽतिबृंहिता ।
श्रुत्यध्ययनसम्पन्नास्तपोबलसमन्विताः ॥ १५॥

English
“Where are these brāhmaṇas in plenty—the brāhmaṇas strengthened by the splendour of the Brahman, well versed in the study of the Vedas and possessing the strength of penance? (15.)

---

Sanskrit
भूयसां ब्राह्मणानां तु स्थानं वाराणसी खलु ।
तामादावुपसंहृत्य यायां तीर्थान्तरं ततः ॥ १६॥

English
It is Vārāṇasī indeed that is the place of many brāhmaṇas. I shall finish that first and then go to other holy centres. (16.)

---

Sanskrit
यत्र यत्र हि तीर्थेषु यत्र यत्राश्रमेषु च ।
सन्ति सर्वेऽग्रजन्मानस्ते मयाद्याः समन्ततः ॥ १७॥

English
In holy centres or hermitages wherever these brāhmaṇas live they shall be devoured by me.” (17.)

---

Sanskrit
इति दुन्दुभिनिर्ह्रादो मतिं कृत्वा कुलोचिताम् ।
प्राप्यापि काशीं दुर्वृत्तो मायावी न्यवधीद् द्विजान् ॥ १८॥

English
After thinking thus in accordance with the nature of his race, Dundubhinirhrāda went to Kāśī and he, the wicked wielder of Māyās, killed the brāhmaṇas. (18.)

---

Sanskrit
समित्कुशान्समादातुं यत्र यान्ति द्विजोत्तमाः ।
अरण्ये तत्र तान्सर्वान्स भक्षयति दुर्मतिः ॥ १९॥

English
When the excellent brāhmaṇas went to the forest to fetch sacrificial twigs and the Darbha grass, the wicked Dānava used to eat them there. (19.)

---

Sanskrit
यथा कोऽपि न वेत्त्येवं तथाऽऽच्छन्नोऽभवत्पुनः ।
वने वनेचरो भूत्वा यादोरूपो जलाशये ॥ २०॥

English
After that he used to lie hidden so that nobody could detect him. In the forest he used to roam about like a forest-dweller and in the waters he used to take the form of an aquatic animal. (20.)

---

Sanskrit
अदृश्यरूपी मायावी देवानामप्यगोचरः ।
दिवा ध्यानपरस्तिष्ठेन्मुनिवन्मुनिमध्यगः ॥ २१॥

प्रवेशमुटजानां च निर्गमं हि विलोकयन् ।
यामिन्यां व्याघ्ररूपेणाभक्षयद् ब्राह्मणान् बहून् ॥ २२॥

English
He was invisible in form. He wielded the art of deception. He could not be seen even by the gods. During the day he stood in the midst of sages engaged in meditation but observing the ingress and egress of persons in the hut. But at night he took the form of a tiger and ate many of them. (21-22.)

---

Sanskrit
निःशङ्कं भक्षयत्येवं न त्यजत्यपि कीकशम् ।
इत्थं निपातितास्तेन विप्रा दुष्टेन भूरिशः ॥ २३॥

English
He used to eat unhesitatingly never leaving even a bone behind. Thus many brāhmaṇas were annihilated by him in this way. (23.)

---

Sanskrit

सपर्यां देवदेवस्य कृत्वा ध्यानस्थितोऽभवत् ॥ २४॥

English
Once on the Śivarātri$^{343}$ day a certain devotee performed the worship of Śiva, the lord of the gods and was engaged in meditation in his own hut. (24.)

[fn 343. Śivarātri or Śiva-caturdaśī is the fourteenth of the dark half of Māgha (January-February) on which a rigorous fast is observed during the day and night.]
---

Sanskrit

English
The lord of Daityas Dundubhinirhrāda, proud of his strength, assumed the form of a tiger and wanted to seize him. (25.)

---

Sanskrit

English
As the devotee was in meditation with a mind concentrated on Śiva and as he had fixed the Astramantra, the Daitya could not attack him. (26.)

---

Sanskrit

English
Śiva, the omnipresent lord, knew his evil intention and decided to slay the Daitya. (27.)

---

Sanskrit

English
While the Daitya in the form of the tiger was about to snatch the devotee, Śiva appeared before him. The three-eyed lord Śiva is very keen in intellect in saving the devotees, nay in protecting the universe. (28.)

---

Sanskrit

English
On seeing Śiva coming out of the phallic image worshipped by the devotee, the Daitya in the form of a tiger increased in size like a big mountain. (29.)

---

Sanskrit

English
The Dānava glanced with a contemptuous look at Śiva but the lord caught him and pressed him under his armpit. (30.)

---

Sanskrit

English
The five-faced lord favourably disposed to his devotees hit the tiger on its head with his fist harder than thunderbolt. (31.)

Sanskrit

English
By the blow of the fist and the pressure at the armpit the tiger groaned aloud in great distress filling heaven and earth with the sound and died. (32.)

English

इति श्रीशिवमहापुराणे द्वितीयायां रुद्रसंहितायां पञ्चम युद्धखण्डे दुन्दुभिनिर्ह्राददैत्यवधवर्णनं नामाष्टपञ्चाशत्तमोऽध्यायः ॥ २.५.५८॥

॥ श्रीगणेशाय नमः ॥ 
॥ श्रीगौरीशङ्कराभ्यां नमः ॥ 
॥ श्रीशिवमहापुराणम् ॥ 
द्वितीयायां रुद्रसंहितायां पञ्चमो युद्धखण्डे
