### **CHAPTER FIFTYNINE - (*Vidala and Utpala are slain*)**

---

Sanskrit
५९

सनत्कुमार उवाच ।
श‍ृणु व्यास सुसम्प्रीत्या चरितं परमेशितुः ।
यथावधीत्स्वप्रियया दैत्यमुद्दिश्य संज्ञया ॥ १॥

English
*Sanatkumāra said:—* O Vyāsa, listen with pleasure to the story of the great lord how he killed through his beloved a Daitya whom he indicated by a sign. (1.)

---

Sanskrit
आस्तां पुरा महादैत्यौ विदलोत्पलसंज्ञकौ ।
अपुंवध्यौ महावीरौ सुदृप्तौ वरतो विधेः ॥ २॥

English
Formerly there were two great Daityas—Vidala and Utpala. They were great heroes, puffed up by the boon from Brahmā that they could not be slain by a man. (2.)

---

Sanskrit
तृणीकृतत्रिजगती पुरुषाभ्यां स्वदोर्बलात् ।
ताभ्यां सर्वे सुरा ब्रह्मन् दैत्याभ्यां निर्जिता रणे ॥ ३॥

English
O Brahmin, the gods had been defeated in the battle by the two Daityas who by the strength of their arms considered the people of the three worlds as insignificant as the blade of grass. (3.)

---

Sanskrit
ताभ्यां पराजिता देवा विधेस्ते शरणं गताः ।
नत्वा तं विधिवत्सर्वे कथयामासुरादरात् ॥ ४॥

English
Defeated by them, the gods sought refuge in Brahmā. After bowing to him duly they submitted to him respectfully. (4.)

---

Sanskrit
इति ब्रह्मा ह्यवोचत्तान् देव्या वध्यौ च तौ ध्रुवम् ।
धैर्यं कुरुत संस्मृत्य सशिवां शिवमादरात् ॥ ५॥

भक्तवत्सलनामासौ सशिवाशङ्करः शिवः ।
शं करिष्यत्यदीर्घेण कालेन परमेश्वरः ॥ ६॥

English
On hearing their account Brahmā said, "They will surely be slain by the goddess. Be bold. Remember Śiva and Pārvatī respectfully. Śiva is auspicious, benevolent favourably disposed to his devotees. The supreme god will bring about welfare ere long." (5-6.)

---

Sanskrit
सनत्कुमार उवाच
इत्युक्त्वा तांस्ततो ब्रह्मा तूष्णीमासीच्छिवं स्म्रन् ।
तेऽपि देवा मुदं प्राप्य स्वं स्वं धाम ययुस्तदा ॥ ७॥

English
*Sanatkumāra said:—* After saying this, Brahmā kept quiet remembering Śiva. The gods too returned to their respective abodes rejoicingly. (7.)

---

Sanskrit
अथ नारददेवर्षिः शिवप्रेरणया तदा ।
गत्वा तदीयभवनं शिवासौन्दर्यमुज्जगौ ॥ ८॥

English
Then at the behest of Śiva, the celestial sage Nārada went to the abode of the Daityas and sang the glory of the beauty of Pārvatī. (8.)

---

Sanskrit
श्रुत्वा तद्वचनं दैत्यावास्तां मायाविमोहितौ ।
देवीं परिजिहीर्षू तौ विषमेषुप्रपीडितौ ॥ ९॥

English
On hearing his words the two Daityas were deluded by deception. Afflicted by the god of lust they desired to abduct the goddess. (9.)

---

Sanskrit
विचारयामासतुस्तौ कदा कुत्र शिवा च सा ।
भविष्यति विधेः प्राप्तोदयान्नाविति ॥ १०॥

English
They thought to themselves where and when they would obtain Pārvatī at the rise of their good fortune. (10.)

---

Sanskrit
एकस्मिन्समये शम्भुर्विजहार सुलीलया ।
कौतुकेनैव चिक्रीडे शिवा कन्दुकलीलया ॥ ११॥

सखीभिः सह सुप्रीत्या कौतुकाच्छिवसन्निधौ ॥ १२॥

English
Once Śiva was engaged in sports. Pārvatī too was playing with a ball along with her friends in the presence of Śiva. (11-12.)

---

Sanskrit
उदञ्चन्त्यञ्चदङ्गानां लाघवं परितन्वती ।
निश्वासामोदमुदितभ्रमराकुलितेक्षणा ॥ १३॥

English
At times she looked up. At times she displayed the lightness of limbs. At times when she took deep breaths, bees hovered round her enticed by the fragrance. At times the bees made her eyes agitated. (13.)

---

Sanskrit
भ्रश्यद्धम्मिल्लसन्माल्यस्वपुराकृतभूमिका ।
स्विद्यत्कपोलपत्रालीस्रवदम्बुकणोज्ज्वला ॥ १४॥

English
Flowers from her tresses fell on the ground in front of her. Her cheeks were perspiring. Drops of sweat dripping from the paintings on her cheek brightened up. (14.)

---

Sanskrit
स्फुटच्चोलांशुकपथतिर्यदङ्गप्रभावृता ।
उल्लसत्कन्दुकास्फालातिश्रोणितकराम्बुजा ॥ १५॥

English
The lustre of her body spread all round through the partings of her gown. By exerting her too much in beating the ball her red hand became redder than the red lotus. (15.)

---

Sanskrit
कन्दुकानुगसद्दृष्टिनर्तितभ्रूलताञ्चला ।
मृडानी किल खेलन्ती ददृशे जगदम्बिका ॥ १६॥

अन्तरिक्षचराभ्यां च दितिजाभ्यां कटाक्षिता ।
क्रोडीकृताभ्यामिव वै समुपस्थितमृत्युना ॥ १७॥

English
When the ball bounced, her eyes too followed it making the brows to dance thereby. As the goddess mother of the universe was playing, she was seen by the Daityas who were going by the aerial path. They were, as it were, held in the lap by the imminent death. (16-17.)

---

Sanskrit
विदलोत्पलसंज्ञाभ्यां दृप्ताभ्यां वरतो विधेः ।
तृणीकृतत्रिजगतीपुरुषाभ्यां स्वदोर्बलात् ॥ १८॥

English
They were the Daityas Vidala and Uptala who had become haughty by the boon of Brahmā and by the might of their arms thought the people of the three worlds as insignificant as the blades of grass.$^{344}$ (18.)

[fn 344. This verse is the repetition of fragments of verses 2 and 3 of this chapter.]
---

Sanskrit
देवीं तां सञ्जिहीर्षन्तौ विषमेषुप्रपीडितौ ।
दिव उत्तेरतुः क्षिप्रं मायां स्वीकृत्य शाम्बरीम् ॥ १९॥

English
Desirous of abducting the goddess as they were tormented by the god of lust, they descended from the sky quickly after adapting the Śāmbari magic skill. (19.)

---

Sanskrit
धृत्वा पारिषदीं मायामायातामम्बिकान्तिकम् ।
तावत्यन्तं सुदुर्वृत्तावतिचञ्चलमानसौ ॥ २०॥

English
The two wicked ones of fickle mind approached Pārvatī in the guise of Śiva's attendants. (20.)

---

Sanskrit
अथ दुष्टनिहन्त्रा वै सावज्ञेन हरेण तौ ।
विज्ञातौ च क्षणादास्तां चाञ्चल्याल्लोचनोद्भवात् ॥ २१॥

English
By the excessive tremulousness of their eyes they were in a trice recognized by Śiva, the chastiser of and contemptuous towards the wicked. (21.)

---

Sanskrit
कटाक्षिताथ देवेन दुर्गा दुर्गतिघातिनी ।
दैत्याविमाविति गणौ नेति सर्वस्वरूपिणा ॥ २२॥

English
The lord shot a significant glance at Pārvatī the destroyer of miseries denoting that they were Daityas and not Gaṇas. They could assume any form. (22.)

---

Sanskrit
अथ सा नेत्रसंज्ञां स्वस्वामिनस्तां बुबोध ह ।
महाकौतुकिनस्तात शङ्करस्य परेशितुः ॥ २३॥

English
O dear, she understood the sign of the eyes of her lord Śiva, the great lord who indulges in fancies. (23.)

---

Sanskrit
ततो विज्ञाय संज्ञां तां सर्वज्ञार्द्धशरीरिणी ।
तेनैव कन्दुकेनाथ युगपन्निर्जघान तौ ॥ २४॥

English
Realising the significant glance, the goddess, the sharer of half the body of Śiva$^{345}$ hit both of them simultaneously with the ball. (24.)

[fn 345. In the Ardhanāriśvara form of Śiva, Pārvatī occupies one half and Śiva the other half. The form suggests the divine origin of men and women.]
---

Sanskrit
महाबलौ महादेव्या कन्दुकेन समाहतौ ।
परिभ्रम्य परिभ्रम्य तौ दुष्टौ विनिपेततुः ॥ २५॥

English
The powerful wicked Daityas hit by the ball whirled and whirled and fell on the ground. (25.)

---

Sanskrit
वृन्तादिव फले पक्वे तालेनानिललोलिते ।
दम्भोलिना परिहते श‍ृङ्गे इव महागिरेः ॥ २६॥

तौ निपात्य महादैत्यावकार्यकरणोद्यतौ ।
ततः परिणतिं यातो लिङ्गरूपेण कन्दुकः ॥ २७॥

English
After making the two Daityas fall like two ripe fruits from the palmyra tree when shaken by the wind, or like the two peaks of a great mountain struck by the thunderbolt, as they had attempted to do an evil action, the ball changed itself into the phallic image. (26-27.)

---

Sanskrit
कन्दुकेश्वरसंज्ञं च तल्लिङ्गमभवत्तदा ।
ज्येष्ठेश्वरसमीपे तु सर्वदुष्टनिवारणम् ॥ २८॥

English
That phallic image came to be known as Kandukeśvara. It is very near Jyeṣṭheśvara. It removes all the wicked things. (28.)

---

Sanskrit
एतस्मिन्नेव समये हरिब्रह्मादयः सुराः ।
शिवाविर्भावमाज्ञाय ऋषयश्च समाययुः ॥ २९॥

English
At the same time, knowing the manifestation of Śiva, Viṣṇu, Brahmā, other gods and the sages came there. (29.)

---

Sanskrit
अथ सर्वे सुराः शम्भोर्वरान्प्राप्य तदाज्ञया ।
स्वधामानि ययुः प्रीतास्तथा काशीनिवासिनः ॥ ३०॥

English
Then all the gods received boons from Śiva and at his bidding returned to their respective abodes delightedly. So were the residents of Kāśī blessed with the boons. (30.)

---

Sanskrit
साम्बिकं शङ्करं दृष्ट्वा कृताञ्जलिपुटाश्च ते ।
प्रणम्य तुष्टुवुर्भक्त्या वाग्भिरिष्टाभिरादरात् ॥ ३१॥

English
On seeing Śiva with Pārvatī they bowed to him with palms joined in reverence and eulogised him with devotion and pleasing words. (31.)

---

Sanskrit
साम्बिकोऽपि शिवो व्यास क्रीडित्वा सुविहारवित् ।
जगाम स्वालयं प्रीतः सगणो भक्तवत्सलः ॥ ३२॥

English
O Vyāsa, Śiva and Pārvatī too, went delightedly to their abode. The lord favourably disposed to his devotees, an expert in divine sports, had already had his game. (32.)

---

Sanskrit
कन्दुकेश्वरलिङ्गं च काश्यां दुष्टनिबर्हणम् ।
भुक्तिमुक्तिप्रदं सर्वकामदं सर्वदा सताम् ॥ ३३॥

English
The Kandukeśvara phallic image at Kāśī destroys the wicked, yields worldly pleasures and salvation. Upon the good it bestows desires always. (33.)

---

Sanskrit
इदमाख्यानमतुलं श‍ृणुयाद्यो मुदान्वितः ।
श्रावयेद्वा पठेद्यश्च तस्य दुःखभयं कुतः ॥ ३४॥

English
Where is the cause of fear to him who hears this incomparable narrative with joy, narrates or reads it? (34.)

---

Sanskrit
इह सर्वसुखं भुक्त्वा नानाविधमनुत्तमम् ।
परत्र लभते दिव्यां गतिं वै देवदुर्लभाम् ॥ ३५॥

English
He enjoys pleasures of various kinds and of excellent nature. Hereafter he attains the divine goal inaccessible even to the gods. (35.)

---

Sanskrit
इति ते वर्णितं तात चरितं परमाद्भुतम् ।
शिवयोर्भक्तवात्सल्यसुचकं शिवदं सताम् ॥ ३६॥

English
O dear, I have thus narrated to you the wonderful story of Śiva and Pārvatī. It indicates their favouritism to the devotees. It bestows welfare to the good. (36.)

---

Sanskrit
ब्रह्मोवाच
इत्युक्त्वामन्त्र्य तं व्यासं तन्नुतो मद्वरात्मजः ।
ययौ विहायसा काशीं चरितं शशिमौलिनः ॥ ३७॥

English
*Brahmā said:—* After narrating the story of the moon-crested lord, my excellent son, Sanatkumāra took leave of Vyāsa. Duly honoured by him he then went to Kāśī by the aerial path. (37.)

---

Sanskrit
युद्धखण्डमिदं प्रोक्तं मया ते मुनिसत्तम ।
रौद्रीयसंहितामध्ये सर्वकामफलप्रदम् ॥ ३८॥

English
Thus the section called “Yuddha” has been narrated to you, O excellent sage. In the Compendium called Rudra, it bestows all cherished desires. (38.)

---

Sanskrit
इयं हि संहिता रौद्री सम्पूर्णा वर्णिता मया ।
सदाशिवप्रियतरा भुक्तिमुक्तिफलप्रदा ॥ ३९॥

English
Thus the whole of Rudrasaṃhitā has been explained by me. It is pleasing to Śiva always. It yields enjoyment here and liberation hereafter. (39.)

---

Sanskrit
इमां यश्च पठेन्नित्यं शत्रुबाधानिवारिकाम् ।
सर्वान्कामानवाप्नोति ततो मुक्तिं लभेत ना ॥ ४०॥

English
The man who reads this Saṃhitā that wards off harassment from enemies shall attain all desires. Thereafter he shall attain liberation. (40.)

---

Sanskrit
सूत उवाच
इति ब्रह्मसुतः श्रुत्वा पित्रा शिवयशः परम् ।
शतनामाप्य शम्भोश्च कृतार्थोऽभूच्छिवानुगः ॥ ४१॥

English
*Sūta said:—* Thus Brahmā’s son Nārada heard from his father the great glory of Śiva. Śatanāmā too was satisfied and became a follower of Śiva. (41.)

---

Sanskrit
ब्रह्मनारदसंवादः सम्पूर्णः कथितो मया ।
शिवः सर्वप्रधानो हिकिं भूयः श्रोतुमिच्छिसि ॥ ४२॥

English
I have completely narrated the conversation between Brahmā and Nārada. Śiva is the most important of all deities. What else do you wish to hear about him? (42.)
