### **CHAPTER TWENTYSEVEN - (The incarnation of Dvijeśvara)⁷¹**

---

Sanskrit
नन्दीश्वर उवाच ।
श‍ृणु तात प्रवक्ष्यामि शिवस्य परमात्मनः ।
द्विजेश्वरावतारं च सशिवं सुखदं सताम् ॥ १॥

English
Nandiśvara said :—  O dear, listen, I shall expound to you the incarnation of Siva the great soul as Dvijeśvara (a leading Brahmin) along with Siva. It is pleasing to the good. (1.)

---

Sanskrit
यः पूर्वं वर्णितस्तात भद्रायुर्नृपसत्तमः ।
यस्मिन्नृषभरूपेणानुग्रहं कृतवाञ्छिवः ॥ २॥

तद्धर्मस्य परीक्षार्थं पुनराविर्बभूव सः ।
द्विजेश्वरस्वरूपेण तदेव कथयाम्यहम् ॥ ३॥

English
O dear, I have already described to you the excellent king Bhadrāyu whom Śiva blessed in the form of a bull. In order to test his steadfastness and piety the lord manifested himself again in the form of a leading brahmin. I shall mention that same story. (2-3.)

---

Sanskrit
ऋषभस्य प्रभावेण शत्रूञ्जित्वा रणे प्रभुः ।
प्राप्तसिंहासनस्तात भद्रायुः सम्बभूव ह ॥ ४॥

English
Conquering the enemies through the power of Rābha in battle, Bhadrāyu attained the throne, O dear. (4.)

---

Sanskrit
चन्द्राङ्गदस्य तनया सीमन्तिन्याः शुभाङ्गजा ।
पत्नी तस्याभवद् ब्रह्मन्सुसाध्वी कीर्तिमालिनी ॥ ५॥

English
O brahmin, the chaste lady Kirtimalini, daughter of Candrangada and Simantini, became his wife. (5.)

---

Sanskrit
स भद्रायुः कदाचित्स्वप्रियया गहनं वनम् ।
प्राविशत्संविहारार्थं वसन्तसमये मुने ॥ ६॥

English
O sage, once after the advent of spring, the king Bhadrāyu entered a thick forest along with his beloved queen, for sport. (6.)

---

Sanskrit
अथ तस्मिन्वने रम्ये विजहार स भूपतिः ।
शरणागतपालिन्या तया स्वप्रियया सह ॥ ७॥

English
In that beautiful forest, the king sported about along with his beloved who protected those who sought refuge in her. (7.)

---

Sanskrit
अथ तद्धर्मदृढतां प्रतीक्षन्परमेश्वरः ।
लीलां चकार तत्रैव शिवया सह शङ्करः ॥ ८॥

English
Then, in order to test his steadfastness and piety, lord Siva carried out his sports there itself along with Siva. (8.)

---

Sanskrit
शिवा शिवश्च भूत्वोभौ तद्वने द्विजदम्पती ।
व्याघ्रं मायामयं कृत्वाविर्भूतौ निजलीलया ॥ ९॥

English
Siva and Siva in their own divine sports assumed the form of a brahmin couple and created an illusory tiger with their Maya. (9.)

---

Sanskrit
अथाविदूरे तस्यैव द्रवन्तौ भयविह्वलौ ।
अन्वीयमानौ व्याघ्रेण रुदन्तौ तौ बभूवतुः ॥ १०॥

English
Not far from the place where the king stood, they began running in great fright on being pursued by the tiger. They cried and shouted too. (10.)

---

Sanskrit
अथ विद्धौ च तौ तात भद्रायुः स महीपतिः ।
ददर्श क्रन्दमानौ हि शरण्यः क्षत्रियर्षभः ॥ ११॥

English
O dear, the king Bhadrāyu, the leading Kṣatriya worthy of being sought refuge in, saw both of them highly distressed and lamenting. (11.)

---

Sanskrit
अथ तौ मुनिशार्दूल स्वमायाद्विजदम्पती ।
भद्रायुषं महाराजमूचतुर्भयविह्वलौ ॥ १२॥

English
O leading sage, the illusory brahmin couple, extremely terrified, spoke to the king Bhadrāyu. (12.)

---

Sanskrit
द्विजदम्पती ऊचतुः ।
पाहि पाहि महाराज नावुभौ धर्मवित्तम ।
एष आयाति शार्दूलो जग्धुमावां महाप्रभो ॥ १३॥

English
The brahmin couple said :— O great king, O foremost of the knowers of virtue save us both. O great lord, this tiger is rushing on in order to devour us. (13.)

---

Sanskrit
एष हिंस्रः कालसमः सर्वप्राणिभयङ्करः ।
यावन्न खादति प्राप्य तावन्नौ रक्ष धर्मवित् ॥ १४॥

English
This beast of prey is like the god of death. It is terrifying to all living beings. Knower of virtue that you are save us before it pounces on us and devours us. (14.)

---

Sanskrit
नन्दीश्वर उवाच ।
इत्थमाक्रन्दितं श्रुत्वा तयोश्च नृपतीश्वरः ।
अति शीघ्रं महावीरः स यावद्धनुराददे ॥ १५॥

तावदभ्येत्य शार्दूलः त्वरमाणोऽतिमायिकः ।
स तस्य द्विजवर्यस्य मध्ये जग्राह तां वधूम् ॥ १६॥

English
Nandīśvara said:—  After hearing the lamentation of the pair, the great king of great heroism immediately took up his bow, but the rushing tiger of Māyic origin, seized the brahmin's wife. (15-16.)

---

Sanskrit
हे नाथ नाथ हे कान्त हा शम्भो हा जगद्गुरो ।
इति रोरूयमानां तां व्याघ्रो जग्रास भीषणः ॥ १७॥

English
The terrible tiger grasped the woman even as she was lamenting—"O lord, O husband, O Siva, O, preceptor of the universe." (17.)

---

Sanskrit
तावत्स राजा निशितैर्भल्लैर्व्याघ्रमताडयत् ।
न स तैर्विव्यथे किञ्चिद्गिरीन्द्र इव वृष्टिभिः ॥ १८॥

English
The king struck the tiger with sharp arrows; but it was not at all affected by them. It stood like a lofty mountain, which is not affected by showers. (18.)

---

Sanskrit
स शार्दूलो महासत्त्वो राज्ञः स्वैरकृतव्यथः ।
बलादाकृष्य तां नारीमपाक्रमत सत्वरः ॥ १९॥

English
The powerful tiger, not at all pained by the missiles of the king, seized the woman forcibly and ran away in a hurry. (19.)

---

Sanskrit
व्याघ्रेणापहृतां नारीं वीक्ष्य विप्रोऽतिविस्मितः ।
लौकिकीं गतिमाश्रित्य रुरोदाति मुहुर्मुहुः ॥ २०॥

English
On seeing his wife seized by the tiger, the brahmin was much bewildered and following the way of the world cried again and again. (20.)

---

Sanskrit
रुदित्वा चिरकालं च स विप्रो माययेश्वरः ।
भद्रायुषं महीपालं प्रोवाच मदहारकः ॥ २१॥

English
After crying for some time that brahmin who was lord Himself wielding his Māyā, spoke to the king Bhadrāyu dispelling his arrogance. (21.)

---

Sanskrit
द्विजेश्वर उवाच ।
राजन् क्व ते महास्त्राणि क्व ते त्राणं महद्धनुः ।
क्व ते द्वादशसाहस्रमहानागयुतं बलम् ॥ २२॥

English
Dvijetvara said:— O king, where are your great weapons? Where is your great bow that professes to protect? Where is your strength of ten or twelve thousand great elephants? (22.)

---

Sanskrit
किं ते खड्गेन शङ्खेन किं ते मन्त्रास्त्रविद्यया ।
किं सत्त्वेन महास्त्राणां किं प्रभावेण भूयसा ॥ २३॥

English
Of what avail is your sword? Your conch? Of what avail is your learning in the lores of mantras and missiles? Of what avail is your might? Of what avail is the strength of your great missiles? (23.)

---

Sanskrit
तत्सर्वं विफलं जातं यच्चान्यत्त्वयि तिष्ठति ।
यस्त्वं वनौकसां घातं न निवारयितुं क्षमः ॥ २४॥

English
All these have become futile, for you are incompetent to meet the onslaught of wild animals. (24.)

---

Sanskrit
क्षत्रस्यायं परो धर्मो क्षताच्च परिरक्षणम् ।
तस्मिन्कुलोचिते धर्मे नष्टे त्वज्जीवितेन किम् ॥ २५॥

English
The greatest duty of a Kṣatriya is the protection from injury. When that virtue hereditarily practised in your family is lost, of what avail is your life?72 (25.)

---

Sanskrit
आर्तानां शरणाप्तानां त्राणं कुर्वन्ति पार्थिवाः ।
प्राणैरर्थैश्च धर्मज्ञास्तद्विना च मृतोपमाः ॥ २६॥

English
Kings protect the distressed people who seek refuge in them with their very lives and assets. They are the knowers of virtue. Without that they are like a dead body. (26.)

---

Sanskrit
आर्तत्राणविहीनानां जीवितान्मरणं वरम् ।
धनिनां दानहीनानां गार्हस्थ्याद्भिक्षुता वरम् ॥ २७॥

English
Better death than life that does not protect the distressed. A mendicant is better than a rich householder who does not possess charitable nature. (27.)

---

Sanskrit
वरं विषाशनं प्राज्ञैर्वरमग्निप्रवेशनम् ।
कृपणानामनाथानां दीनानामपरक्षणात् ॥ २८॥

English
Better to swallow poison or enter fire than cease to protect the oppressed, the helpless and the poor. (28.)

---

Sanskrit
नन्दीश्वर उवाच ।
इत्थं विलपितं तस्य स्ववीर्यस्य च गर्हणम् ।
निशम्य नृपतिः शोकादात्मन्येवमचिन्तयत् ॥ २९॥

English
Nandīśvara said:—  On hearing his lamentation and the decrial of his prowess, the king thought within himself thus (29.)

---

Sanskrit
अहो मे पौरुषं नष्टमद्य दैवविपर्ययात् ।
अद्य कीर्तिश्च मे नष्टा पातकं प्राप्तमुत्कटम् ॥ ३०॥

English
"Alas, my manliness is lost today due to the adverse fate. Today my fame has been quashed. Great sin has been acquired. (30.)

---

Sanskrit
धर्मः कुलोचितो नष्टो मन्दभाग्यस्य दुर्मतेः ।
नूनं मे सम्पदो राज्यमायुष्यं क्षयमेष्यति ॥ ३१॥

English
The hereditary virtue has been smothered. I am unfortunate and confused in mind. Certainly my riches, my kingdom, my longevity will dwindle. (31.)

---

Sanskrit
अद्य चैनं द्विजन्मानं हतदारं शुचार्दितम् ।
हतशोकं करिष्यामि दत्त्वा प्राणानतिप्रियान् ॥ ३२॥

English
By surrendering my own life, lovable though it is, I shall make this brahmin free from grief, this brahmin whose wife has been seized by the tiger and who is pained extremely." (32.)

---

Sanskrit
इति निश्चित्य मनसा स भद्रायुर्नृपोत्तमः ।
पतित्वा पादयोस्तस्य बभाषे परिसान्त्वयन् ॥ ३३॥

English
Having decided thus in his mind that excellent king Bhadrāyu fell at his feet and spoke to him in conciliatory tone. (33.)

---

Sanskrit
भद्रायुरुवाच ।
कृपां कृत्वा मयि ब्रह्मन् क्षत्रबन्धौ हतौजसि ।
शोकन्त्यज महाप्राज्ञ दास्याम्यद्य तु वाञ्छितम् ॥ ३४॥

English
Bhadrāyu said:—  O Brahmin, I am a base Kṣatriya bereft of strength. You take pity on me. O intelligent one, do not grieve. I shall give you what you desire. (34.)

---

Sanskrit
इदं राज्यमियं राज्ञी ममेदं च कलेवरम् ।
त्वदधीनमिदं सर्वं किन्तेऽभिलषितं वरम् ॥ ३५॥

English
This kingdom, this queen, this body of mine, everything is subservient to you. What is the greatest thing that you desire? (35.)

---

Sanskrit
ब्राह्मण उवाच ।
किमादर्शेन चान्धस्य किं गृहेर्भैक्ष्यजीविनः ।
किं पुस्तकेन मूढस्य निस्त्रीकस्य धनेन किम् ॥ ३६॥

English
The Brahmin said:—  What can a blind man do with a mirror? Of what avail is a house to him who lives on alms? What can a fool do with a book? Of what avail is wealth to him who is bereft of his wife? (36.)

---

Sanskrit
अतोऽहं हतपत्नीको भुक्तभोगो न कर्हिचित् ।
इमान्तवाग्रमहिषीं कामये दीयतामिति ॥ ३७॥

English
Hence I who am not satiated with the enjoyment of pleasures but I whose wife is snatched away desire your crowned queen. Let her be given to me. (37.)

---

Sanskrit
भद्रायुरुवाच
दाता रसान्तवित्तस्य राज्यस्य गजवाजिनाम् ।
आत्मदेहस्य कस्यापि कलत्रस्य न कर्हिचित् ॥ ३८॥

English
Bhadrāyu said:—  I can give all my assets to the extent of the earth, my kingdom, horses and elephants and even my body to anyone but never can I give away my wife.
39 The sin that accrues by indulging in sexual intercourse with another man's wife cannot be wiped off even by hundreds of expiatory rites. (38.)

---

Sanskrit
ब्राह्मण उवाच ।
आस्तां ब्रह्मवधं घोरमपि मद्यनिषेवणम् ।
तपसा विधमिष्यामि किं पुनः पारदारिकम् ॥ ४०॥

English
The Brahmin said :—  Let it be the terrible sin of the slaughter of a brahmin; let it be the sin of sipping wine, I shall quell it with my power of penance. What then the sin of enjoying another man's wife? (40.)

---

Sanskrit
तस्मात्प्रयच्छ भार्यां स्वामियां कामो न मेऽपरः ।
अरक्षणाद्भयार्तानां गन्तासि निरयं ध्रुवम् ॥ ४१॥

English
Hence give me your wife. I have no other desire. Certainly you will go to hell by your inability to protect the oppressed. (41.)

---

Sanskrit
नन्दीश्वर उवाच ।
इति विप्रगिरा भीतश्चिन्तयामास पार्थिवः ।
अरक्षणान्महापापं पत्नीदानं ततो वरम् ॥ ४२॥

English
Nandāvara said:—  At the words of the brahmin, the frightened king thought to himself, "It is a sin to withhold protection. It is proper under the circumstance to give away my wife." (42.)

---

Sanskrit
अतः पत्नीं द्विजाग्र्याय दत्त्वा निर्मुक्तकिल्बिषः ।
सद्यो वह्निं प्रवेक्ष्यामि कीर्तिश्च विदिता भवेत् ॥ ४३॥

English
Hence I shall give my wife to this deserving brahmin and escape sin. Immediately thereafter I shall enter fire. My fame then shall be known." (43.)

---

Sanskrit
इति निश्चित्य मनसा समुज्ज्वाल्य हुताशनम् ।
तमाहूय द्विजं चक्रे पत्नीदानं सहोदकम् ॥ ४४॥

English
After deciding mentally like this, he kindled a blazing fire. He called the brahmin and gifted away his wife with water. (44.)

---

Sanskrit
स्वयं स्नातः शुचिर्भूत्वा प्रणम्य विबुधेश्वरान् ।
तमग्निं त्रिः परिक्रम्य शिवं दध्यौ समाहितः ॥ ४५॥

English
He took the ceremonial bath and became pure. He bowed to gods went round the fire thrice and meditated on Siva with pure mind. (45.)

---

Sanskrit

प्रत्यषेधत विश्वेशः प्रादुर्भूतो द्विजेश्वरः ॥ ४६॥

English
He was just to fall into the fire with his mind fixed at the feet of Siva, the lord of the universe when Dvijeśvara revealed himself and stayed him. (46.)

---

Sanskrit

मृणालगौरं गजचर्मवाससं गङ्गातरङ्गोक्षितमौलिदेशकम् ।
नागेन्द्रहारावलिकण्ठभूषणं किरीटकाञ्च्यङ्गदकङ्कणोज्ज्वलम् ॥ ४८॥

शूलासिखट्वाङ्गकुठारचर्म-
मृगाभयाष्टाङ्गपिनाकहस्तम् ।
वृषोपरिस्थं शितिकण्ठभूषणं
प्रोद्भूतमग्रे स नृपो ददर्श ॥ ४९॥

English
The king saw in front of him the five-faced, the three-eyed lord with the Pinaka in his hand, bedecked by the digit of the moon, with hanging matted hair shedding tawny lustre, having the brilliance of a crore of midday blazing suns, white as the lotus stalk fibre, wearing the hide of the elephant, with his head drenched by the waves of Gangā, having the necklaces of great serpents, bedecked in coronet, waistband, shoulderlet and shining bangles, holding in his hands, the trident, the sword, the missile Khaṭvāṅga, the dagger, the shield, the deer, the mystic sign of protection, the eight articles of worship and the Pinaka, seated on his bull and blue-necked. (47-49.)

Sanskrit
