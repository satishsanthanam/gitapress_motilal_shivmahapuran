### **CHAPTER SIX - (The Brahmin lady attains heaven)**

---

Sanskrit
सूत उवाच ।
गौश्चैकाप्यभवत्तत्र ह्यङ्गणे बन्धिता शुभा ।
तदैव ब्राह्मणो रात्रावाजगाम बहिर्गतः ॥ १॥

English
Sūta said :—  There was a fine cow tied up in the courtyard. The brahmin of the house who had gone out returned after nightfall. (1.)

---

Sanskrit
स उवाच प्रियां स्वीयां दृष्ट्वा गामङ्गणे स्थिताम् ।
अदुग्धां खेदनिर्विण्णो दोग्धुकामो मुनीश्वराः ॥ २॥

English
O great sages, on seeing the cow, tethered in the courtyard, not yet milked, the distressed brahmin desirous of milking it told his wife. (2.)

---

Sanskrit
सूत उवाच ।
इत्येतद्वचनं श्रुत्वा स्वमातुर्गोर्द्विजोत्तमाः ।
मौनत्वं स्वीकृतं तत्र वत्सेनोक्तं न किञ्चन ॥ ३॥

English
In order to milk the cow he called his wife quickly and said “O beloved, the cow is not yet milked.” Thus urged she brought the calf. (3.)

---

Sanskrit
वत्सं कीले स्वयं बद्धुं यत्नं चैवाकरोत्तदा ।
ब्राह्मणः स गृहस्वामी मुनयो दुग्धलालसः ॥ ४॥

English
O sages, the owner of the house, the brahmin, desirous of milk tried to tie up the calf to the peg. (4.)

---

Sanskrit
वत्सोऽपि कर्षमाणश्च पादे वै पादपीडनम् ।
चकार ब्राह्मणश्चैव कष्टं प्राप्तश्च सुव्रताः ॥ ५॥

English
Dragged by the leg the calf kicked. O great one of good rites, the brahmin was put to pain. (5.)

---

Sanskrit
तेन पादप्रहारेण स द्विजः क्रोधमूर्छितः ।
वत्सं च ताडयामास कूपैर्दृढतरं तदा ॥ ६॥

English
Infuriated by the kick of the calf, he hit the calf severely with the pieces of firewood. (6.)

---

Sanskrit
वत्सोऽपि पीडितस्तेन श्रान्तश्चैवाभवत्तदा ।
दुग्धा गौर्मोचितो वत्सो न क्रोधेन द्विजन्मना ॥ ७॥

English
Hit by him the calf became utterly exhausted. Due to anger neither the cow was milked nor the calf was untied by the brahmin. (7.)

---

Sanskrit
गौर्दोग्धुं च महत्प्रीत्या रोदनं चाकरोत्तदा ।
दृष्ट्वा च रोदनं तस्या वत्सो वाक्यमथाब्रवीत् ॥ ८॥

English
For the pleasure of getting milked, the cow mooed loudly. On hearing the mooing sound of the cow, the calf said. (8.)

---

Sanskrit
एवं ज्ञात्वा त्वया मातर्दुःखं त्याज्यं सुयत्नतः ।
सुभगाचरणं कार्यं परलोकसुखेप्सया ॥ ९॥

English
The calf said:—  "O mother, why do you cry? What is the misery that has befallen you? Please tell me lovingly." On hearing this, the cow said. (9.)

---

Sanskrit
श्रूयतां पुत्र मे दुःखं वक्तुं शक्नोम्यहं न हि ।
दुष्टेन ताडितस्त्वं च तेन दुःखं ममाप्यभूत् ॥ १०॥

English
"O son, listen to me. Although I am unable to express my grief adequately, you have been hit by the wicked man. Hence I am distressed." (10.)

---

Sanskrit
सूत उवाच ।
स्वमातुर्वचनं श्रुत्वा स वत्सः प्रत्यबोधयत् ।
प्रत्युवाच स्वजननीं प्रारब्धपरिनिष्ठितः ॥ ११॥

English
*Sūta said:—* On hearing his mother's words, the calf taking his stand by the principle of previous action replied enlightening his mother. (11.)

---

Sanskrit
किं कर्त्तव्यं क्व गन्तव्यं कर्मबद्धा वयं यतः ।
कृतं चैव यथा पूर्वं भुज्यते च तथाधुना ॥ १२॥

English
"What should be done? Where should we go? Since the results are bound by previous actions, we experience joy or sorrow accordingly. (12.)

---

Sanskrit
हसता क्रियते कर्म रुदता परिभुज्यते ।
दुःखदाता न कोऽप्यस्ति सुखदाता न कश्चन ॥ १३॥

English
Activities are pursued with pleasure and fruits are derived sorrowfully. There is none who gives misery and none who gives happiness. (13.)

---

Sanskrit
सुखदुःखे परो दत्त इत्येषा कुमतिर्मता ।
अहं चापि करोम्यत्र मिथ्याज्ञानं तदोच्यते ॥ १४॥

English
This is a wrong notion that another man gives us sorrow or pleasure. This is also an illusory concept that it is I who do this or that. (14.)

---

Sanskrit
स्वकर्मणा भवेद्दुःखं सुखं तेनैव कर्मणा ।
तस्माच्च पूज्यते कर्म सर्वं कर्मणि संस्थितम् ॥ १५॥

English
Misery or happiness results from our own actions. Hence action is prominent and everything is founded on action. (15.)

---

Sanskrit
त्वं चैवाहं च जननी इमे जीवादयश्च ये ।
ते सर्वे कर्मणा बद्धा न शोच्याः कर्हिचित्त्वया ॥ १६॥

English
You the mother, I the child and all the living beings are bound by action. They are not to be bewailed by you. (16.)

---

Sanskrit
सूत उवाच ।
एवं श्रुत्वा स्वपुत्रस्य वचनं ज्ञानगर्भितम् ।
पुत्रशोकान्विता दीना सा च गौरब्रवीदिदम् ॥ १७॥

English
*Sūta said:—* On hearing these words of her calf full of wisdom, the distressed cow sorrowing for her calf, said thus. (17.)

---

Sanskrit
गौरुवाच ।
वत्स सर्वं विजानासि कर्माधीनाः प्रजा इति ।
तथापि मायया ग्रस्ता दुःखं प्राप्नोम्यहं पुनः ॥ १८॥

English
The cow said:—  “O dear son, I know fully well that all the people are subservient to their actions. Still, seized by Māyā I feel much distressed. (18.)

---

Sanskrit
रोदनं च कृतं भूरि दुःखशान्तिर्भवेन्न हि ।
इत्येतद्वचनं श्रुत्वा प्रसूं वत्सोऽब्रवीदिदम् ॥ १९॥

English
I have cried a lot but the sorrow has not subsided.” On hearing these words, the calf spoke to her again. (19.)

---

Sanskrit
वत्स उवाच ।
यद्येवं च विजानासि पुनश्च रुदनं कुतः ।
कृत्वा च साध्यते किञ्चित्तस्माद्दुःखं त्यजाधुना ॥ २०॥

English
The calf said:—  If you know thus, can anything be gained by crying? Hence leave off your sorrow. (20.)

---

Sanskrit
सूत उवाच ।
एवं पुत्रवचः श्रुत्वा तन्माता दुःखसंयुता ।
निःश्वस्याति तदा धेनुर्वत्सं वचनमब्रवीत् ॥ २१॥

English
*Sūta said:—* On hearing the words of her son, the distressed cow heaved a deep sigh and spoke to the calf. (21.)

---

Sanskrit
गौरुवाच ।
मम दुःखं तदा गच्छेद्यदा दुःखं तथाविधम् ।
भवेद्धि ब्राह्मणस्यापि सत्यमेतद्ब्रवीम्यहम् ॥ २२॥

English
The cow said:—  My distress will disappear only when a similar misery befalls the brahmin. I am telling you the truth. (22.)

---

Sanskrit
प्रातश्चैव मया पुत्र श‍ृङ्गाभ्यां हि हनिष्यते ।
हतस्य जीवितं सद्यो यास्यत्यस्य न संशयः ॥ २३॥

English
Tomorrow morning I shall hit him by my horns. No doubt, when hit he will lose his life. (23.)

---

Sanskrit
वत्स उवाच ।
प्रथमं यत्कृतं कर्म तत्फलं भुज्यतेऽधुना ।
अस्याश्च ब्रह्महत्याया मातः किं फलमाप्स्यसे ॥ २४॥

English
The calf said:—  The fruit of previous actions we experience now. What is the fruit you are going to reap as a result of this brahmin-slaughter? (24.)

---

Sanskrit
समाभ्यां पुण्यपापाभ्यां भवेज्जन्म च भारते ।
तयोः क्षये च भोगेन मातर्मुक्तिरवाप्यते ॥ २५॥

English
When merits and demerits are on a par one takes birth in Bhārata. O mother, when they are wiped off by enjoyment or suffering, liberation is achieved. (25.)

---

Sanskrit
कदापि कर्मणो नाशः कदा भोगः प्रजायते ।
तस्माच्च पुनरेवं त्वं कर्म मा कर्तुमुद्यता ॥ २६॥

English
Some time the action is destroyed, some time the action results. That is why you have begun to perform this misdeed. (26.)

---

Sanskrit
अहं कुतस्ते पुत्रोऽद्य त्वं माता कुत एव च ।
वृथाभिमानः पुत्रत्वे मातृत्वे च विचार्यताम् ॥ २७॥

English
Whence am I your son? Whence are you my mother? This fond consciousness of sonhood and motherhood is futile. Ponder. (27.)

---

Sanskrit
क्व माता क्व पिता विद्धि क्व स्वामी क्व कलत्रकम् ।
न कोऽपि कस्य चास्तीह सर्वेऽपि स्वकृतम्भुजः ॥ २८॥

English
Where is mother? Where is father? Where is husband? Where is wife? Know that none belongs to any one here. All reap the fruits of their own actions. (28.)

---

English
O mother, knowing thus you cast off your misery strenuously. If there is a desire for pleasure hereafter you shall do good deeds here. (29.)

---

Sanskrit
गौरुवाच ।
एवं जानाम्यहं पुत्र माया मां न जहात्यसौ ।
त्वद्दुःखेन समं दुःखं तस्मै दास्ये तदेव हि ॥ ३०॥

English
The Cow said :—  O son, I know this but delusion does not forsake me. I am much distressed by your misery. I shall return tit for tat. (30.)

---

Sanskrit
पुनश्च ब्रह्महत्याया नाशो यत्र भवेदिह ।
तत्स्थलं च मया दृष्टं हत्या मे हि गमिष्यति ॥ ३१॥

English
I know the place that dispels the sin of brahminslaughter. Going there I can get rid of the sin of Brahminslaughter. (31.)

---

English
Sūta said :—  O excellent brahmins, on hearing these words of the mother cow, the calf observed silence. It did not say anything more. (32.)

---

Sanskrit
तयोस्तदद्भुतं वृत्तं श्रुत्वा पान्थो द्विजस्तदा ।
हृदा विचारयामास विस्मितो हि मुनीश्वराः ॥ ३३॥

English
O great sages, on hearing this wonderful dialogue between the two, the pilgrim brahmin was surprised and he thought within himself. (33.)

---

Sanskrit
इदमत्यद्भुतं वृत्तं दृष्ट्वा प्रातर्मया खलु ।
गन्तव्यं पुनरेवातो गन्तव्यं तत्स्थलं पुनः ॥ ३४॥

English
"I shall leave in the morning only after seeing the wonderful sequel. I shall go to that holy spot myself." (34.)

---

Sanskrit
सूत उवाच ।
विचार्येति हृदा विप्रः स द्विजाः सेवकेन च ।
सुष्वाप तत्र जननीभक्तः परमविस्मितः ॥ ३५॥

English
O brahmins, after thinking thus the pilgrim-brahmin, devoted to his mother, became surprised. He and his servant went to sleep. (35.)

---

Sanskrit
प्रातःकाले तदा जाते गृहस्वामी समुत्थितः ।
बोधयामास तं पान्थं वचनं चेदमब्रवीत् ॥ ३६॥

English
When the day dawned, the owner of the house got up. Awakening the traveller he spoke these words. (36.)

---

Sanskrit
द्विज उवाच ।
स्वपिषि त्वं किमर्थं हि प्रातःकालो भवत्यलम् ।
स्वयात्रां कुरु तं देशं गमनेच्छा च यत्र ह ॥ ३७॥

English
The brahmin said :—  "Why do you sleep? It is already day-break. Go on your journey to the place where you desire to go." (37.)

---

Sanskrit
तेनोक्तं श्रूयतां ब्रह्मन् शरीरे सेवकस्य मे ।
वर्तते हि व्यथा स्थित्वा मुहूर्तं गम्यते ततः ॥ ३८॥

English
He replied—"Please listen, O brahmin, there is an acute pain in the stomach of my servant. We will stay a little longer and go thereafter." (38.)

---

Sanskrit
सूत उवाच ।
इत्येवं च मिषं कृत्वा सुष्वाप पुरुषस्तदा ।
तद्वृत्तमखिलं ज्ञातुमद्भुतं विस्मयावहम् ॥ ३९॥

English
Sūta said :—  Creating such a pretext the man continued to sleep, desiring to know the details of the wonderful incident that had surprised him much. (39.)

---

Sanskrit
दोहनस्य तदा काले ब्राह्मणः स्वसुतं प्रति ।
उवाच गन्तुकामश्च कार्यार्थं कुत्रचिच्च सः ॥ ४०॥

English
At the time of milking, the brahmin who wanted to go somewhere on some business spoke to his son. (40.)

---

Sanskrit
पितोवाच ।
मया तु गम्यते पुत्र कार्यार्थं कुत्रचित्पुनः ।
धेनुर्दोह्या त्वया वत्स सावधानादियं निजा ॥ ४१॥

English
The father said :—  O son, I am going out on some business. Dear, you shall carefully milk our cow. (41.)

---

Sanskrit
सूत उवाच ।
इत्युक्त्वा ब्राह्मणवरः स जगाम च कुत्रचित् ।
पुत्रः समुत्थितस्तत्र वत्सं च मुक्तवांस्तदा ॥ ४२॥

English
Sūta said :—  After saying this, the brahmin left the house. Thereafter the son got up and untied the calf. (42.)

---

Sanskrit
माता च तस्य दोहार्थमाजगाम स्वयं तदा ।
द्विजपुत्रस्तदा वत्सं खिन्नं कीलेन ताडितम् ॥ ४३॥

बन्धनार्थं हि गोः पार्श्वमनयद्दुग्धलालसः ।
पुनर्गौश्च तदा क्रुद्धा श‍ृङ्गेनाताडयच्च तम् ॥ ४४॥

English
The cow approached him herself for being milked. The brahmin's son, eager for milk, took the distressed calf hit by his father near the cow in order to tie it up. The infuriated cow hit him with her horn. (43-44.)

---

Sanskrit
पपात मूर्च्छां सम्प्राप्य सोऽपि मर्मणि ताडितः ।
लोकाश्च मिलितास्तत्र गवा बालो विहिंसितः ॥ ४५॥

जलं जलं वदन्तस्ते पित्राद्या यत्र संस्थिताः ।
यत्नश्च क्रियते यावत्तावद् बालो मृतस्तदा ॥ ४६॥

English
The boy, hit in a vulnerable point, fell unconscious. People gathered there, saying—"O, the boy is struck by the cow. Fetch water? fetch water?" By the time they attempted to do something, the boy died. (45-46.)

---

Sanskrit
मृते च बालके तत्र हाहाकारो महानभूत् ।
तन्माता दुःखिता ह्यासीद् रुरोद च पुनः पुनः ॥ ४७॥

English
When the boy died, there was a great hue and cry. His mother was struck with grief. She cried again and again. (47.)

---

Sanskrit
किं करोमि क्व गच्छामि को मे दुःखं व्यपोहयेत् ।
रुदित्वेति तदा गां च ताडयित्वा व्यमोचयत् ॥ ४८॥

English
"What shall I do? Where shall I go? Who can remove my grief?" After lamenting thus she struck the cow and let it off. (48.)

---

Sanskrit
श्वेतवर्णा तदा सा गौर्द्रुतं श्यामा व्यदृश्यत ।
अहो च दृश्यतां लोकाश्चुक्रुशुश्च परस्परम् ॥ ४९॥

English
The cow which had been white in colour turned black immediately. People cried to one another "O see O see." (49.)

---

Sanskrit
ब्राह्मणश्च तदा पान्थो दृष्ट्वाश्चर्यं विनिर्गतः ।
यत्र गौश्च गता तत्र तामनु ब्राह्मणो गतः ॥ ५०॥

English
On seeing this surprising event the brahmin traveller set out and followed the cow as it ran away. (50.)

---

Sanskrit
ऊर्ध्वपुच्छं तदा कृत्वा शीघ्रं गौर्नर्मदां प्रति ।
आगत्य नन्दिकस्यास्य समीपे नर्मदाजले ॥ ५१॥

सन्निमज्य त्रिवारं तु श्वेतत्वं च गता हि सा ।
यथागतं गता सा च ब्राह्मणो विस्मयं गतः ॥ ५२॥

English
Lifting up its tail, the cow ran off to the river Narmadā. Coming near the Nandikeśa shrine, it dipped itself into the waters of the Narmadā thrice. It regained its white colour and went away the way it came. The brahmin was surprised. (51-52.)

---

Sanskrit
अहो धन्यतमं तीर्थं ब्रह्महत्यानिवारणम् ।
स्वयं ममज्ज तत्रासौ ब्राह्मणः सेवकस्तथा ॥ ५३॥

English
Ah, blessed indeed is this holy centre that removes the sin of Brahmin-slaughter. The brahmin and the servant took their baths there. (53.)

---

Sanskrit
निमज्ज्य हि गतौ तौ च प्रशंसन्तौ नदीं च ताम् ।
मार्गे च मिलिता काचित्सुन्दरी भूषणान्विता ॥ ५४॥

English
After the holy dip they started on their journey praising the river. On the way they met a beautiful maiden fully bedecked in ornaments. (54.)

---

Sanskrit
तयोक्तं तं च भोः पान्थ कुतो यासि सुविस्मितः ।
सत्यं ब्रूहि च्छलं त्यक्त्वा विप्रवर्य ममाग्रतः ॥ ५५॥

English
She said:—  "O brahmin-traveller where do you go in such bewilderment? Eschewing all deceit tell me the truth here. (55.)

Sanskrit
सूत उवाच ।
एवं वचस्तदा श्रुत्वा द्विजेनोक्तं यथातथम् ।
पुनश्चायं द्विजस्तत्र स्त्रियोक्तः स्थीयतां त्वया ॥ ५६॥

English
On hearing these words, the brahmin narrated everything truthfully. The brahmin was again addressed by the women, "Stay here". (56.)

Sanskrit
तयोक्तं च समाकर्ण्य स्थितः स ब्राह्मणस्ततः ।
प्रत्युवाच विनीतात्मा कथ्यते किं वदेति च ॥ ५७॥

English
On hearing her words, the brahmin stopped and replied humbly—"Speak out what you desire to say. (57.)

Sanskrit
सा चाह पुनरेवात्र त्वया दृष्टं स्थलं च यत् ।
तत्राधुना क्षिपास्थीनि मातुः किं गम्यतेऽन्यतः ॥ ५८॥

English
She said again "In the holy spot that you have just seen, cast off the bones of your mother. Why shall you go elsewhere? (58.)

Sanskrit
तव माता पान्थवर्य साक्षाद्दिव्यमयं वरम् ।
देहं धृत्वा द्रुतं साक्षाच्छम्भोर्यास्यति सद्गतिम् ॥ ५९॥

English
O excellent traveller, your mother will assume a divine form and will immediately attain the good goal of Siva. (59.)

---

Sanskrit
वैशाखे चैव सम्प्राप्ते सप्तम्याश्च दिने शुभे ।
सिते पक्षे सदा गङ्गा ह्यायाति द्विजसत्तम ॥ ६०॥

English
O excellent brahmin, in the month of Vaisakha on the auspicious seventh day in the bright half, Gangā comes here always. (60.)

---

Sanskrit
अद्यैव सप्तमी या सा गन्गाऽहं यामि तत्र वै ।
इत्युक्त्वान्तर्दधे देवी सा गङ्गा मुनिसत्तमाः ॥ ६१॥

English
Today is that seventh day. The river is in the form of Gangā there." Saying so the lady vanished. O excellent sages, she was Gangā herself. (61.)

---

Sanskrit
निवृत्तश्च द्विजः सोऽपि मात्रस्थ्यर्द्धं स्ववस्त्रतः ।
क्षिपेद्यावत्तत्र तीर्थे तावच्चित्रमभूत्तदा ॥ ६२॥

English
The brahmin returned. As soon as he cast off half the bones of his mother from his bundle into the holy river, a wonderful incident happened. (62.)

---

Sanskrit
दिव्यदेहत्वमापन्ना स्वमाता च व्यदृश्यत ।
धन्योऽसि कृतकृत्योऽसि पवित्रं च कुलं त्वया ॥ ६३॥

धनं धान्यं तथा चायुर्वंशो वै वर्धतां तव ।
इत्याशिषं मुहुर्दत्त्वा स्वपुत्राय दिवं गता ॥ ६४॥

English
He saw his mother endowed with a divine form. She said—"You are blessed. You have fulfilled your duty. The family has been sanctified by you. May your wealth, food-grains, longevity and line flourish." Blessing her son frequently, she went to heaven. (63-64.)

---

Sanskrit
तत्र भुक्त्वा सुखं भूरि चिरकालं महोत्तमम् ।
शङ्करस्य प्रसादेन गता सा ह्युत्तमां गतिम् ॥ ६५॥

English
After enjoying excellent happiness there for a long time, she attained the excellent goal, thanks to the grace of Siva. (65.)

---

Sanskrit
ब्राह्मणश्च सुतस्तस्याः क्षिप्त्वास्थीनि पुनस्ततः ।
प्रसन्नमानसोऽभूत्स शुद्धात्मा स्वगृहं गतः ॥ ६६॥

English
Her son, the brahmin, cast off the remaining bones too. He was much delighted in his mind. With contented soul he returned to his abode. (66.)
