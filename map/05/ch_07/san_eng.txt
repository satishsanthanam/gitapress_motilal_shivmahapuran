### **CHAPTER SEVEN - (Pathway to Hell and the Emissaries of Yama)**

---

Sanskrit
सनत्कुमार उवाच ।
अथ पापैर्नरा यान्ति यमलोकं चतुर्विधैः ।
सन्त्रासजननं घोरं विवशाः सर्वदेहिनः ॥ १॥

English
Sanatkumāra said :—  As a result of the four kinds of sins, all living beings go to Yamaloka. They are helpless. (1.)

---

Sanskrit
गर्भस्थैर्जायमानैश्च बालैस्तरुणमध्यमैः ।
स्त्रीपुन्नपुंसकैर्जीवैर्ज्ञातव्यं सर्वजन्तुषु ॥ २॥

English
It shall be known to all living beings whether in the womb, in the process of birth, as children, youth or middling whether women, man or a eunuch. (2.)

---

Sanskrit
शुभाशुभफलं चात्र देहिनां संविचार्यते ।
चित्रगुप्तादिभिः सर्वैः वसिष्ठप्रमुखैस्तथा ॥ ३॥

English
Here the result of the auspicious and the inauspicious actions of all embodied beings is being considered by Citragupta Vasiṣṭha and others. (3.)

---

Sanskrit
न केचित्प्राणिनः सन्ति ये न यान्ति यमक्षयम् ।
अवश्यं हि कृतं कर्म भोक्तव्यं तद्विचार्यताम् ॥ ४॥

English
There are no living beings who do not go to Yama's abode. The result of the action must of necessity be undergone. Let that be considered. (4.)

---

Sanskrit
तत्र ये शुभकर्माणः सौम्यचित्ता दयान्विताः ।
ते नरा यान्ति सौम्येन पूर्वं यमनिकेतनम् ॥ ५॥

English
Men who have performed auspicious rites who are of gentle minds, endowed with the quality of compassion, go to Yama's abode through the gentle entrance at the East. (5.)

---

Sanskrit
ये पुनः पापकर्माणः पापा दानविवर्जिताः ।
ते घोरेण पथा यान्ति दक्षिणेन यमालयम् ॥ ६॥

English
Sinners, habitually performing sinful actions and devoid of charitable nature, pass through the terrible path and enter Yama's abode by the southern gate. (6.)

---

Sanskrit
षडशीतिसहस्राणि योजनानामतीत्य तत् ।
वैवस्वतपुरं ज्ञेयं नानारूपमवस्थितम् ॥ ७॥

English
It must be known that the multiformed city of Yama²³⁰ is situated at a distance of eightysix thousand Yojanas from the Earth. (7.)

---

Sanskrit
समीपस्थमिवाभाति नराणां पुण्यकर्मणाम् ।
पापिनामतिदूरस्थं यथा रौद्रेण गच्छताम् ॥ ८॥

English
To men of auspicious deeds it appears to be very near, but to the sinners who go along the terrible path it is situated far off. (8.)

---

Sanskrit
तीक्ष्णकण्टकयुक्तेन शर्कराविचितेन च ।
क्षुरधारानिभैस्तीक्ष्णैः पाषाणै रचितेन च ॥ ९॥

English
At places the path is strewn with sharp thorns, at places it is full of sand, elsewhere it is full of pebbles sharp like the razor-edge. (9.)

---

Sanskrit
क्वचित्पङ्केन महता जलौकाभिश्च सङ्कुलैः ।
लोहसूचीनिभैर्दर्भैः सम्पन्नेन पथा क्वचित् ॥ १०॥

English
Somewhere the place is marshy, somewhere full of long or short Darbha grass like iron pins that split the legs. (10.)

---

Sanskrit
तटप्रायातिविषमैः पर्वतैर्वृक्षसङ्कुलैः ।
प्रतप्ताङ्गारयुक्तेन यान्ति मार्गेण दुःखिताः ॥ ११॥

English
Elsewhere it is full of mountains overgrown with trees and resembling impassable bunds. The distressed persons go along the path full of glowing coal. (11.)

---

Sanskrit
क्वचिद्विषमगर्तैश्च क्वचिल्लोष्ठैः सुदुष्करैः ।
सुतप्तबालुकाभिश्च तथा तीक्ष्णैश्च शङ्कुभिः ॥ १२॥

English
In other places it is full of deep irregular chasms and canyons, elsewhere, of rugged lumps of clay; of burning sands here and sharp spikes there. (12.)

---

Sanskrit
अनेकशाखाविततैर्व्याप्तं वंशवनैः क्वचित् ।
कष्टेन तमसा मार्गेणानालम्बेन कुत्रचित् ॥ १३॥

English
Somewhere it is pervaded by bamboo groves with many branches spreading, elsewhere the path is enveloped in darkness, in some places the path has no support at all. (13.)

---

Sanskrit
अयः श‍ृङ्गाटकैस्तीक्ष्णैः क्वचिद्दावाग्निना पुनः ।
क्वचित्तप्तशिलाभिश्च क्वचिद्व्याप्तं हिमेन च ॥ १४॥

English
Somewhere the cross-roads are full of sharp iron pieces, elsewhere there is a forest-fire; in other places these are hot rocks; in some places snow spreads over it. (14.)

---

Sanskrit
क्वचिद्वालुकया व्याप्तमाकण्ठान्तः प्रवेशया ।
क्वचिद्दुष्टाम्बुना व्याप्तं क्वचिच्च करिषाग्निना ॥ १५॥

English
Some places are full of fine sand where the persons sink up to the neck; elsewhere it is full of stinking muddy water and some places are covered with burning balls of dry cowdung. (15.)

---

Sanskrit
क्वचित् सिहैर्वृकैर्व्याघ्रैर्मशकैश्च सुदारुणैः ।
क्वचिन्महाजलौकाभिः क्वचिच्चाजगरैस्तथा ॥ १६॥

English
Different places are infested with different terrible beasts of prey such as lions, wolves, tigers and huge pythons or terrible mosquitoes or huge leeches. (16.)

---

Sanskrit
मक्षिकाभिश्च रौद्राभिः क्वचित् सर्पैर्विषोल्बणैः ।
मत्तमातङ्गयूथैश्च बलोन्मत्तैः प्रमाथिभिः ॥ १७॥

English
Terrible flies, extremely poisonous serpents, herds of elephants in rut mad and mighty, crushing everything under their feet infest the paths. (17.)

---

Sanskrit
पन्थानमुल्लिखद्भिश्च सूकरैस्तीक्ष्णदंष्ट्रिभिः ।
तीक्ष्णश‍ृङ्गैश्च महिषैः सर्वभूतैश्च श्वापदैः ॥ १८॥

English
-19. The persons who go that way are tortured and harassed by big boars digging and butting against the path with their sharp fangs, buffaloes with sharp horns, all sorts of beasts of prey, terrible evil spirits like Dakinis, horrible Raksasas and pernicious diseases. (18.)

---

Sanskrit
महाधूलिविमिश्रेण महाचण्डेन वायुना ।
महापाषाणवर्षेण हन्यमाना निराश्रयाः ॥ २०॥

English
They are without any shelter when fierce gusts of wind raising huge columns of dust blow sharply against them or showers of massive stones smother them. (20.)

---

Sanskrit
क्वचिद्विद्युत्प्रपातेन दह्यमाना व्रजन्ति च ।
महता बाणवर्षेण विध्यमानाश्च सर्वतः ॥ २१॥

English
They go on, burnt and scorched by lightning falls and pierced through by heavy showers of arrows. (21.)

---

Sanskrit
पतद्भिर्वज्रपातैश्च उल्कापातैश्च दारुणैः ।
प्रदीप्ताङ्गारवर्षेण दह्यमानाश्च सन्ति हि ॥ २२॥

English
They are scorched and burnt by the showers of burning coal, the falling terrible thunderbolts and meteors. (22.)

---

Sanskrit
महता पांसुवर्षेण पूर्यमाणा रुदन्ति च ।
महामेघरवैर्घोरैस्त्रस्यन्ते च मुहुर्मुहुः ॥ २३॥

English
They cry when heavy showers of dust envelop them. They tremble with fear ever and anon at the terrible rumbling sounds of massive clouds. (23.)

---

Sanskrit
निशितायुधवर्षेण भिद्यमानाश्च सर्वतः ।
महाक्षाराम्बुधाराभिः सिच्यमाना व्रजन्ति च ॥ २४॥

English
They are split by the shower of sharp weapons and drenched with acid-currents as they go on. (24.)

---

Sanskrit
महीशीतेन मरुता रूक्षेण परुषेण च ।
समन्ताद् बाध्यमानाश्च शुष्यन्ते सङ्कुचन्ति च ॥ २५॥

English
They shrink and wither when oppressed by the rough and chill wind all round. (25.)

---

Sanskrit
इत्थं मार्गेण रौद्रेण पाथेयरहितेन च ।
निरालम्बेन दुर्गेण निर्जलेन समन्ततः ॥ २६॥

विषमेणैव महता निर्जनापाश्रयेण च ।
तमोरूपेण कष्टेन सर्वदुष्टाश्रयेण च ॥ २७॥

नीयन्ते देहिनः सर्वे ये मूढाः पापकर्मिणः ।
यमदूतैर्महाघोरैस्तदाज्ञाकारिभिर्बलात् ॥ २८॥

English
All those foolish persons who habitually sin are led through such paths by the terrible emissaries of Yam forcefully, in carrying out his behests.-the paths that are terrible, devoid of feeding, devoid of basic support, impassable, lacking in water, rugged, desolate, dark and gloomy and full of pain and misery and all sorts of evil things. (26-28.)

---

Sanskrit
एकाकिनः पराधीना मित्रबन्धुविवर्जिताः ।
शोचन्तः स्वानि कर्माणि रुदन्तश्च मुहुर्मुहुः ॥ २९॥

English
They are lonely, devoid of friends and relatives. They are dependent on others. They bewail their evil actions. They cry again and again. (29.)

---

Sanskrit
प्रेता भूत्वा विवस्त्राश्च शुष्ककण्ठौष्ठतालुकाः ।
असौम्या भयभीताश्च दह्यमानाः क्षुधान्विताः ॥ ३०॥

English
They have by this time become ghosts. They have no cloth on. Their throats, lips and palates are parched. They are frightened of terror. They are hungry and feel a burning sensation all over. (30.)

---

Sanskrit
बद्धाः श‍ृङ्खलया केचिदुत्ता नपादका नराः ।
कृष्यन्ते कृष्यमाणाश्च यमदूतैर्बलोत्कटैः ॥ ३१॥

English
Some are bound with fetters and forced to keep their legs lifted up. They are dragged again and again by the extraordinarily powerful emissaries of Yama. (31.)

---

Sanskrit
उरसाधोमुखाश्चान्ये घृष्यमाणाः सुदुःखिताः ।
केशपाशनिबन्धेन सङ्कृष्यन्ते च रज्जुना ॥ ३२॥

English
Others with downcast faces are against the chest. They are distressed. They are dragged with a rope fastened to their tresses. (32.)

---

Sanskrit
ललाटे चाङ्कुशेनान्ये भिन्ना दुष्यन्ति देहिनः ।
उत्तानाः कण्टकपथा क्वचिदङ्गारवर्त्मना ॥ ३३॥

English
Men in supine position are dragged along the thorny path or that covered with burning coal, by means of a goad clutching at their foreheads. (33.)

---

Sanskrit
पश्चाद् बाहुनिबद्धाश्च जठरेण प्रपीडिताः ।
पूरिताः श‍ृङ्खलाभिश्च हस्तयोश्च सुकीलिताः ॥ ३४॥

English
The hands of some are tied behind and they are hit in the belly. Others are completely bound with iron fetters. Still others are nailed in their hands. (34.)

---

Sanskrit
ग्रीवापाशेन कृष्यन्ते प्रयान्त्यन्ये सुदुःखिताः ।
जिह्वाङ्कुशप्रवेशेन रज्ज्वाकृष्यन्त एव ते ॥ ३५॥

English
A few others are dragged with a noose tied round their necks. They go ahead in distress. Others are dragged by ropes. Their tongues are pierced through by goads. (35.)

---

Sanskrit
नासाभेदेन रज्ज्वा च त्वाकृष्यन्ते तथापरे ।
भिन्नाः कपोलयो रज्ज्वा कृष्यन्तेऽन्ये तथौष्ठयोः ॥ ३६॥

English
The noses of others are bored and ropes are tied through them and they are dragged. Similarly others have their cheeks and lips bored and are dragged by ropes. (36.)

---

Sanskrit
छिन्नाग्रपादहस्ताश्च छिन्नकर्णोष्ठनासिकाः ।
सञ्छिन्नशिश्नवृषणाः छिन्नभिन्नाङ्गसन्धयः ॥ ३७॥

English
The tips of the hands and the legs of some are cut off. The ears, noses and lips of others are cut. The penis and the scrotum of some are cut. A few others have all their limbs and joints cut. (37.)

---

Sanskrit
आभिद्यमानाः कुन्तैश्च भिद्यमानाश्च सायकैः ।
इतश्चेतश्च धावन्तः क्रन्दमाना निराश्रयाः ॥ ३८॥

English
Pushed, pierced and thrust by spears and arrows some run about helplessly here and there shrieking and squealing. (38.)

---

Sanskrit
मुद्गरैर्लोहदण्डैश्च हन्यमाना मुहुर्मुहुः ।
कण्टकैर्विविधैर्घोरैर्ज्वलनार्कसमप्रभैः ॥ ३९॥

भिन्दिपालैर्विभिद्यन्ते स्रवतः पूयशोणितम् ।
शकृता कृमिदिग्धाश्च नीयन्ते विवशा नराः ॥ ४०॥

English
Hit and thrashed by iron clubs and rods, bruised by terrible thorns of various sorts, luminous like fire and sun and pierced through by javelins, some men shed putrid blood or evacuate faeces infested with worms. They are taken ahead thus. (39-40.)

---

Sanskrit
याचमानाश्च सलिलमन्नं वापि बुभुक्षिताः ।
छायां प्रार्थयमानाश्च शीतार्ताश्चानलं पुनः ॥ ४१॥

दानहीनाः प्रयान्त्येवं प्रार्थयन्तः सुखं नराः ।
गृहीतदानपाथेयाः सुखं यान्ति यमालयम् ॥ ४२॥

English
Those who had not made any charitable gifts in the world feel thirsty along this path and vainly beg for water, feeling hungry they beg for food; oppressed by sunshine they beg for shade and distressed with chillness they request for fire. They vainly beg for happiness. But those who had made charitable gifts in the world have all the food and drink necessary for this journey and go ahead to Yama's abode happily. (41-42.)

---

Sanskrit
एवं न्यायेन कष्टेन प्राप्ताः प्रेतपुरं यदा ।
प्रज्ञापितास्ततो दूतैर्निवेश्यन्ते यमाग्रतः ॥ ४३॥

English
Having thus traversed the path they finally reach the city of the dead with great hardship. They are then ushered into the presence of Yama by the emissaries after due announcement. (43.)

---

Sanskrit
तत्र ये शुभकर्माणस्तांस्तु सम्मानयेद्यमः ।
स्वागतासनदानेन पाद्यार्घ्येण प्रियेण च ॥ ४४॥

English
Yama welcomes with pleasure and due honour, all those who had performed auspicious rites in this world. He offers them seat, padya and Arghya. (44.)

---

Sanskrit
धन्या यूयं महात्मानो निगमोदितकारिणः ।
यैश्च दिव्यसुखार्थाय भवद्भिः सुकृतं कृतम् ॥ ४५॥

English
Yama tells them—"You are noble souls duly blessed, since you have performed what is ordained in the Vedas. Good deeds that are conducive to divine happiness have been performed by you. (45.)

---

Sanskrit
दिव्यं विमानमारुह्य दिव्यस्त्रीभोगभूषितम् ।
स्वर्गं गच्छध्वममलं सर्वकामसमन्वितम् ॥ ४६॥

English
Ascend the celestial aerial chariot and go to heaven to enjoy the pleasures in the company of celestial damsels and fulfil your cherished desires. (46.)

---

Sanskrit
तत्र भुक्त्वा महाभोगानन्ते पुण्यस्य सङ्क्षयात् ।
यत्किञ्चिदल्पमशुभं पुनस्तदिह भोक्ष्यथ ॥ ४७॥

English
After enjoying pleasures there, in the end when the merit is exhausted return to this place for reaping the fruit of what little evil you may have committed." (47.)

---

Sanskrit
धर्मात्मानो नरा ये च मित्रभूता इवात्मनः ।
सौम्यं मुखं प्रपश्यन्ति धर्मराजनमेव च ॥ ४८॥

English
Men who have been virtuous are treated as friends by Yama. They see Yama with a gentle face. (48.)

---

Sanskrit
ये पुनः क्रूरकर्माणस्ते पश्यन्ति भयानकम् ।
दंष्ट्राकरालवदनं भृकुटीकुटिलेक्षणम् ॥ ४९॥

ऊर्ध्वकेशं महाश्मश्रुमूर्ध्वप्रस्फुरिताधरम् ।
अष्टादशभुजं क्रुद्धं नीलाञ्जनचयोपमम् ॥ ५०॥

सर्वायुधोद्धृतकरं सर्वदण्डेन तर्जयन् ।
महामहिषमारूढं दीप्ताग्निसमलोचनम् ॥ ५१॥

रक्तमाल्याम्बरधरं महामेरुमिवोच्छ्रितम् ।
प्रलयाम्बुदनिर्घोषं पिबन्निव महोदधिम् ॥ ५२॥

ग्रसन्तमिव शैलेन्द्रमुद्गिरन्तमिवानलम् ।
मृत्युश्चैव समीपस्थः कालानलसमप्रभः ॥ ५३॥

English
Men who have been guilty of cruelties see him in a terrible form. His face is terrible with curved fangs. His eyes are cruel with knit eyebrows. The hair on his head stand lifted up. He has a big moustache. His lips are pouted and they throb. He has eighteen hands. He is furious. He resembles black collyrium. His uplifted hands hold all weapons. He threatens with punishment. He is seated on a great buffalo. His eyes resemble blazing fire. He wears red garlands and garments. He is as tall as the mountain Mahā Meru. His voice resembles the rumbling sound of the clouds at the time of dissolution. He appears ready to drink up even the big ocean. (49-53.)

---

Sanskrit
ग्रसन्तमिव शैलेन्द्रमुद्गिरन्तमिवानलम् ।
मृत्युश्चैव समीपस्थः कालानलसमप्रभः ॥ ५३॥

English
He appears ready to swallow even great mountains. He appears to vomit fire. Very near him is Mrtyu, whose lustre is like that of black fire. (53.)

---

Sanskrit
कालश्चाञ्जनसङ्काशः कृतान्तश्च भयानकः ।
मारी चोग्रमहामारी कालरात्रिश्च दारुणा ॥ ५४॥

English
Black in colour resembling collyrium he is very terrifying. Mari, Ugramahamari and Kalaratri are terrible spirits attending on Yama. (54.)

---

Sanskrit
विविधा व्याधयः कुष्ठा नानारूपा भयावहाः ।
शक्तिशूलाङ्कुशधराः पाशचक्रासिपाणयः ॥ ५५॥

English
Various ailments, leproscis of various forms are terrible. They hold Saktis, tridents, goads, nooses, discuses and swords in their hands. (55.)

---

Sanskrit
वज्रतुण्डधरा रौद्राः क्षुरतूणधनुर्द्धराः ।
नानायुधधराः सर्वे महावीरा भयङ्कराः ॥ ५६॥

English
All of them are heroic and terrible. They have curved snouts. They bear shafts, quivers and bows and weapons of various sorts. (56.)

---

Sanskrit
असङ्ख्याता महावीराः कालाञ्जनसमप्रभाः ।
सर्वायुधोद्यतकरा यमदूता भयानकाः ॥ ५७॥

English
The attendants of Yama are innumerable. They are great heroes. They have complexions like the black collyrium. They appear terrifying with weapons lifted up. (57.)

---

Sanskrit
अनेन परिचारेण वृतं तं घोरदर्शनम् ।
यमं पश्यन्ति पापिष्ठाश्चित्रगुप्तं च भीषणम् ॥ ५८॥

English
The sinners see Yama terrific to behold, surrounded by his attendants and they see Citragupta too equally terrible. (58.)

---

Sanskrit
निर्भर्त्सयति चात्यन्तं यमस्तान्पापकर्मणः ।
चित्रगुप्तश्च भगवान् धर्मवाक्यैः प्रबोधयेत् ॥ ५९॥

English
Yama rebukes and reproaches the sinners. Lord Citragupta enlightens them with statements on virtue. (59.)

---

डाकिनीभिश्च रौद्राभिर्विकरालैश्च राक्षसैः ।
व्याधिभिश्च महाघोरैः पीड्यमाना व्रजन्ति हि ॥ १९॥
