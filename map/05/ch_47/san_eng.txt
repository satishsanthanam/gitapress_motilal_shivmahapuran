### **CHAPTER FORTYSEVEN - (Dhūmralocana, Caṇḍa, Muṇḍa and Raktabīja are slain)**

---

Sanskrit
ऋषिरुवाच ।
आसीच्छुम्भासुरो दैत्यो निशुम्भश्च प्रतापवान् ।
त्रैलोक्यमोजसाक्रान्तं भ्रातृभ्यां सचराचरम् ॥ १॥

English
The sage said :—  There was a Daitya Śumbha and another equally powerful Niśumbha. The three worlds including mobile and immobile beings were attacked by the two brothers. (1.)

---

Sanskrit
ताभ्यां प्रपीडिता देवा हिमवन्तं समाययुः ।
जननीं सर्वभूतानां कामदात्रीं ववन्दिरे ॥ २॥

English
The gods afflicted and harassed by them went to Himālaya and saluted the mother of all living beings, the bestower of all desires. (2.)

---

Sanskrit
देवा ऊचुः ।

त्रैलोक्यत्राणकारिण्यै शिवायै ते नमो नमः ॥ ३॥

English
The gods said :—  O goddess Durgā, Victory be to you. O, beloved of your kinsmen, be victorious. Obeisance to you, the cause of the protection of the three worlds. (3.)

---

Sanskrit

English
Obeisance to you, the bestower of salvation, obeisance to the great mother, the cause of the creation, sustenance and annihilation of the world. (4.)

---

Sanskrit

English
O goddess of the form of Kālikā, Tārā. Chinnamastā, Śrīvidyā, Obeisance to you. (5.)

---

Sanskrit

English
O goddess of the worlds, of the form of Bhairava, obeisance to you. Obeisance to Bagalāmukhī (stork-faced). Obeisance, to Dhūmāvatī. (6.)

---

Sanskrit

English
Obeisance to Tripurasundarī, to Mātaṅgī, to Ajitā (the unconquered) and to Vijayā. (7.)

---

Sanskrit

English
Obeisance to Jayā, Maṅgalā Vilāsinī, to you of the form of milking maid and of terrible form. (8.)

---

Sanskrit

English
Obeisance to you, O goddess, of the form of the unconquered, of permanent form, the protectress of those who seek refuge in you. (9.)

---

Sanskrit

English
Obeisance to the one knowable through Vedānta; obeisance to you the great soul, the heroine of infinite worlds of this universe. (10.)

---

Sanskrit

English
Eulogised thus by the gods the delighted goddess the bestower of boons, spoke, to the gods—"Who is being eulogised by you here ?" (11.)

---

Sanskrit

English
Then a virgin came out of the body of the goddess. Even as they were winking she spoke to the goddess with great reverence. (12.)

---

Sanskrit

English
"O mother, this eulogy is addressed to me by the heaven-dwellers harassed by the powerful Daityas Sumbha and Nisumbha." (13.)

---

Sanskrit

English
Since she emerged out of the inner vestures of her body she is called Kausiki. The destroyer of the Asura Sumbha is being sung in that name. (14.)

---

Sanskrit

English
She alone is said to be Ugratārikā and great Ugratārikā. She is called Mātaṅi after the source from which she manifested herself. (15.)

---

Sanskrit

English
She addressed the gods—"All of you stay here fearlessly. I shall carry out your task without any strain. I am independent". (16.)

---

Sanskrit

English
After saying this, the goddess vanished. Canda and Munda—the attendants of Sumbha and Nisumba saw the goddess. (17.)

---

Sanskrit

English
On seeing her beautiful form pleasing to the eyes they were fascinated. They lost their consciousness and fell on the ground. (18.)

---

Sanskrit

English
Returning to their king they narrated the details to him "O king, a certain beautiful lady hitherto unseen has been seen by us. (19.)

---

Sanskrit

English
Seated on a lion, on the beautiful peak of the Himavat she was served by the virgins of the gods with palms joined in reverence. (20.)

---

Sanskrit

English
They massaged her feet and hands, decorated her tresses, and applied collyrium to her eyes. (21.)

---

Sanskrit

English
A certain damsel held out the mirror to her face. Another handed over the betel-leaf along with cloves and cardamom. (22.)

---

Sanskrit

English

---

Sanskrit

English
Her calves and thighs are like the stem of the plantain tree. Her nose is like that of a parrot. Her arms are like serpents and creepers. She wears a beautiful girdle. The anklets round her feet are jingling. (24.)

---

Sanskrit

English
Pearl necklaces heave over her breasts rendered fragrant with the musk. Necklaces shine round her neck. She was sporting about bedecked in chaplets. (25.)

---

Sanskrit

English

Sanskrit

English

---

Sanskrit

English

---

Sanskrit

English

---

Sanskrit

English

English
He sent the Asuras-Caṇḍa, Munda and Raktabīja in order. The Daityas, thus ordered, went to the spot where Ambikā was stationed. (54.)

---

Sanskrit
सिंहारूढा भगवतीमणिमादिभिराश्रिताम् ।
भासयन्तीं दिशो भासा दृष्ट्वोचुर्दानवर्षभाः ॥ ५५॥

English
On seeing the goddess seated on a lion, possessed of Aṇimā⁸⁵ and other Siddhis and dazzling the quarters with her brilliance, the leading Dānavas said. (55.)

---

Sanskrit
हे देवि तरसा मूलं याहि शुम्भनिशुम्भयोः ।
अन्यथा घातयिष्यामः सगणां त्वां सवाहनाम् ॥ ५६॥

English
"O lady, hasten to approach Śumbha and Niśumbha. Otherwise we shall kill you along with your Gaṇas and your lion. (56.)

---

Sanskrit
वृणीष्व तं पतिं वामे लोकपालादिभिः स्तुतम् ।
प्रपत्स्यसे महानन्दं देवानामपि दुर्लभम् ॥ ५७॥

English
O lady, choose him as your husband. He is eulogised by the guardians of the quarters and others. You will attain a great bliss rare to attain by the gods." (57.)

---

Sanskrit
इत्युक्तमाकलय्याम्बा स्मयित्वा परमेश्वरी ।
उदाजहार सा देवी सूनृतं रसवद्वचः ॥ ५८॥

English
On hearing these words the goddess Ambā smiled and spoke interesting and truthful words. (58.)

---

Sanskrit
देव्युवाच ।
अद्वितीयो महेशानः परब्रह्म सदाशिवः ।
यत्तत्त्वं न विदुर्वेदा विष्ण्वादीनां च का कथा ॥ ५९॥

English
Lord Śiva, the great Brahman, the eternal god is without a second. Even the Vedas do not understand his reality. What then of Viṣṇu and others? (59.)

---

Sanskrit
तस्याहं प्रकृतिः सूक्ष्मा कथमन्यं पतिं वृणे ।
सिंही कामातुरा नैव जम्बुकं वृणुते क्वचित् ॥ ६०॥

English
I am His subtle Prakṛti. How can I choose another as my husband? Even if she is overwhelmed by love does a lioness choose a jackal as her mate? (60.)

---

Sanskrit
करेणुर्गर्दभं नैव द्वीपिनी शशकं न वा ।
मृषा वदत भो दैत्यो मृत्युव्यालनियन्त्रिताः ॥ ६१॥

English
A female elephant does not take an ass nor does a leopardess take a hare. O Daityas, overcome by the Serpent of Death you speak in vain. (61.)

---

Sanskrit
यूयं प्रयात पातालं युध्यध्वं शक्तिरस्ति चेत् ।
इति क्रोधकरं वाक्यं श्रुत्वोचुस्ते परस्परम् ॥ ६२॥

English
Descend to Pātāla⁸⁶ all of you. If you have the power, fight." On hearing her provocative words the Asuras said to one another. (62.)

---

Sanskrit
अबलां मनसि ज्ञात्वा न हन्मो भवतीं वयम् ।
अथो स्थिरैहि पञ्चास्ये युद्धेच्छा मानसेऽस्ति चेत् ॥ ६३॥

English
"We do not kill you as you are a woman. But if you desire for battle be ready with your lion." (63.)

---

Sanskrit
तेषामेवं विवदतां कलहः समवर्धत ।
ववृषुः समरे बाणा उभयोर्दलयोः शिताः ॥ ६४॥

English
While they spoke thus the fight ensued. The arrows rained in the battle, sharp arrows from either side. (64.)

---

Sanskrit
एवं तैः समरं कृत्वा लीलया परमेश्वरी ।
जघान चण्डमुण्डाभ्यां रक्तबीजं महासुरम् ॥ ६५॥

English
Thus fighting with them sportively the goddess slew the Asura along with Caṇḍa and Muṇḍa. (65.)

---

Sanskrit
द्वेषबुद्धिं विधायापि त्रिदशारातयोऽप्यमी ।
अन्ते प्रापन्परं लोकं यँल्लोकं यान्ति तज्जनाः ॥ ६६॥

English
Though they had an inimical feeling these enemies of the gods attained in the end that world which her followers go to. (66.)
