### **CHAPTER TWELVE - (The glory of the five-syllabled mantra of Śiva)**

---

Sanskrit
१२

श्रीकृष्ण उवाच ।
महर्षिवर सर्वज्ञ सर्वज्ञानमहोदधे ।
पञ्चाक्षरस्य माहात्म्यं श्रोतुमिच्छामि तत्त्वतः ॥ १॥

English
Lord Kṛṣṇa said:—  O omniscient excellent sage, ocean of all knowledge, I wish to hear precisely about the glory of the five-syllabled mantra. (1.)

---

Sanskrit
उपमन्युरुवाच ।
पञ्चाक्षरस्य माहात्म्यं वर्षकोटिशतैरपि ।
अशक्यं विस्तराद्वक्तुं तस्मात्सङ्क्षेपतः श‍ृणु ॥ २॥

English
Upamanyu said:—  It is impossible to explain in detail the glory of the five-syllabled mantra even in hundreds of crores of years. Hence hear it in brief. (2.)

---

Sanskrit
वेदे शिवागमे चायमुभयत्र षडक्षरः ।
सर्वेषां शिवभक्तानामशेषार्थप्रसाधकः ॥ ३॥

तदल्पाक्षरमर्थाढ्यं वेदसारं विमुक्तिदम् ।
आज्ञासिद्धमसन्दिग्धं वाक्यमेतत् शिवात्मकम् ॥ ४॥

नानासिद्धियुतं दिव्यं लोकचित्तानुरञ्जकम् ।
सुनिश्चितार्थं गम्भीरं वाक्यं तत्पारमेश्वरम् ॥ ५॥

English
The six-syllabled mantra is found in the Veda and Śivāgama. It facilitates the understanding of all topics by the devotees of Śiva. It consists of very few syllables but is pregnant with meaning. It is the essence of the Vedas. It is conducive to salvation. This expression of auspicious nature is devoid of doubts. It is achieved by the order of Śiva. It is attended by many Siddhis. It is divine and delightful to the mind. That expression 'lord Śiva' is majestic and decisive in meaning. (3-5.)

---

Sanskrit
मन्त्रं सुखमुखोच्चार्यमशेषार्थप्रसिद्धये ।
प्राहोन्नमः शिवायेति सर्वज्ञः सर्वदेहिनाम् ॥ ६॥

English
The omniscient lord Śiva mentioned the mantra, "Om Namah Śivāya" for the acquisition of all topics and meanings by the embodied beings since it can be easily uttered through the mouth. (6.)

---

Sanskrit
तद्बीजं सर्वविद्यानां मन्त्रमाद्यं षडक्षरम् ।
अतिसूक्ष्मं महार्थं च ज्ञेयं तद्वटबीजवत् ॥ ७॥

English
The first mantra consisting of six syllables is the seed of all lores. It is very subtle but serves a great purpose. It shall be known like the seed of the banyan tree. (7.)

---

Sanskrit
देवो गुणत्रयातीतः सर्वज्ञः सर्वकृत्प्रभुः ।
ओमित्येकाक्षरे मन्त्रे स्थितः सर्वगतः शिवः ॥ ८॥

English
The omniscient lord, the creator of everything, the all-pervasive Śiva who is beyond the three attributes, is stationed in the single-syllabled mantra Om. (8.)

---

Sanskrit
इशानाद्यानि सूक्ष्माणि ब्रह्माण्येकाक्षराणि तु ।
मन्त्रे नमः शिवायेति संस्थितानि यथाक्रमम् ।
मन्त्रे षडक्षरे सूक्ष्मे पञ्चब्रह्मतनुः शिवः ॥ ९॥

वाच्यवाचकभावेन स्थितः साक्षात्स्वभावतः ।
वाच्यः शिवोऽप्रमेयत्वात् मन्त्रस्तद्वाचकः स्मृतः ॥ १०॥

English
The five subtle Brahmans are stationed in the mantra "Namah Śivāya" occupying one syllable each. Thus in the six-syllabled subtle mantra, Śiva in the form of Pañca Brahmans²⁵⁹ is stationed in the way of Expressed and Expressive. Innately Śiva is 'Expressed' and the mantra is 'Expressive' due to its comprehensibility. (9-10.)

---

Sanskrit
वाच्यवाचकभावोऽयमनादिः संस्थितस्तयोः ।
यथानादिप्रवृत्तोऽयं घोरसंसारसागरः ॥ ११॥

English
This state of being the expressive and the expressed is beginningless inasmuch as this terrible ocean of worldly existence functions without a beginning. (11.)

---

Sanskrit
शिवोऽपि हि तथानादिः संसारान्मोचकः स्थितः ।
व्याधीनां भेषजं यद्वत्प्रतिपक्षः स्वभावतः ॥ १२॥

तद्वत्संसारदोषाणां प्रतिपक्षः शिवः स्मृतः ।
असत्यस्मिन् जगन्नाथे तमोभूतमिदं भवेत् ॥ १३॥

अचेतनत्वात्प्रकृतेरज्ञत्वात्पुरषस्य च ।
प्रधानपरमाण्वादि यावत्किञ्चिदचेतनम् ॥ १४॥

न तत्कर्तृ स्वयं दृष्टं बुद्धिमत्कारणं विना ।
धर्माधर्मोपदेशश्च बन्धमोक्षौ विचारणात् ॥ १५॥

न सर्वज्ञं विना पुंसामादिसर्गः प्रसिद्ध्यति ।
वैद्यं विना निरानन्दाः क्लिश्यन्ते रोगिणो यथा ॥ १६॥

English
Śiva too is beginningless and he is the releaser of persons from the worldly existence. Just as the medicine is naturally antagonistic to ailments so also Śiva is antagonistic to the ills of worldly existence. If the lord of the universe had not been in existence, the whole universe would have been gloomy, since the Prakṛti is insentient and the Puruṣa is ignorant. Pradhāna, the atom etc. are insentient. They never function as the makers themselves without an intelligent cause. The instruction in virtue and evil, the bondage and salvation, the activity of reflection—in view of all these things the first creation of men would not have been possible without the omniscient lord. Just as the patients will be devoid of joy and be distressed without the physician so also the people would be in distress without the lord. (12-16.)

---

Sanskrit
तस्मादनादिः सर्वज्ञः परिपूर्णः सदाशिवः ।
अस्ति नाथः परित्राता पुंसां संसारसागरात् ॥ १७॥

English
Hence, surely there is the lord, the primordial omniscient, perfect, Sadāśiva, the protector of persons from the ocean of worldly existence. (17.)

---

Sanskrit
आदिमध्यान्तनिर्मुक्तः स्वभावविमलः प्रभुः ।
सर्वज्ञः परिपूर्णश्च शिवो ज्ञेयः शिवागमे ॥ १८॥

English
Śiva is devoid of beginning, middle and end. He is the lord innately pure, omniscient and perfect as mentioned in Śaivite Āgamas. (18.)

---

Sanskrit
तस्याभिधानमन्त्रोऽयमभिधेयश्च स स्मृतः ।
अभिधानाभिधेयत्वात् मन्त्रः सिद्धः परः शिवः ॥ १९॥

English
This mantra expresses him. He is the person expressed by the great Mantra. (19.)

---

Sanskrit
एतावत्तु शिवज्ञानमेतावत्परमं पदम् ।
यदोन्नमः शिवायेति शिववाक्यं षडक्षरम् ॥ २०॥

English
The Śivajñāna, is as extensive as the expression of Śiva, the six-syllabled mantra, 'Om Namaḥ Śivāya'. (20.)

---

Sanskrit
विधिवाक्यमिदं शैवं नार्थवादं शिवात्मकम् ।
यः सर्वज्ञः सुसम्पूर्णः स्वभावविमलः शिवः ॥ २१॥

लोकानुग्रहकर्त्ता च स मृषार्थं कथं वदेत् ।
यद्यथावस्थितं वस्तु गुणदोषैः स्वभावतः ॥ २२॥

यावत्फलं च तत्पूर्णं सर्वज्ञस्तु यथा वदेत् ।
रागाज्ञानादिभिर्दोषैर्ग्रस्तत्वादनृतं वदेत् ॥ २३॥

English
This mantra is a positive statement and not a parable. How can Śiva, who is all-perfect, all-pervasive, innately pure and who blesses the worlds, mention a false theory? As things stand by nature along with their virtues and flaws, and the fruits they are capable of producing, how can the omniscient being mention untruth? Only one influenced by passion, ignorance and other flaws will speak untruth. (21-23.)

---

Sanskrit
ते चेश्वरे न विद्येते ब्रूयात्स कथमन्यथा ।
अज्ञाताशेषदोषेण सर्वज्ञेय शिवेन यत् ।
प्रणीतममलं वाक्यं तत्प्रमाणं न संशयः ॥ २४॥

English
Those two faults are not present in the lord. How can he then make a wrong statement? Surely therefore the expression that is uttered by Śiva, the omniscient without any flaw, is authoritative. (24.)

---

Sanskrit
तस्मादीश्वरवाक्यानि श्रद्धेयानि विपश्चिता ।
यथार्थपुण्यपापेषु तदश्रद्धो व्रजत्यधः ॥ २५॥

English
Hence, the statements of lord Śiva shall be faithfully considered by a learned man. In regard to merits and sins as they are, a person having no faith in him falls. (25.)

---

Sanskrit
स्वर्गापवर्गसिद्ध्यर्थं भाषितं यत्सुशोभनम् ।
वाक्यं मुनिवरैः शान्तैस्तद्विज्ञेयं सुभाषितम् ॥ २६॥

English
The good statements uttered by the sages quiet and calm for the achievement of heaven and salvation shall be considered 'sacred utterances.' (26.)

---

Sanskrit
रागद्वेषानृतक्रोधकामतृष्णानुसारि यत् ।
वाक्यं निरयहेतुत्वात्तद्दुर्भाषितमुच्यते ॥ २७॥

English
The utterances that are actuated by passion, hatred, falsehood, anger, lust and greediness are bad. They cause one's fall into hell. (27.)

---

Sanskrit
संस्कृतेनाऽपि किं तेन मृदुना ललितेन वा ।
अविद्यारागवाक्येन संसारक्लेशहेतुना ॥ २८॥

English
Of what avail is that statement of ignorance and lust which is the cause of worldly pain even if it be polished, soft and charming? (28.)

---

Sanskrit
यच्छ्रुत्वा जायते श्रेयो रागादीनां च सङ्क्षयः ।
विरूपमपि तद्वाक्यं विज्ञेयमिति शोभनम् ॥ २९॥

English
Even that ugly statement which on being heard brings in welfare and the destruction of lust etc., should be considered auspicious. (29.)

---

Sanskrit
बहुत्वेऽपि हि मन्त्राणां सर्वज्ञेन शिवेन यः ।
प्रणीतो विमलो मन्त्रो न तेन सदृशः क्वचित् ॥ ३०॥

English
Although there are many mantras, there is nothing like the holy mantra uttered by Śiva. (30.)

---

Sanskrit
साङ्गानि वेदशास्त्राणि संस्थितानि षडक्षरे ।
न तेन सदृशस्तस्मान्मन्त्रोऽप्यस्त्यपरः क्वचित् ॥ ३१॥

English
The Vedas and Śāstras along with their ancillaries are present in the six syllables. Hence there is no other mantra equal to this. (31.)

---

Sanskrit
सप्तकोटिमहामन्त्रैरुपमन्त्रैरनेकधा ।
मन्त्रः षडक्षरो भिन्नः सूत्रं वृत्त्यात्मना यथा ॥ ३२॥

English
Just as an aphorism is ramified and expanded by its gloss, the six-syllabled mantra is expanded by seven crores of great and subsidiary mantras. (32.)

---

Sanskrit
शिवज्ञानानि यावन्ति विद्यास्थानानि यानि च ।
षडक्षरस्य सूत्रस्य तानि भाष्यं समासतः ॥ ३३॥

English
Whatever texts there are, the texts expounding Śiva's knowledge, the repositories of lore, they are the commentaries of the succinct aphorism, the six-syllabled mantra. (33.)

---

Sanskrit
किं तस्य बहुभिर्मन्त्रैः शास्त्रैर्वा बहुविस्तरैः ।
यस्योन्नमः शिवायेति मन्त्रोऽयं हृदि संस्थितः ॥ ३४॥

English
Of what avail are many mantras and Śāstras full of details to one whose heart is firmly established in the mantra "Om Namah Śivāya?" (34.)

---

Sanskrit
तेनाधीतं श्रुतं तेन कृतं सर्वमनुष्ठितम् ।
येनोन्नमः शिवायेति मन्त्राभ्यासः स्थिरीकृतः ॥ ३५॥

English
If anyone has stabilised the Mantra "Om Namah Śivāya" by frequent practice, he has learnt all, heard all and performed all. (35.)

---

Sanskrit
नमस्कारादिसंयुक्तं शिवायेत्यक्षरत्रयम् ।
जिह्वाग्रे वर्तते यस्य सफलं तस्य जीवितम् ॥ ३६॥

English
Life is fruitful indeed, of the person, at the tip of whose tongue is present the set of three syllables 'Śivāya' prefixed with the word denoting obeisance. (36.)

---

Sanskrit
अन्त्यजो वाधमो वापि मूर्खो वा पण्डितोऽपि वा ।
पञ्चाक्षरजपे निष्ठो मुच्यते पापपञ्जरात् ॥ ३७॥

English
A person steady in the Japa of the five-syllabled mantra is released from the cage of sins whether he be a Śūdra, base-born, fool or a learned man. (37.)

---

Sanskrit
इत्युक्तं परमेशेन देव्या पृष्टेन शूलिना ।
हिताय सर्वमर्त्यानां द्विजानां तु विशेषतः ॥ ३८॥

English
This was mentioned by the lord when asked by the goddess, for the benefit of all men, particularly of brahmins. (38.)
